What Your Reranker Never Sees

Cross-encoder rerankers stop reading long documents part-way through. chunk_rescorer sends them the passage that matters instead.

A live demo on Elastic Cloud Serverless with the Elastic Inference Service and jina-reranker-v3.5

The problem

Rerankers read the first ~8,000 tokens and discard the rest

  • A cross-encoder reranker takes the query and the document text together and scores them jointly. That is why it beats vector similarity, and why it has a hard input limit.
  • jina-reranker-v3.5 on EIS reads about 8,192 tokens per document. Past that, the content is dropped, not sampled.
  • Measured live: the same filler document with the answer at five depths. Seen at 2,000, 6,000 and 7,500 tokens. Invisible at 9,000 and 12,000.

Why it matters

  • ~8,000 tokens is roughly 25 pages.
  • Manuals, contracts, policies and specs put the answer at the back.
  • The retriever can find the document. The reranker then judges it on the wrong pages.

The demo

A hardware retailer's support search, end to end on Serverless

  • Scenario. A shopper types a symptom. The right answer is the manual whose troubleshooting section lists that fault.
  • Corpus. Twelve synthetic manuals: three categories of three same-brand models, plus three short ones. Siblings share their first 8,000 tokens almost word for word.
  • The catch. Every long manual's troubleshooting section starts past the cap, at 11,700 to 26,000 tokens in.
  • Stack. Terraform provisions the project. One notebook indexes, queries and tears down. All calls go through the Python client; the reranker is the preconfigured EIS endpoint.

Two configurations, same query, same three manuals

Rerank sends whole manuals. Chunk Rerank sends the best chunk of each.

{"text_similarity_reranker": {
  "retriever": { standard: category filter + match on content },
  "field": "content",  "inference_id": ".jina-reranker-v3.5",
  "inference_text": query,  "rank_window_size": 3,
  "chunk_rescorer": { "size": 1,
    "chunking_settings": { "strategy": "sentence",
      "max_chunk_size": 300, "sentence_overlap": 1 } }
}}
  • One option on the retriever. No re-indexing, the index is untouched.
  • On the shard, each manual is split into 300-word chunks, every chunk is scored against the query lexically, and only the best one goes to the reranker.
  • Set chunking_settings explicitly. The service default is 7,000-word chunks, which are themselves over the cap.

Widen the window to the whole catalog

Whole documents cannot even be submitted at scale. Chunks can.

  • Drop the category filter, set the window to all twelve manuals, about 228,000 tokens.
  • Rerank is rejected before inference: "Request too large; estimated total tokens exceed the maximum limit of 200,000."
  • Chunk Rerank sends twelve chunks, about 5,000 tokens, and ranks the catalog correctly in 269 ms. The right manual is first by 0.69.

The takeaway

  • With whole documents, the rerank window is capped at a handful of long candidates by the request limit.
  • With chunks, the window can be as wide as the first stage returns.

The obvious objection

"Just use semantic_text." It finds the manual. The reranker still reads the whole thing.

  • Same manuals indexed as semantic_text, chunked and embedded at ingest. The semantic query scores each manual by its best chunk and finds the buried entry: 8 of 9.
  • Put the whole-document reranker on top and it falls to 4 of 9, with margins identical to the plain text field, query for query.
  • Why. Vectors chose the window. A cross-encoder reads text, not vectors, so the retriever fetches the whole original text and truncates it exactly as before.
  • Add chunk_rescorer and it is 9 of 9 again. Retrieval chunking and reranking chunking are separate decisions.

The one caveat

When the answer is already in view, whole documents can do better

  • Three spec queries answered near the front of the manual, inside the visible range. Rerank: 3 of 3. Chunk Rerank: 2 of 3.
  • The miss: "garage door opener with built-in camera and battery backup." Only one model has a camera; all three have battery backup.
  • Chunk selection is per manual. The siblings each contribute a dense battery-backup passage, and no single passage can say this manual never mentions a camera.
  • Chunking trades document-level context for one passage. That trade pays when the passage is out of view, and only then.

Two places to chunk

Query-time chunking keeps the document as the unit. Ingest-time chunking makes the chunk the record.

Elasticsearch chunk_rescorer, at query time

  • Pro. No re-indexing: one option on a text field you already have.
  • Pro. Results are still documents, with their metadata.
  • Pro. Retrieval chunking and reranking chunking are tuned independently.
  • Con. Chunk selection is lexical: the customer's words must appear in the passage.
  • Con. Chunking is redone per query for each document in the window.

Other platforms: chunk at ingest, rank chunks

  • Pro. The reranker limit is a non-issue by construction.
  • Pro. Chunking is done once and can follow document layout.
  • Con. The corpus must be re-indexed as chunks first.
  • Con. Results are chunks. Reassembling ranked, deduplicated documents is your code.
  • Con. Per-record limits on such rankers are far tighter than a document.

  • The cap is a property of what the reranker is sent, not of how the index was built. semantic_text does not exempt you.
  • Recommended configuration. Explicit sentence chunking at ~300 words with one sentence of overlap, one chunk per document, no top_n on the endpoint. Read ranks and gaps within a request, never scores across requests.
  • Skip it when your answers live in the visible head. Then whole documents are as good or better.
