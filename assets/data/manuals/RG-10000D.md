### Carbon Monoxide Hazards and the CO Sentinel Detection System
DANGER: Carbon monoxide is a highly toxic, invisible, odorless, and tasteless gas produced as a natural byproduct of the internal combustion process of the 420cc OHV engine. Breathing carbon monoxide gas can cause brain damage, permanent neurological impairment, or death within a matter of minutes.
DANGER: Never operate this portable generator inside any home, garage, basement, crawlspace, shed, camper, or other partially or fully enclosed structure. Even if you use open doors and windows or fans, carbon monoxide gas will rapidly build up to lethal levels and cannot be ventilated from these structures.
DANGER: The CO Sentinel carbon monoxide detection system is an active safety feature designed to monitor ambient carbon monoxide. If carbon monoxide levels around the generator become unsafe, the CO Sentinel system will immediately shut down the engine. A flashing red light on the control panel will signify that a carbon monoxide shutdown has occurred.
WARNING: A carbon monoxide shutdown indicates the generator was operated in an unsafe location with inadequate ventilation. If a shutdown occurs, immediately turn off all electrical loads, exit the area, and move to fresh air. Relocate the generator to a fully open, well-ventilated outdoor area before resuming operation.
WARNING: The CO Sentinel system is an auxiliary safety device and is not a substitute for outdoor operation. Never attempt to disable, bypass, or modify the CO Sentinel sensor module. Disabling this system will expose occupants to lethal gas and will void the manufacturer warranty.
To minimize the risk of carbon monoxide poisoning, always implement the following procedures:
1. Only operate the generator outdoors in an open area with continuous, unobstructed airflow on all sides.
2. Position the generator at least twenty feet away from any occupied building, window, door, or ventilation intake.
3. Point the exhaust muffler outlet away from structures, windows, or doors, keeping the exhaust path free of obstructions.
4. Install battery-powered carbon monoxide detectors inside your home and check their batteries regularly.
5. If anyone experiences headache, dizziness, nausea, or confusion, shut down the generator, move to fresh air immediately, and seek emergency medical attention.

### Fuel Handling, Refueling, and Fire Prevention
DANGER: Gasoline is highly flammable and explosive. Fuel vapors can travel along the ground to distant ignition sources, resulting in a flash fire or explosion that can cause severe burns or death.
WARNING: Observe the following fuel safety rules to prevent fire or explosion:
1. Always shut down the engine and allow it to cool for at least two full minutes before adding fuel to the 8-gallon fuel tank.
2. Slowly turn the fuel cap counterclockwise to release any accumulated pressure within the 8-gallon fuel tank. Wait for any hissing sound to stop before removing the cap entirely.
3. Refuel the generator outdoors in a well-ventilated, open area. Never refuel the generator inside a garage, shed, vehicle, or other enclosed space.
4. Do not overfill the fuel tank. Fill the tank to approximately one and one-half inches below the top of the neck to allow room for fuel expansion. Do not fill the tank to the very top of the neck.
5. If fuel spills during refueling, clean it up completely and wait for the remaining vapors to dissipate before starting the engine.
6. Never smoke or allow open flames, lighters, or sparks within fifty feet of the generator or fuel storage sites.
7. Store fuel in approved containers in a cool, well-ventilated location, far away from occupied buildings or ignition sources.
8. Regularly inspect the fuel line, fuel tank cap, and fuel tank for cracks, leaks, or deterioration. Replace any damaged components before operating the generator.

### Electrical Safety and High Voltage Electrocution Risks
DANGER: The generator produces high voltage that can cause severe electrical shock, electrocution, or death if not properly managed.
WARNING: To minimize the risk of electrocution, always adhere to the following safety precautions:
1. Do not operate the generator in wet conditions, including rain, snow, or high humidity. Do not touch the generator or electrical cords with wet hands or while standing in water.
2. Ensure that your hands are completely dry before touching the generator, the control panel, the power outlets, or any connected electrical cords.
3. The control panel is equipped with four 120V GFCI duplex outlets and one 120/240V twist-lock outlet. Test the ground fault circuit interrupters monthly. Press the test button to verify the circuit cuts power, then press the reset button to restore power.
4. Do not connect the generator directly to a building electrical system. Improper connection can cause electrical backfeeding into utility lines, which can electrocute utility workers and cause serious structure fires.
5. Connection of a generator to a building electrical system must be performed by a licensed electrician who must install a double-throw transfer switch.
6. Inspect all electrical cords, extension cords, and connected appliances for frayed insulation, exposed wires, or damaged plugs before each use.
7. Ensure that all extension cords are rated for outdoor use, have a ground wire, and are of the appropriate wire gauge to carry the electrical load.
8. Do not overload the generator. Ensure that the total electrical demand of all connected appliances does not exceed the continuous running wattage or starting wattage of the RG-10000D generator.

### Grounding the Portable Generator
WARNING: To ensure safe operation and prevent electrical shock from static electricity or insulation breakdown, the generator must be properly connected to an approved earth ground.
1. Locate the grounding terminal, which is clearly marked on the control panel of the generator and consists of a threaded brass stud and wing nut.
2. Strip approximately one inch of insulation from both ends of a length of heavy copper grounding wire. The wire must be a minimum of number eight gauge.
3. Wrap one end of the copper wire securely around the grounding terminal stud, and tighten the wing nut firmly to ensure a solid electrical connection.
4. Drive an approved copper-clad steel grounding rod at least eight feet in length deep into the earth, leaving only a few inches of the rod exposed above the soil surface.
5. Secure the opposite end of the copper wire to the grounding rod using an approved, heavy-duty grounding clamp. Tighten the clamp screw securely.
6. Check and comply with all local, state, and national electrical codes regarding grounding requirements for portable generators before operating the machine.

### Fire Hazards, Clearances, and Burn Prevention
WARNING: The 420cc OHV engine and its exhaust muffler become extremely hot during operation. Contact with hot engine components can cause severe second-degree and third-degree skin burns, and heat radiated from the engine can ignite nearby combustible materials.
1. Never touch any part of the engine block, exhaust muffler, heat shield, cylinder head, or spark plug during operation or within thirty minutes after the engine has been shut down.
2. Only handle the generator using the designated folding handle and the wheel kit when transporting or adjusting the position of the machine.
3. Maintain a minimum clearance of at least five feet of open space on all sides of the generator, including above the unit, during operation. Keep the generator away from wooden structures, dry brush, grass, weeds, leaves, overhanging tree branches, and any combustible building materials.
4. Ensure that the cooling air intake and discharge vents of the engine and alternator are kept clean and free of debris. Blocked cooling vents can cause the generator to overheat, resulting in severe damage to internal components and posing a significant fire hazard.
5. Allow the generator to cool completely for at least thirty minutes before placing a protective storage cover over the machine or storing it inside a garage, shed, or vehicle.

### Recoil Starter and Engine Operation Safety
WARNING: Operating the engine starter mechanisms requires proper physical control and awareness to avoid physical injury or strain.
1. The engine can be started using the electric start switch or the manual recoil backup starter. When using the manual recoil backup starter, always grasp the starter handle firmly with your hand. Pull the starter cord slowly until you feel resistance, then pull the cord rapidly in one swift, continuous motion.
2. Do not let the starter handle snap back violently against the engine shroud when you release it. Guide the rope back into the starter housing slowly and gently to prevent damage to the recoil starter assembly, the starter rope, or your hand.
3. Keep your hair, clothing, jewelry, fingers, feet, and any personal belongings far away from the recoil starter, the cooling fan, the alternator vents, and any other rotating parts of the engine.
4. Do not alter, adjust, or tamper with the engine governor linkage or the carburetor settings to increase the engine speed. Operating the engine at higher speeds than the factory specifications can damage connected electronic appliances, cause the generator to overheat, and increase the risk of catastrophic mechanical failure.
5. If the engine begins to vibrate excessively, make unusual clanking noises, or misfire, shut down the generator immediately. Do not attempt to run the generator under these conditions, as it could result in severe structural damage or operator injury.

### Battery Handling, Acid Precautions, and Electric Start Safety
WARNING: The electric start system on this generator is powered by a twelve-volt lead-acid battery. Lead-acid batteries contain sulfuric acid and can release highly explosive hydrogen gas, posing a serious risk of chemical burns, blinding, or explosions if handled improperly.
1. The battery electrolyte is an extremely corrosive sulfuric acid solution. Always wear heavy-duty rubber gloves, safety goggles, a face shield, and protective clothing when connecting, disconnecting, or performing maintenance on the battery.
2. If battery acid comes into contact with your skin or clothing, immediately flush the area with clean water for at least fifteen minutes and wash with soap. If battery acid enters your eyes, flush them immediately with clean water for fifteen minutes and seek emergency medical care without delay.
3. Batteries generate explosive hydrogen gas during normal operation and charging. Never allow any sparks, open flames, lit cigarettes, or other ignition sources near the battery. Charge the battery only in a well-ventilated, open outdoor area.
4. When connecting the battery cables to the battery terminals, always connect the positive red cable first, followed by the negative black cable. When disconnecting the battery, always disconnect the negative black cable first, followed by the positive red cable. This specific order prevents accidental electrical short circuits between the positive terminal and the metal frame of the generator.
5. Keep the battery terminals clean and free of corrosion. Inspect the terminal bolts regularly and ensure they are tightened securely. Store the battery in a cool, dry location when the generator is placed in long-term storage.

### Safe Maintenance Practices and Spark Plug Management
WARNING: Performing inspection, cleaning, or maintenance while the engine is running or hot can cause serious injury or death.
1. Before performing any maintenance, turn the engine switch to the off position, close the fuel valve, and disconnect the spark plug wire from the spark plug. Secure the spark plug wire away from the spark plug terminal to prevent accidental starting of the engine.
2. Never operate the generator if any protective guards, air filter covers, or safety shields have been removed. Ensure that all safety equipment is securely reinstalled and tightened after completing any maintenance or repair procedure.
3. Use only genuine Ridgeline Outdoor Power replacement parts, spark plugs, air filters, and accessories. Non-standard, generic, or unapproved parts can degrade the performance of the generator, increase emissions, void the manufacturer warranty, and compromise safe operation.
4. Keep the exterior of the generator clean and free of grease, oil, dirt, grass, leaves, or other combustible materials. Clean the control panel, outlets, and frame with a damp cloth, and use a soft-bristled brush to remove dust from the cooling vents.
5. Never spray water directly onto the generator or use a high-pressure washer to clean the machine. Liquid entering the electrical windings, control panel, or fuel system can cause catastrophic component failure and create a severe risk of electric shock or electrocution.

### Spark Arrestor and Environmental Emissions Standards
WARNING: The 420cc OHV engine of this generator is designed and certified to comply with federal Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and California Air Resources Board (CARB) emissions regulations. Operating this equipment in certain areas requires strict adherence to local environmental laws.
1. Under the California Public Resources Code, section 4442, it is a legal violation to operate an engine-driven generator on any forest-covered, brush-covered, or grass-covered land unless the exhaust system is equipped with a properly functioning spark arrestor screen. Other states or federal jurisdictions may have similar environmental laws.
2. The exhaust muffler of the RG-10000D generator is equipped with an integrated spark arrestor screen that captures hot carbon particles before they can exit the exhaust system and ignite wildland fires.
3. The spark arrestor screen must be inspected, cleaned, and serviced every fifty hours of engine operation, or more frequently if the engine is operated in dusty or harsh environments.
4. Never operate the generator if the spark arrestor screen is damaged, torn, heavily clogged, or missing from the exhaust muffler assembly. Refer to the maintenance section of this manual for detailed instructions on how to safely clean and replace the spark arrestor screen.

### Transporting and Storing the Generator Safely
WARNING: Improper transportation or storage of this generator can result in fuel leaks, engine damage, property damage, or severe personal injury.
1. Before transporting the generator, turn the engine switch to the off position, allow the engine to cool for at least thirty minutes, and turn the fuel valve to the closed off position to prevent fuel from flooding the carburetor or leaking during transit.
2. Ensure that the generator is kept in an upright, level position during transport. Tilting the generator can cause fuel to leak from the fuel cap or carburetor, or can cause engine oil to seep into the combustion chamber and air filter.
3. Secure the generator firmly using heavy-down straps or ropes when transporting it in a truck bed, trailer, or vehicle cargo area. Do not use the folding handle as a primary tie-down point.
4. When placing the generator in long-term storage exceeding thirty days, drain all fuel from the fuel tank and carburetor bowl into an approved container, or add a high-quality fuel stabilizer to the fuel tank and run the engine for several minutes to distribute the stabilizer through the fuel system.
5. Store the generator in a dry, well-ventilated location that is inaccessible to children, away from any appliances with pilot lights, water heaters, furnaces, or other sources of heat or open flames.

### Digital Hour Meter and Operational Monitoring Safety
CAUTION: The control panel features a digital hour meter that tracks the total accumulated running time of the generator. Operating the generator beyond the recommended maintenance intervals can lead to premature component wear, engine failure, and unsafe operating conditions.
1. Monitor the digital hour meter regularly to track operational hours and schedule routine maintenance, including oil changes, air filter cleaning, and spark plug inspections.
2. Do not allow the generator to operate continuously for extended periods without checking the engine oil level. The 420cc OHV engine is equipped with a low-oil shutdown system that automatically stops the engine if the oil level drops below a safe threshold, but this system is not a substitute for manual daily oil checks.
3. Pay close attention to any changes in the digital hour meter display or the behavior of the generator during operation. If the generator exhibits unstable voltage, abnormal engine speed fluctuation, or electrical output drop, shut down the engine immediately and perform a complete inspection.

### Power Management and Loading Calculations
Understanding performance specifications is essential to avoid overloading. Operating the generator above rated capacity will trip circuit breakers and can damage connected appliances.
Running wattage is the power required to keep appliances operating under steady-state conditions. Starting wattage is the momentary power surge required to start motor-driven appliances.
To calculate your electrical load, follow these steps:
1. Identify all electrical devices you intend to power simultaneously and locate their rated running wattages on the manufacturer specification labels.
2. Sum the running wattages of all devices. This total must not exceed the running wattage rating of 8000 watts on gasoline or 7200 watts on propane.
3. Identify the single appliance with the highest starting wattage requirement.
4. Add that highest starting wattage requirement to the sum of the running wattages of the other appliances. This total must not exceed the starting wattage rating of 10000 watts on gasoline or 9000 watts on propane.

## 3. Assembly and Setup

The RG-10000D portable generator requires minor assembly before its initial operation. This section provides detailed instructions for unpacking the unit, installing the wheel kit, mounting the folding handle, filling the engine crankcase with lubricating oil, connecting the electric start starter battery, and executing the mandatory electrical grounding procedure. Read and understand all instructions, safety standards, and operational guidelines before attempting to assemble or operate this outdoor power equipment.

### Unpacking the Generator and Inventorying Contents
The generator is shipped in a heavy-duty cardboard carton designed to protect the machine and its accessories during transit. Due to the heavy dry weight of the generator, which is specified as 228 pounds in Technical Specifications, do not attempt to lift or unpack the unit alone. Proper mechanical assistance or multiple capable individuals must be utilized to prevent physical strain, back injury, or property damage.
To unpack the generator, execute the following procedural steps:
1. Place the shipping carton on a flat, level, and solid surface outdoors or in a well-ventilated workshop space.
2. Using a utility knife, carefully cut the external packaging bands securing the outer carton, taking care not to puncture the cardboard deep enough to contact the generator frame, engine components, or control panel.
3. Open the top flaps of the carton and remove the upper cardboard protective inserts, user manual packet, and any loose accessory boxes containing assembly hardware and hand tools.
4. Carefully cut the four vertical corners of the cardboard carton from the top down to the base, allowing the cardboard sides to fold outward flat against the floor. This creates a flat workspace around the generator and eliminates the need to lift the heavy machine out of a deep box.
5. Slide the protective plastic wrap off the generator frame and visually inspect the entire machine for any signs of physical damage that may have occurred during shipping. If any damage is detected, do not attempt to assemble or operate the generator; contact Ridgeline Outdoor Power support immediately.
Verify that all standard accessories, loose hardware, and support components are present in the packaging. The standard inventory of loose parts includes the following items:
- One main generator assembly including the 459cc OHV engine, alternator, 8.5-gallon fuel tank, and steel tube roll cage
- Two solid polyurethane wheels, measuring 10.0 inches in diameter, mounted on plastic hubs
- Two steel axle pins with retaining cotter pins and flat washers
- Two support legs with integrated vibration-isolation rubber pads
- One folding handle assembly equipped with a rubberized grip and mounting brackets
- One propane regulator with excess-flow safety and flexible fuel hose
- One remote start key fob controller

### Installing the Wheel Kit
The wheel kit and folding handle are essential for ensuring the portability of the generator, allowing it to be positioned at the required safe operating clearance of 20 feet from occupied buildings as specified in Important Safety Instructions. Do not attempt to run the generator without the wheel kit and support legs installed, as the frame must be elevated to prevent moisture and vibration.
WARNING: The generator frame is heavy and can pinch, crush, or injure hands and feet if it tips over during installation. Ensure the generator is supported securely by wooden blocks.
To install the support legs and wheel kit, perform these steps:
1. Lift the front of the generator frame and slide a wooden block under the lower tubes to elevate the frame five inches.
2. Align the mounting holes in the top bracket of one support leg with the holes in the front cross-member.
3. Insert M8 flange bolts down through the frame tube and support leg bracket.
4. Thread M8 nylon lock nuts onto the bolts. Tighten with a 12 mm wrench and 13 mm socket to 15 foot-pounds.
5. Repeat on the opposite side to secure the second support leg.
6. Remove the wooden support block and lower the legs to the ground.
7. Lift the rear of the generator frame and place the support block under the lower tubes near the alternator.
8. Slide a flat washer onto the axle pin, followed by a 10.0-inch wheel, ensuring the wheel hub faces inward.
9. Slide the axle assembly into the rear frame bracket.
10. Insert a cotter pin through the axle pin hole and bend its legs outward to lock it.
11. Repeat on the opposite side.
12. Remove the block and verify the wheels rotate freely.

### Installing the Folding Handle Assembly
The folding handle assembly is mounted to the upper front frame cross-member, providing an ergonomic grip for maneuvering the generator over various terrains. The folding design allows the handle to lie flat against the frame when the generator is stationary, saving space.
CAUTION: Ensure the folding handle does not pinch wires or cables during installation. Keep fingers clear of the folding joints to prevent pinching as the handle is raised or lowered.
To install the folding handle assembly, proceed as follows:
1. Locate the folding handle, the rubberized grip, the handle brackets on the frame, and the hardware pack containing the pivot pins and cotter pins.
2. Align the pivot holes in the mounting ends of the handle with the holes in the frame brackets.
3. Push the handle tubes into the brackets, ensuring the curved side of the handle faces upward so it clears the frame when folded.
4. Slide a steel pivot pin through the bracket holes and the handle pivot holes.
5. Insert a cotter pin through the hole at the end of the pivot pin. Use pliers to bend the ends of the cotter pin outward.
6. Repeat this process for the opposite side of the folding handle to secure both pivot pins.
7. Lift the handle into the horizontal towing position until it locks, then fold it downward against the frame to verify smooth operation.

### Filling the Engine Crankcase with Lubricating Oil
The 459cc OHV engine is shipped from the factory without any oil in the crankcase. Operating the engine without the correct volume and viscosity of lubricating oil will cause immediate mechanical failure and void the manufacturer warranty.
WARNING: The low-oil shutdown system is not a substitute for manual oil level inspection. Always check the oil level before starting the engine, and never attempt to start the engine without filling the crankcase with the specified type and volume of lubricating oil.
NOTICE: Only use premium 4-stroke engine oil meeting API Service Category SJ or higher standards. Do not use non-detergent or 2-stroke engine oils.
To fill the engine crankcase with oil, follow these steps:
1. Ensure the generator is positioned on a flat, solid, and completely level surface to obtain accurate oil level readings.
2. Clean the area around the oil fill cap to prevent dirt or debris from entering the crankcase.
3. Rotate the oil fill cap counterclockwise to remove it from the crankcase. Wipe the integrated metal dipstick clean.
4. Insert the provided plastic funnel into the oil fill opening.
5. Slowly pour the provided 1.16-quart bottle of SAE 10W-30 engine oil into the crankcase.
6. Remove the funnel and insert the dipstick into the oil fill neck. Do not screw the dipstick into the threads; rest the cap flat against the opening.
7. Pull the dipstick straight out and observe the oil level on the hatched marking area. The oil level must be at the upper limit mark on the dipstick.
8. Reinsert the oil fill cap and screw it clockwise firmly by hand.
9. Wipe away any spilled oil from the engine block and frame to prevent slip hazards and reduce the risk of fire when hot.

### Filling the Fuel Tank
The generator is equipped with an 8.5-gallon fuel tank made of heavy-gauge steel, complete with an integrated mechanical fuel level gauge. This large fuel capacity provides extended operating runtimes but requires strict safety precautions when handling fuel to prevent fires, explosions, or environmental contamination.
DANGER: Gasoline is extremely volatile and highly flammable. Never fill the fuel tank while the engine is running or hot. Always allow the engine to cool for at least two full minutes before opening the fuel cap, and perform all refueling operations outdoors in well-ventilated spaces far away from ignition sources.
WARNING: Do not use stale, contaminated, or leaded gasoline. Avoid use of gasoline blends containing more than 10 percent ethanol by volume, as ethanol attracts moisture, clogs delicate carburetor pathways, and deforms internal rubber seals.
To fill the 8.5-gallon fuel tank with unleaded gasoline, execute the following procedure:
1. Position the generator on a flat, level surface outdoors in an open area with continuous airflow.
2. Shut down the engine if it is running, and allow the machine to cool for a minimum of two minutes.
3. Clean the area around the fuel tank cap to prevent dirt or moisture from falling into the fuel tank when the cap is removed.
4. Slowly turn the fuel tank cap counterclockwise to release any built-up vapor pressure. Wait for any hissing sound to stop before removing the cap entirely.
5. Inspect the fuel tank filter screen located inside the filler neck. Ensure the screen is clean, intact, and correctly seated. Do not remove this filter screen while adding fuel.
6. Using an approved container, slowly pour fresh, unleaded gasoline with a minimum octane rating of 87 into the fuel tank. Monitor the top-mounted mechanical fuel level gauge to track the fuel volume.
7. Do not fill the fuel tank to the top of the filler neck. Stop refueling when the fuel level reaches approximately one and one-half inches below the top of the neck, or near the lower edge of the fuel filter screen basket.
8. Install the fuel tank cap and rotate it clockwise until it clicks or locks firmly in place, sealing the tank.
9. Carefully wipe away any spilled fuel from the fuel tank, frame, and engine shrouds using a dry rag.
WARNING: Propane is highly flammable and under pressure. When operating on propane, ensure the LPG cylinder is placed upright on a flat surface at least ten feet from the generator.
To connect the propane cylinder, execute the following procedure:
1. Turn the fuel selector valve on the control panel to the propane position.
2. Inspect the propane regulator with excess-flow safety and flexible hose for any wear or damage.
3. Thread the regulator coupler onto the cylinder valve. Rotate the coupler knob clockwise by hand until fully hand-tight. Do not use tools.
4. Thread the opposite hose end onto the panel propane inlet and tighten securely with a nineteen millimeter wrench.
5. Open the cylinder valve slowly to prime the system.
6. Apply soapy water to all joints to check for leaks. If bubbles form, close the valve and tighten the connection.

### Connecting the Starter Battery
The electric starting system is powered by a pre-installed twelve-volt lead-acid battery. For safety during shipment, the negative black battery cable is disconnected and must be manually connected before first use.
WARNING: Lead-acid batteries produce highly explosive hydrogen gas and contain corrosive sulfuric acid. Keep all sparks, open flames, and cigarettes far away from the battery compartment, and always wear chemical-resistant safety goggles and heavy rubber gloves when completing this installation.
To connect the starter battery, perform the following specific electrical procedures:
1. Locate the battery positioned securely on the lower frame plate directly beneath the engine cylinder.
2. Slide back the black rubber terminal boot from the loose negative battery cable connector.
3. Align the negative cable ring terminal with the negative terminal slot on the battery post.
4. Insert the M6 connection bolt through the ring terminal and the negative battery terminal slot.
5. Thread the hex nut onto the bolt and tighten securely to forty inch-pounds using a ten millimeter wrench.
6. Slide the black rubber terminal boot completely over the negative terminal to prevent accidental short circuits.
7. Verify both positive red and negative black battery cables are routed completely away from hot engine block components.
8. Confirm the positive red cable connection is also tight.

### Grounding the Generator
Operating the generator without a proper earth ground can expose operators to serious high-voltage electrocution risks under various conditions. Under local and national electrical codes, the generator frame must be connected to an approved earth ground to provide a safe path for static charge. Refer to Grounding the Portable Generator in Section 1.
WARNING: Failure to properly ground the generator can result in severe electrical shock, permanent tissue damage, or electrocution. Always use an eight-gauge copper wire and a steel grounding rod.
To ground the generator, perform the following procedure:
1. Locate the grounding terminal stud, positioned on the lower right side of the control panel, designated by a brass stud, washer, and wing nut.
2. Strip exactly one inch of insulation from both ends of an eight-gauge copper grounding wire.
3. Wrap one end of the copper wire around the grounding terminal stud, between the flat washer and panel surface.
4. Thread the wing nut onto the stud and tighten it firmly by hand to secure the connection.
5. Drive an approved copper-clad steel grounding rod, measuring eight feet in length, deep into the soil near the generator.
6. Connect the opposite end of the wire to the rod using an approved grounding clamp and tighten securely with a socket wrench to establish a solid ground bond.

## 4. Operation

Operating a high-power outdoor machine like the Ridgeline Outdoor Power RG-10000D dual-fuel portable generator requires compliance with safe operating standards, mechanical steps, and electrical principles. The 459cc OHV engine consumes gasoline or propane to produce rotating kinetic energy, which the alternator converts into alternating current. Improper use can lead to serious risks such as lethal gas poisoning, high-voltage electrocution, flash fires, or catastrophic engine failure. This section outlines the essential procedures for operating the RG-10000D generator safely and effectively on both fuel sources.

The dual-fuel system allows operation on gasoline or liquid propane gas. On gasoline, the generator provides 10000 starting watts and 8000 running watts, drawing from the 8.5-gallon fuel tank. On propane, it provides 9000 starting watts and 7200 running watts via a specialized regulator connected to an LPG cylinder. The operator must select the correct fuel source and configure the valves before starting.

### Safety Precautions for Daily Operation
The most critical factor in the safe operation of this portable generator is the surrounding environment. The 459cc OHV engine releases exhaust fumes containing toxic carbon monoxide gas as a byproduct of combustion.

DANGER: Carbon monoxide is a colorless, odorless, tasteless, and highly toxic gas. Inhaling carbon monoxide can cause rapid brain damage, loss of consciousness, and death within minutes. Never operate this generator inside any residential home, garage, basement, crawlspace, shed, cabin, camper, or semi-enclosed structure, even if doors and windows are open.

WARNING: Always operate the generator outdoors on a flat, solid, and level surface with a minimum clearance of twenty feet from any occupied building, including windows, doors, and ventilation intakes. Maintain a minimum clearance of five feet on all sides of the generator, including overhead, from any combustible materials, structures, or dry vegetation.

The operator must utilize the folding handle and wheel kit to position the generator in an appropriate outdoor environment before starting. Never position the generator under low overhanging tree branches, on wooden decks, or on uneven terrain. Always point the exhaust muffler away from structures to allow the wind to carry the exhaust gas away.

Operating the generator in wet conditions creates an immediate risk of high-voltage electrocution. In wet weather, the generator must be placed under an approved, well-ventilated, non-combustible temporary shelter that keeps the control panel, outlets, and alternator vents dry while maintaining the required clearances.

Fuel handling requires extreme caution. Gasoline is highly volatile and flammable. Liquid propane is stored under pressure and can cause freezing burns. Propane leaks pose an explosion hazard. Inspect the propane hose and regulator for damage and perform a soap-and-water leak test on all fittings before operation. Never store fuel containers near the operating generator.

### Control Panel Interfaces and Electrical Outlets
The control panel manages the electrical output of the RG-10000D generator. Understanding each component is necessary to prevent errors and distribute electrical power safely.

1. Electric Start Switch: This three-position rocker switch controls engine ignition. Off disables the ignition. Run enables the starting circuit and remote receiver. Start engages the starter motor to spin the engine.

2. Remote Start button and indicator: This button pairs the remote key fob with the internal receiver, while the LED displays pairing status and system readiness.

3. Digital Multimeter Display: This screen provides diagnostic telemetry of the alternator's output, cycling through voltage, frequency, session run time, and total lifetime running hours.

4. CO Sentinel System Indicator Light: When the carbon monoxide sensor detects dangerous levels of gas, it triggers an automatic shutdown, stopping the engine and flashing a red light.

5. Fuel Selector Valve: This rotary valve switches between fuel sources. The horizontal position opens the carburetor gravity line, and the vertical position opens the pressurized propane inlet.

6. Four 120V GFCI Duplex Outlets: These NEMA 5-20R receptacles provide 120-volt alternating current up to twenty amperes, featuring ground fault circuit interrupter protection to safeguard users.

7. One 120/240V Twist-Lock Outlet: This NEMA L14-30R receptacle provides 120-volt or 240-volt alternating current up to thirty amperes to power large appliances or transfer switches.

8. One 120/240V 50A Outlet: This NEMA 14-50R outlet provides high-capacity electrical output up to fifty amperes at 120/240 volts for recreational vehicles or heavy machinery.

9. Four 20A Push-Button Thermal Protectors: These circuit breakers protect the NEMA 5-20R outlets, popping out to cut power if an outlet exceeds twenty amperes.

10. One 30A Double-Pole Main Circuit Breaker: This toggle breaker protects the NEMA L14-30R locking outlet, tripping to cut power if the load exceeds thirty amperes.

11. One 50A Double-Pole Main Circuit Breaker: This toggle breaker protects the high-capacity NEMA 14-50R outlet, tripping to cut power if the load exceeds fifty amperes.

12. Grounding Terminal: This threaded brass stud and wing nut connects the generator frame to an approved earth grounding rod.

### Engine Mechanics and Control Components
To operate the generator, the operator must be familiar with the mechanical controls located on the engine block and frame.

1. Propane Inlet Fitting: This brass connection on the control panel receives the flexible fuel line from the propane regulator.

2. Gasoline Fuel Valve: Located beneath the 8.5-gallon fuel tank, this rotary valve controls gasoline flow. Set vertically to open for gasoline, and horizontally to close for propane, transport, or storage.

3. Choke Lever: Located above the air filter housing, this sliding lever regulates air intake. Slide left to close the choke for cold starting, and right to open the choke once running.

7. Remote Start Key Fob: This handheld wireless transmitter allows starting and stopping the generator from up to seventy-five feet away under clear line-of-sight conditions.

### Pre-Operation Inspection Checklist
Executing a thorough pre-operation inspection before starting the engine is critical to ensure safe, reliable, and uninterrupted operation on either gasoline or propane.

To perform the inspection, execute the following steps:

1. Place the generator on a flat, solid, level outdoor surface. Operating on an incline can prevent proper engine lubrication, triggering low-oil shutdown.

2. Verify at least twenty feet of clearance from building openings, windows, doors, or vents. Ensure no combustible materials are within five feet of the generator.

5. If operating on propane, check the LPG cylinder fuel level. Inspect the propane regulator and the flexible hose for cracks, dry rot, cuts, or leaks. Connect the regulator to the cylinder and the hose to the panel inlet. Open the cylinder valve slowly and check for leaks with soapy water.

6. Inspect the battery terminals. Verify the positive red cable is secured to the positive battery post and the negative black cable is secured to the negative post, with both protective boots covering the terminals.

7. Confirm the spark plug wire is pushed firmly onto the spark plug.

8. Inspect the generator for fuel or oil leaks, loose hardware, or damaged components. Do not operate if leaks or damages are found.

9. Ensure all electrical loads are disconnected. Do not start with appliances plugged in or with the main circuit breakers in the on position.

### Fuel Source Selection and System Preparation
The dual-fuel capability allows the operator to select between unleaded gasoline and liquid propane gas depending on fuel availability and operating conditions. Proper preparation of the fuel system is required to prevent starting issues.

To configure the generator for gasoline operation, perform these steps:

1. Ensure the propane cylinder valve is closed and the flexible propane hose is disconnected from the panel inlet.
2. Turn the fuel selector valve on the control panel to the horizontal gasoline position.
3. Turn the manual gasoline fuel valve beneath the 8.5-gallon fuel tank to the vertical open position.
4. Verify that the carburetor drain screw is tightened securely.

To configure the generator for propane operation, perform these steps:

3. Turn the fuel selector valve on the control panel to the vertical propane position.
4. Connect the propane regulator to the LPG cylinder and the control panel propane inlet.
5. Open the propane cylinder valve slowly. If opened too quickly, the regulator excess-flow valve may trip, blocking fuel flow. If this occurs, close the valve, wait ten seconds, and open again slowly.

### Starting the Engine Using the Electric Start Switch
The electric start system utilizes the twelve-volt battery to spin the starter motor, providing a convenient method for starting the 459cc OHV engine.

WARNING: Before starting the generator, verify that no electrical cords are connected to any of the control panel outlets. Starting the generator under load can cause severe damage to connected appliances, the alternator, or the engine governor.

To start the generator using the electric start switch on gasoline, perform these steps:

1. Position the generator on a flat, level surface outdoors at least twenty feet from buildings.
2. Turn the fuel selector valve on the control panel to the gasoline position.
3. Turn the manual gasoline fuel valve beneath the 8.5-gallon fuel tank to the vertical open position.
4. Move the choke lever to the left to the choke closed position. If the engine is warm, leave the lever in the rightward choke open position.
5. Verify that the main circuit breakers are in the off position.
6. Stand to the side of the generator, keeping clear of the exhaust area.
7. Press and hold the electric start switch in the start position for no more than five seconds.
8. As soon as the engine starts, release the switch immediately.
9. As the engine warms up, gradually slide the choke lever to the right toward the choke open position.
10. Allow the engine to run under no load for two minutes to stabilize speed and voltage before connecting devices.

To start the generator using the electric start switch on propane, perform these steps:

1. Position the generator on a flat, level surface outdoors at least twenty feet from buildings.
2. Turn the manual gasoline fuel valve beneath the 8.5-gallon fuel tank to the horizontal closed position.
3. Turn the fuel selector valve on the control panel to the propane position.
4. Connect the propane regulator to the cylinder and the panel propane inlet.
5. Open the propane cylinder valve slowly and completely.
6. Slide the choke lever to the left to the choke closed position. If the engine is warm, leave the lever in the rightward choke open position.
7. Verify that the main circuit breakers are in the off position.
8. Press and hold the electric start switch in the start position for no more than five seconds.
9. As soon as the engine starts, release the switch immediately.
10. As the engine warms up, gradually slide the choke lever to the right toward the choke open position.
11. Allow the engine to run under no load for two minutes before connecting devices.

CAUTION: To prevent overheating of the starter motor, do not crank the engine for more than five seconds at a time. If the engine fails to start, wait at least ten seconds before attempting to crank the engine again.

### Starting the Engine Using the Remote Start Fob
The remote start system allows starting and stopping the generator from up to seventy-five feet away. This feature utilizes the pre-installed twelve-volt battery and a wireless key fob.

WARNING: Never attempt to start the generator remotely unless you have a clear line of sight to the unit and can verify that the surrounding area is clear of people, pets, and combustible materials. Ensure the generator is positioned outdoors with a minimum clearance of twenty feet from occupied buildings.

To pair the remote start fob, perform these steps:

1. Turn the electric start switch on the control panel to the run position.
2. Press and hold the pairing button on the control panel until the indicator light flashes.
3. Press any button on the remote start fob. The indicator light will illuminate solid to confirm successful pairing.

To start the generator using the remote start fob, perform these steps:

1. Complete all steps in the Pre-Operation Inspection Checklist.
2. Open the appropriate fuel valve and select the fuel source using the fuel selector valve.
3. Slide the choke lever left to the choke closed position.
4. Verify all circuit breakers are off and no cords are plugged in.
5. Turn the electric start switch to run.
6. From up to seventy-five feet away, press and hold the start button on the remote key fob for two seconds.
7. The receiver will engage the starter motor. Once started, the starter will disengage.
8. If starting fails, the receiver will pause ten seconds and make up to three attempts before shutting down the remote start system to protect the battery.
9. Gradually move the choke lever to the open position.
10. Allow the engine to run under no load for two minutes to stabilize before applying electrical loads.

To stop the generator using the remote start fob, press and hold the stop button on the fob for two seconds. Turn the electric start switch to off to prevent battery drain.

### Starting the Engine Using the Manual Recoil Backup
If the twelve-volt battery is discharged or disconnected, the manual recoil backup starter can be used. This mechanical starter requires proper physical technique to prevent personal injury.

WARNING: Grasp the manual recoil starter handle firmly before pulling. High engine compression can cause rapid rope retraction, pulling your arm violently toward the engine, which can cause severe muscle strains, joint dislocations, or fractures.

To start the generator using the manual recoil backup starter, perform these steps:

1. Position the generator on a flat, solid, level outdoor surface at least twenty feet away from buildings.
2. Select the fuel source and open the corresponding fuel valve. Set the fuel selector valve to match.
3. Slide the choke lever left to the choke closed position. If warm, leave in the choke open position.
4. Verify all circuit breakers are off and no cords are plugged in.
5. Press the electric start switch to run to enable the ignition.
6. Stand on the side of the generator, place one hand on the frame tube to stabilize the machine, and grasp the recoil handle.
7. Pull the rope slowly until physical resistance is felt, indicating the compression stroke.
8. Pull the handle backward in one swift, continuous, and powerful motion straight outward.
9. Guide the starter rope back slowly. Do not let go and allow it to snap back.

CAUTION: Letting the starter rope snap back uncontrolled can damage the recoil starter housing or internal components. Maintain your grip and guide the cord back manually.

10. If the engine fails to start, repeat steps 6 through 9.
11. Gradually slide the choke lever right to the choke open position. Allow the engine to idle under no load for two minutes before turning on the circuit breakers.

### CO Sentinel Carbon Monoxide Shutdown Response
The CO Sentinel system is an advanced safety feature designed to monitor the atmosphere around the generator for elevated levels of carbon monoxide to protect occupants from toxic gas poisoning.

During normal operation, the system continuously samples the ambient air. If the sensor detects a carbon monoxide concentration that exceeds safety thresholds, the system will ground the engine ignition circuit, causing the 459cc OHV engine to shut down immediately. A flashing red light on the control panel will illuminate to signify that a carbon monoxide shutdown occurred.

DANGER: A carbon monoxide shutdown indicates that the generator was operated in an unsafe location with inadequate ventilation. Carbon monoxide is a lethal poison. If the flashing red light on the control panel is active, do not approach the generator immediately.

If a carbon monoxide shutdown occurs, execute the following safety protocol:

1. Immediately evacuate the area and direct all personnel to move to fresh air. If anyone exhibits symptoms of carbon monoxide exposure, seek emergency medical attention.
2. Wait five to ten minutes for the accumulated carbon monoxide gas to dissipate before approaching the generator.
3. Turn the electric start switch on the control panel to the off position.
4. Relocate the generator to a fully open, flat, and level outdoor area at least twenty feet away from building openings, windows, doors, or vents with unobstructed airflow.
5. Turn the fuel valve to open, set the choke lever to choke closed, and press the electric start switch to restart the engine. The flashing red light will automatically turn off once restarted in a safe location.

WARNING: Never attempt to bypass, disable, or modify the CO Sentinel carbon monoxide sensor module. Doing so will expose occupants to lethal carbon monoxide gas, void the manufacturer warranty, and violate federal safety regulations.

### Low-Oil Shutdown Field Verification
The 459cc OHV engine features an active low-oil shutdown system that protects internal components from damage due to inadequate lubrication. This system utilizes a float-switch sensor mounted inside the crankcase.

If the engine oil level drops below a safe threshold during operation, the sensor grounds the ignition system, instantly stopping the engine. When a low-oil shutdown occurs, the engine stops running, but the electric start switch remains in the run position.

To verify if an unexpected engine shutdown was caused by the low-oil shutdown system, perform these steps:

1. Ensure the generator is positioned on a flat, solid, level surface to prevent incorrect float sensor readings.
2. Turn the electric start switch to the off position.
3. Disconnect all electrical loads and verify circuit breakers are in the off position.
4. Remove the oil fill cap dipstick and wipe it clean.
5. Insert the dipstick back into the fill neck without threading it. Pull it out and inspect the hatched marking area.
6. If the oil level is below the lower limit or near the bottom of the hatched area, a low-oil shutdown occurred.
7. Add SAE 10W-30 engine oil until the level reaches the upper limit mark on the dipstick. Do not overfill the crankcase.
8. Reinstall the oil fill cap securely.
9. Restart the engine and verify it runs smoothly and does not shut down.

### Connecting Electrical Loads and Power Management
Properly managing the electrical loads connected to the generator is essential to prevent overloading, protect connected appliances, and ensure the longevity of the alternator.

The RG-10000D generator has two ratings: starting wattage and running wattage. Starting wattage is the maximum surge power the alternator can produce for a short duration to start electric motors. Running wattage is the continuous power the generator can supply to keep appliances operating. On gasoline, the generator provides 10000 starting watts and 8000 running watts. On propane, it provides 9000 starting watts and 7200 running watts.

WARNING: Never connect electrical loads to the generator before starting the engine or before the engine has warmed up and stabilized. Always start the engine and let it run for two minutes under no load before plugging in or turning on any appliances.

To connect electrical loads and manage power distribution, perform these steps:

1. Ensure the engine has been running smoothly for at least two minutes and the digital multimeter displays a stable voltage of approximately 120V or 240V and a frequency of 60Hz.
2. Turn on the main circuit breakers by toggling them to the on position.
3. Inspect all extension cords and power cables. Ensure they are grounded, rated for outdoor use, and of the correct wire gauge for the current they will carry.
4. Plug in and turn on the appliance with the highest starting wattage requirement first. This is typically a motor-driven device such as a refrigerator, air conditioner, or well pump.
5. Allow the engine speed to stabilize. The automatic voltage regulator will adjust to the load, and the engine governor will increase throttle to maintain 3600 RPM.
6. Plug in and turn on the next highest load appliance.
7. Continue adding appliances one at a time, allowing the engine to stabilize after each addition.
8. Monitor the total wattage being drawn. The sum of the running wattages of all connected appliances must not exceed 8000 watts when running on gasoline, or 7200 watts when running on propane.

If the generator is overloaded, the circuit breakers will trip to protect the alternator. If a circuit breaker trips, perform these steps:

1. Disconnect all electrical loads from the control panel.
2. Turn the tripped circuit breaker to the off position, then back to the on position to reset it.
3. Reduce the electrical load by disconnecting one or more appliances.
4. Reconnect the appliances one at a time, starting with the largest load, ensuring the total load remains within the generator's rated capacity.

### Shutting Down the Generator
Proper shutdown of the generator is necessary to prevent electrical surges from damaging connected appliances and to prepare the fuel system for storage or transport.

To shut down the generator, perform these steps:

1. Turn off and unplug all electrical appliances and cords from the control panel. Never stop the engine with electrical loads connected.
2. Let the engine run under no load for approximately two minutes. This allows the internal temperatures of the 459cc OHV engine and the alternator windings to stabilize and cool down.
3. If operating on gasoline, perform these steps:
   a. Turn the manual gasoline fuel valve beneath the 8.5-gallon fuel tank to the horizontal closed position.
   b. Turn the electric start switch on the control panel to the off position. This cuts the ignition circuit and stops the engine.
4. If operating on propane, perform these steps:
   a. Close the valve on the propane cylinder completely. This allows the engine to consume the remaining gas in the flexible hose and regulator, purging the line.
   b. Once the engine stalls and stops running, turn the electric start switch on the control panel to the off position.
   c. Disconnect the flexible propane hose from the panel propane inlet and the regulator from the propane cylinder.
5. If the generator will not be used for an extended period, turn the electric start switch to the off position to prevent the twelve-volt battery from discharging through the wireless remote receiver.
6. Allow the generator to cool down for at least thirty minutes before transporting it or putting on a storage cover.

## 5. Maintenance and Storage

### Maintenance Safety Standards
Regular inspection, cleaning, and maintenance ensure the safe, reliable, and efficient operation of your Ridgeline Outdoor Power RG-10000D portable generator. Working on high-voltage and engine-driven equipment presents severe hazards if appropriate safety guidelines are neglected.
WARNING: Before executing any cleaning, adjustments, or mechanical repairs, turn the electric start switch to the off position, close the manual gasoline fuel valve, close the propane cylinder valve, and disconnect the spark plug wire. Secure the spark plug wire completely away from the spark plug terminal to prevent accidental or automatic starting of the 459cc OHV engine.
WARNING: The engine cylinder block, exhaust muffler, and heat shields become extremely hot during operation. Contact with hot surfaces causes severe skin burns. Allow the generator to cool for thirty minutes before performing maintenance.
WARNING: Unleaded gasoline is highly flammable and volatile. Liquid propane gas is highly flammable and stored under high pressure. Refuel and perform fuel system maintenance outdoors in well-ventilated areas away from sparks, open flames, and pilot lights. Clean up fuel spills immediately and perform leak checks on all pressurized gas lines.
WARNING: Battery electrolyte contains corrosive sulfuric acid and releases explosive hydrogen gas. Wear chemical-resistant rubber gloves, safety goggles, a face shield, and protective clothing when performing battery maintenance.
NOTICE: Under California Proposition 65, mechanical components, fuels, oils, and battery posts contain chemicals known to the State of California to cause cancer and reproductive harm. Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water after handling these parts.

### Periodic Maintenance Schedule
To preserve emissions compliance, performance, and warranty of the generator, adhere strictly to the maintenance intervals specified below. Perform maintenance more frequently when operating in extremely dusty, sandy, or high-temperature environments.

Daily or Before Each Use:
1. Check engine oil level and add SAE 10W-30 oil if low.
2. Inspect generator for gasoline, oil, or propane leaks.
3. Verify surrounding clearance is twenty feet from building openings and five feet from combustibles.
4. Inspect air filter foam element.
5. Test ground fault circuit interrupters on the four 120V GFCI duplex outlets.

First Month or First 20 Hours of Operation:
1. Change engine lubricating oil.

Every 3 Months or Every 50 Hours:

2. Inspect and clean stainless steel spark arrestor screen inside the exhaust muffler.

Every 6 Months or Every 100 Hours:

2. Inspect, clean, and gap spark plug.

4. Inspect spark plug wire boot.

Every Year or Every 300 Hours:

2. Replace air filter foam element.

5. Clean engine combustion chamber deposits.

### Engine Oil Service
The 459cc OHV engine utilizes splash lubrication requiring premium 4-stroke engine oil meeting API Service Category SJ or higher standards. The engine oil capacity is 1.16 quarts (1.1 liters). Change the oil after the first twenty break-in hours, and then every one hundred operating hours or six months.
To change the engine oil, execute these steps:
1. Place generator on a flat, solid, completely level surface.
2. Start engine and run for five minutes to warm the oil, then turn the electric start switch off and disconnect the spark plug wire boot.
3. Place an approved catch pan under base of engine block near oil drain plug.
4. Wipe oil fill cap and drain plug area with a clean rag.
5. Remove oil fill cap counterclockwise to vent the crankcase.
6. Using a fourteen millimeter wrench, remove oil drain plug counterclockwise and drain oil completely into catch pan.
7. Inspect drain plug sealing washer and replace if worn.
8. Reinstall oil drain plug and tighten clockwise to eighteen foot-pounds.
9. Insert funnel into oil fill neck and slowly pour 1.16 quarts of SAE 10W-30 engine oil.
10. Remove funnel, wipe dipstick clean, and insert into fill neck without threading.
11. Pull dipstick straight out to verify oil level aligns with upper limit marking.
12. Reinstall oil fill cap clockwise and hand-tighten.
13. Dispose of used oil at an authorized recycling facility.

### Air Filter Maintenance
A clogged air filter restricts air flowing to the carburetor, riching the air-fuel ratio, causing misfires, high emissions, and power loss. Clean air filter foam element every fifty operating hours, or more frequently in dusty environments.
To service the air filter foam element, execute these steps:
1. Turn off generator and disconnect spark plug wire.
2. Locate air filter housing above carburetor.
3. Release upper and lower metal spring clips securing air filter cover.
4. Remove cover and pull out foam filter element.
5. Inspect foam element and replace with a genuine Ridgeline replacement if torn or cracked.
6. Wash foam element in warm water and liquid dish soap, squeezing gently. Do not wring or twist.
7. Rinse foam element thoroughly in clean water and let it air-dry completely.
8. Pour small quantity of clean SAE 10W-30 engine oil onto dry foam element.
9. Squeeze foam repeatedly to distribute oil evenly. Foam must be damp, not dripping.
10. Wrap foam in paper towel and squeeze to remove excess oil.
11. Wipe dust from air filter housing and cover.
12. Seat foam element flat in housing, align cover, and secure spring clips.

### Spark Plug Service
The spark plug ignites the fuel-air mixture. For optimal efficiency, the spark plug must be clean, free of deposits, and gapped to specifications. Service spark plug every one hundred operating hours, and replace annually with a new Ridgeline F7TC or equivalent plug.
To perform spark plug maintenance, execute these steps:
1. Turn electric start switch off and allow engine to cool for thirty minutes, then disconnect spark plug wire boot.
2. Clean area around base of spark plug.
3. Using provided spark plug wrench, remove spark plug counterclockwise.
4. Inspect plug electrode and ceramic insulator. Replace immediately if ceramic is cracked or electrode is eroded.
5. Clean light carbon deposits from electrodes using a wire brush.
6. Measure gap between electrodes with feeler gauge. Gap must be 0.028 to 0.031 inches (0.7 to 0.8 mm).
7. Adjust gap by bending side electrode gently with a gapping tool.
8. Ensure crush washer is in place and undamaged.
9. Thread spark plug into cylinder head by hand clockwise.
10. Tighten plug using spark plug wrench. Tighten used plug one-eighth to one-quarter turn past finger-tight; tighten new plug one-half turn past finger-tight.
11. Alternatively, torque plug to eighteen to twenty-two foot-pounds (twenty-five to thirty Newton-meters).
12. Reconnect spark plug wire boot firmly.

### Spark Arrestor Cleaning
The exhaust muffler contains an integrated stainless steel spark arrestor screen preventing hot carbon particles from escaping and causing wildland fires. Clean spark arrestor screen every fifty operating hours to prevent exhaust restriction.
WARNING: Exhaust muffler and heat shield become extremely hot during operation. Contact causes severe skin burns. Let exhaust system cool for thirty minutes before servicing.
To service the spark arrestor screen, perform these steps:
1. Shut down engine and disconnect spark plug wire boot.
2. Locate spark arrestor retaining plate on outlet nozzle of exhaust muffler.
3. Using a screwdriver, remove retaining screws securing plate.
4. Slide stainless steel spark arrestor screen out of muffler outlet.
5. Inspect screen for holes or tears. Replace if damaged.
6. Clean screen thoroughly using a stiff wire brush to remove carbon deposits.
7. Reinsert clean screen, position retaining plate, and tighten screws.

### Fuel Sediment Cup Service
The gravity-feed manual gasoline fuel valve under the 8.5-gallon fuel tank contains an integrated sediment cup trapping water and heavy sediment before they enter the carburetor. Clean sediment cup every one hundred operating hours or six months.
WARNING: Gasoline is highly flammable and volatile. Work outdoors in a well-ventilated area with no open flames or sparks nearby.
To clean the fuel sediment cup, follow these steps:
1. Turn manual gasoline fuel valve handle horizontally to closed position.
2. Place small container under fuel valve to catch residual fuel.
3. Using a wrench, rotate metal sediment cup counterclockwise to remove it.
4. Remove rubber O-ring gasket and plastic fuel filter screen.
5. Clean sediment cup, filter screen, and gasket with fresh gasoline.
6. Inspect O-ring gasket and replace if damaged.
7. Reinstall plastic filter screen and O-ring gasket onto sediment cup.
8. Thread sediment cup clockwise onto fuel valve body and tighten firmly with a wrench.
9. Turn manual gasoline fuel valve to vertical open position and inspect for fuel leaks.

### Battery Care and Fuse Replacement
The twelve-volt lead-acid battery must be kept clean and securely tightened for reliable starting. The battery self-discharges when stored, requiring auxiliary charging.
WARNING: Battery electrolyte contains corrosive sulfuric acid and releases explosive hydrogen gas. Wear chemical-resistant gloves and safety goggles.
To service the battery, follow these procedures:
1. Disconnect negative black cable with a ten millimeter wrench. Always disconnect negative black cable first.
2. Slide back red boot and disconnect positive red cable.
3. Clean terminals and connectors using wire battery brush or baking soda and warm water.
4. Apply thin coating of dielectric grease to terminals to prevent corrosion.
5. Reconnect positive red cable first, followed by negative black cable, and tighten to forty inch-pounds.
If starter motor fails to spin, check the inline battery fuse:
1. Locate inline fuse holder, containing 15 Amp blade fuse along positive red battery cable.
2. Pull open rubber cap and replace fuse with new 15 Amp inline blade fuse if metal element is melted.
If battery voltage drops below twelve volts, charge it:
1. Connect positive red charger clamp to positive battery terminal and negative black clamp to negative terminal.
2. Plug charger into wall outlet. Charge at 1.2 to 1.5 Amps for six to eight hours. Do not use rapid chargers exceeding two Amps.
3. Unplug charger before disconnecting clamps.

### Carburetor and Fuel Tank Draining
Gasoline stored in fuel tank and carburetor degrades over time, forming gum and varnish clogging fuel passages. Draining fuel system is required before storing generator for more than thirty days.
WARNING: Gasoline is highly volatile and extremely flammable. Drain fuel system outdoors in well-ventilated open area far away from ignition sources.
To drain fuel tank and carburetor, perform these steps:
1. Allow engine to cool completely for thirty minutes.
2. Place approved gasoline container beneath fuel valve assembly.
3. Turn manual gasoline fuel valve to closed position.
4. Disconnect fuel line from carburetor inlet by sliding back hose clamp with pliers.
5. Place open end of fuel line into gasoline container.
6. Turn manual gasoline fuel valve to open position and drain 8.5-gallon fuel tank completely.
7. Turn manual gasoline fuel valve back to closed position and reconnect fuel line.
8. Locate carburetor drain bolt on lower side of float bowl. Do not remove center bolt.
9. Place small container beneath carburetor bowl.
10. Using a ten millimeter wrench, remove carburetor drain bolt counterclockwise.
11. Allow fuel remaining inside carburetor bowl to drain completely.
12. Reinstall carburetor drain bolt and tighten securely clockwise. Wipe up spilled fuel.

### Short-Term and Long-Term Storage Protocols
If generator is stored for extended periods, specific steps must be executed to protect the fuel system and engine internals.
Short-term storage (30 to 90 days):
1. Add fuel stabilizer to gasoline in 8.5-gallon fuel tank, following recommended mixing ratio.
2. Start engine and run generator for ten minutes to distribute stabilized fuel.
3. Turn electric start switch off and turn manual gasoline fuel valve to closed position.
4. Clean exterior of generator and verify all fasteners are tight.
5. Store generator in dry, well-ventilated location away from appliances with pilot lights.

Long-term storage (over 90 days):
1. Drain 8.5-gallon fuel tank and carburetor bowl completely as described in Carburetor and Fuel Tank Draining.
2. Change engine lubricating oil as described in Engine Oil Service.
3. Disconnect spark plug wire and remove spark plug.
4. Pour one tablespoon (fifteen milliliters) of clean SAE 10W-30 engine oil into spark plug hole.
5. Cover spark plug hole with clean rag.
6. Pull recoil starter handle slowly three to five times to distribute oil and coat cylinder walls, then reinstall spark plug and torque to eighteen to twenty-two foot-pounds.
7. Pull recoil starter slowly until resistance is felt, placing engine on compression stroke with valves closed.
8. Disconnect negative black battery cable first, then positive red cable, and remove battery.
9. Store battery in cool, dry location and charge every three months.
10. Cover generator with breathable protective cover and store in dry, dust-free space.

### Engine and Fuel Troubleshooting

Problem: The engine will not start when pulling the manual recoil starter rope.
Possible cause: Manual gasoline fuel valve is closed when selector is on gasoline; selector valve is on gasoline but tank is empty; selector valve is on propane but cylinder valve is closed or empty; choke lever is in open position during a cold start; spark plug wire boot is disconnected; spark plug is fouled.
Corrective action: Open manual gasoline fuel valve; refill 8.5-gallon fuel tank; open propane cylinder valve or replace propane cylinder; slide choke lever to closed position; push spark plug wire boot onto spark plug terminal; clean, gap, or replace spark plug as detailed in Spark Plug Service.

Problem: The engine starts but immediately shuts down.
Possible cause: Engine oil level is extremely low; CO Sentinel carbon monoxide sensor detected toxic gas; fuel contains water or is stale; carburetor jet passages are clogged; excess-flow valve in propane regulator has tripped.
Corrective action: Check engine oil level and add SAE 10W-30 oil to upper limit mark on dipstick as detailed in Engine Oil Service; relocate generator to well-ventilated outdoor area at least twenty feet from buildings and allow carbon monoxide to dissipate; drain 8.5-gallon fuel tank and carburetor float bowl, then refill with fresh gasoline; clean fuel sediment cup as detailed in Fuel Sediment Cup Service; close propane cylinder valve, wait ten seconds, and open valve slowly to reset regulator.

Problem: The engine runs rough, misfires, or lacks power.
Possible cause: Spark plug is fouled, carbon-covered, or cracked; air filter foam element is dirty; carburetor is restricted or contains stale fuel; fuel selector valve is not fully engaged.
Corrective action: Inspect, clean, and gap spark plug, or replace as detailed in Spark Plug Service; wash air filter foam element in soapy water, dry, lightly oil, and reinstall as detailed in Air Filter Maintenance; drain carburetor bowl and clean jets; verify fuel selector valve is turned completely to either gasoline or propane position.

Problem: The engine speed fluctuates erratically under load.
Possible cause: Fuel flow is restricted; governor linkage is dirty, bound, or damaged; carburetor contains water or sediment; propane tank pressure is low due to freezing.
Corrective action: Clean fuel sediment cup and inspect fuel line; clean governor springs and mechanical linkage with electrical cleaner; drain carburetor bowl to remove water; warm propane cylinder by placing in a warmer environment or transition to gasoline.

Problem: Engine produces excessive black smoke from exhaust muffler.
Possible cause: Choke lever is in closed position; air filter foam element is clogged with dirt; carburetor float is stuck open.
Corrective action: Slide choke lever to open position; clean or replace air filter foam element as detailed in Air Filter Maintenance; tap carburetor float bowl gently to free stuck float or clean float assembly.

Problem: The engine backfires when shut down or running.
Possible cause: Spark plug electrode gap is incorrect; carbon buildup in engine combustion chamber; lean fuel mixture caused by restricted fuel supply.
Corrective action: Clean, gap, and reinstall spark plug as detailed in Spark Plug Service; contact authorized service dealer to clean combustion chamber; clean sediment cup and inspect fuel lines.

Problem: The engine runs for a short period and then stalls on propane only.
Possible cause: Propane cylinder is empty; excess-flow safety valve has activated due to a leak or rapid opening; propane hose is kinked or restricted.
Corrective action: Check propane cylinder level and replace if empty; close propane cylinder valve, check all fittings with soapy water, and open valve slowly; inspect and straighten flexible propane hose.

### Electrical and Alternator Troubleshooting

Problem: No electrical power is output to any control panel receptacles.
Possible cause: Double-pole main circuit breakers are in off position; GFCI outlet is tripped; carbon monoxide shutdown occurred; automatic voltage regulator is damaged.
Corrective action: Toggle 30A and 50A main circuit breakers to on position; press reset button on each of the four 120V GFCI duplex outlets; check CO Sentinel light, relocate generator, and restart; contact authorized service center to replace automatic voltage regulator.

Problem: One of the 120V duplex outlets has no power while others function correctly.
Possible cause: GFCI outlet is tripped; 20A push-button thermal protector has popped out; loose wire connection behind control panel.
Corrective action: Press reset button on affected 120V GFCI duplex outlet; check corresponding 20A push-button thermal protector, reduce connected load, and press button back inward to reset; contact service center.

Problem: Connected electrical appliances run slowly or behave erratically.
Possible cause: Engine speed is too low; generator is overloaded; voltage drop across thin or excessively long extension cords.
Corrective action: Verify engine speed corresponds to sixty hertz on digital multimeter; disconnect non-essential electrical devices; replace thin extension cords with heavy-gauge grounded cords.

Problem: The 30A or 50A double-pole main circuit breaker trips immediately when appliances are turned on.
Possible cause: Connected electrical devices exceed generator capacity; short circuit in appliance or cord; starting current of connected motor is too high.
Corrective action: Recalculate total load to ensure it is below 8000 running watts on gasoline or 7200 running watts on propane; disconnect all devices, reset breaker, and connect appliances individually to identify shorted unit; connect appliances with smaller starting currents first.

Problem: The 120V GFCI duplex outlet trips repeatedly when a specific appliance is connected.
Possible cause: Ground fault inside appliance; appliance is operating in damp or wet conditions; cord insulation is damaged.
Corrective action: Inspect appliance and cord for damaged insulation; operate appliances on dry surfaces and never run generator in rain or wet grass; replace appliance or cord.

Problem: The digital multimeter screen does not display any information.
Possible cause: Multimeter wire harness is disconnected; multimeter module is damaged; generator is not producing power.
Corrective action: Inspect wiring harness connections behind control panel; contact authorized service dealer for multimeter replacement; check AC voltage at receptacles to confirm if generator is producing power.

Problem: Heavy sputtering or electrical noise is present on sensitive electronic devices.
Possible cause: Total harmonic distortion is high due to overloading; generator is grounded poorly; electrical devices lack shielded power cables.
Corrective action: Disconnect non-essential electrical devices to reduce load below rated running capacity; check and tighten grounding wire to copper-clad steel grounding rod as detailed in Grounding the Generator in Section 3; use high-quality shielded cables.

### Starting System and Safety Sensors Troubleshooting

Problem: The engine electric starter motor will not crank when switch is held in start position.
Possible cause: Battery is discharged or disconnected; negative black battery cable is disconnected; inline 15 Amp battery fuse is blown; electric start switch is damaged.
Corrective action: Charge battery using automatic charger at 1.5 Amps as detailed in Battery Care and Fuse Replacement; connect negative black battery cable to battery terminal; replace inline 15 Amp blade fuse inside rubber holder; contact Ridgeline service center to replace switch.

Problem: The starter motor spins slowly and fails to start the engine.
Possible cause: Battery charge is low; battery terminal connections are loose or corroded.
Corrective action: Charge battery at 1.5 Amps; clean terminals with wire brush, tighten terminal bolts to forty inch-pounds, and slide rubber boots over terminals.

Problem: The CO Sentinel indicator light on the control panel flashes red and the engine will not start.
Possible cause: Carbon monoxide gas is still present around generator; CO Sentinel sensor module is dirty; sensor failure has occurred.
Corrective action: Allow generator to sit in well-ventilated open area for ten minutes to clear sensor as detailed in CO Sentinel Carbon Monoxide Shutdown Response; check sensor for dust and blow clean; contact Ridgeline support.

Problem: The engine shuts down suddenly but digital multimeter remains lit and no error lights flash.
Possible cause: Low-oil float-switch has grounded ignition; generator was tilted, triggering low-oil sensor; out of fuel.
Corrective action: Allow engine to cool, pull dipstick, and add SAE 10W-30 engine oil to upper limit mark as detailed in Low-Oil Shutdown Field Verification; place generator on flat, completely level surface; check fuel level in 8.5-gallon fuel tank or propane cylinder.

Problem: Remote start fob fails to start engine when button is pressed.
Possible cause: Electric start switch is in off position; remote key fob battery is discharged; remote key fob is out of range; key fob is not paired with receiver.
Corrective action: Turn electric start switch to run position; replace key fob battery; move closer to generator (within seventy-five feet); pair remote start fob as detailed in Starting the Engine Using the Remote Start Fob in Section 4.

Problem: The starter motor cranks but the engine fails to start when using the remote key fob.
Possible cause: Choke lever is in open position when engine is cold; fuel selector valve is not set correctly; spark plug is dirty.
Corrective action: Move choke lever to closed position; verify fuel selector valve matches fuel source; inspect, clean, or replace spark plug as detailed in Spark Plug Service.

## 6. Troubleshooting

This troubleshooting section assists the owner in diagnosing and correcting common operational, mechanical, and electrical issues with the Ridgeline Outdoor Power RG-10000D portable generator. Many minor problems can be solved by performing simple inspections using basic hand tools. Always prioritize safety: turn the electric start switch to the off position, close the manual gasoline fuel valve, close the propane cylinder valve, disconnect the spark plug wire boot, and allow the engine to cool before inspecting any components. If a problem persists, contact an authorized Ridgeline service center or a qualified technician for professional repair.

### Engine and Mechanical Subsystems

Problem: The engine recoil starter rope is extremely difficult to pull, and the engine feels locked or seized when attempting manual start.
Possible cause: Lubricating oil has bypassed the piston rings and flooded the cylinder because the generator was tilted during transport; fuel has leaked into the crankcase due to an open manual fuel valve, causing hydrostatic lock; internal components are seized from operating without adequate oil.
Corrective action: Place the generator on a level surface. Disconnect the spark plug wire and remove the spark plug. Cover the plug hole with a rag and pull the recoil handle three to five times to expel fluids. Verify spark plug gap, clean electrodes, and reinstall. Check engine oil level; if contaminated with gasoline, drain and refill the crankcase with fresh SAE 10W-30 oil.

Problem: The engine recoil starter rope pulls out of the recoil housing but does not retract back into the assembly when released.
Possible cause: The internal recoil spring has broken or slipped; the starter rope is tangled around the pulley; dirt or debris has accumulated inside the recoil housing.
Corrective action: Turn the electric start switch to off. Remove the bolts securing the recoil starter shroud. Clean any dirt from the internal pulley using compressed air. If the recoil spring is broken, replace the recoil starter assembly with a genuine Ridgeline replacement part.

Problem: The engine vibrates excessively during operation, causing the generator to walk or drift.
Possible cause: Mounting bolts securing the engine and alternator are loose; rubber vibration-isolation pads are worn or damaged; the generator is operating on an uneven surface.
Corrective action: Shut down the engine. Inspect support legs and wheel kit to ensure mounting bolts are tight. Check rubber vibration-isolation pads on support legs for wear; replace damaged pads with genuine Ridgeline parts. Place the generator on a flat, level concrete surface and verify that all four vibration-isolation mounts under the engine and alternator are tight.

### Fuel Delivery and Carburetion Problems

Problem: The engine runs normally on gasoline but stalls within a few seconds after the fuel selector is moved to PROPANE, even with a full propane tank connected.
Possible cause: The propane regulator's excess-flow safety (part RG-PR-10) has tripped because the tank valve was opened quickly, and it now restricts propane flow to a trickle.
Corrective action: Move the fuel selector to OFF, close the propane tank valve, wait thirty seconds for the RG-PR-10 excess-flow safety to reset, reopen the tank valve slowly no more than a quarter turn at first, then set the selector to PROPANE and restart.

Problem: The engine starts and runs but stalls immediately when the choke lever is moved to the open position.
Possible cause: The carburetor main jet is partially clogged with gum or varnish; there is a restriction in the fuel line or manual fuel valve; the engine is cold and has not been allowed to idle long enough to warm up.
Corrective action: Move choke lever to closed and restart. Let engine warm up under no load for two minutes before opening the choke. If engine still stalls, shut down, close fuel valve, and drain the carburetor float bowl. Clean the fuel sediment cup and carburetor jets.

Problem: Gasoline is leaking steadily from the bottom of the carburetor float bowl or the air filter housing.
Possible cause: The carburetor float is stuck in the down position due to dirt or varnish; the rubber O-ring gasket on the float bowl is damaged.
Corrective action: Close the manual gasoline fuel valve immediately. Place a container beneath the carburetor. Tighten the carburetor drain bolt. If the leak continues, remove the drain bolt to drain fuel, then gently tap the side of the float bowl to free a stuck float. If this fails, disassemble the bowl to clean the float needle and replace the O-ring gasket.

Problem: The engine runs rough, surges up and down in speed, or hunts continuously under a constant electrical load.
Possible cause: The governor linkage or springs are dirty or restricted; carburetor fuel jets are partially obstructed; the fuel tank cap vent is clogged.
Corrective action: Clean any dirt from governor springs and linkage to ensure they move freely. Loosen fuel cap slowly; if the engine stabilizes, clean or replace the cap. Drain the carburetor bowl and clean the fuel sediment cup filter screen.

### Electrical Output and Control Panel Problems

Problem: The 120V GFCI duplex outlets trip immediately when any appliance is plugged in, even when the appliance is turned off.
Possible cause: There is a direct ground fault or short circuit inside the connected appliance, the power cord, or the plug; moisture has entered the GFCI receptacle housing; the GFCI receptacle has failed.
Corrective action: Disconnect the appliance and press the reset button on the GFCI receptacle. Inspect the power cord for frayed insulation. Ensure the control panel is completely dry. Test the outlet with a different appliance. If the GFCI outlet continues to trip with no loads connected, replace the receptacle.

Problem: The generator engine runs normally, but the digital multimeter display is completely dark and there is no electrical output at any receptacle.
Possible cause: The main circuit breakers are off; the automatic voltage regulator (AVR) has failed or is disconnected; the alternator brushes are worn or not making contact.
Corrective action: Verify that the 30A double-pole main circuit breaker and 50A double-pole main circuit breaker are on. Shut down the engine, disconnect the spark plug wire, and remove the end cover of the alternator housing. Verify that the automatic voltage regulator wiring connector is plugged in. Inspect alternator brushes; replace if worn down to less than one-quarter inch.

Problem: The high-capacity 120/240V 50A outlet has no electrical power, but the four 120V GFCI duplex outlets function normally.
Possible cause: The 50A double-pole main circuit breaker has tripped due to an overload or short circuit; internal wiring connections behind the 50A receptacle have become loose.
Corrective action: Shut down the engine and disconnect all appliances from the 50A outlet. Toggle the 50A double-pole main circuit breaker off, then firmly back on to reset it. Reduce the electrical load below the rated 50A capacity before restarting the generator and reconnecting appliances.

### Battery and Starting Circuit Problems

Problem: The engine will not crank when the electric start switch is held in the start position, and there is no sound from the starter motor.
Possible cause: The twelve-volt battery is completely discharged; battery cables are loose or disconnected; the inline 15 Amp blade fuse is blown; the electric start switch is defective.
Corrective action: Tighten the battery cable connections on the battery posts. Locate the inline fuse holder along the positive red battery cable, open the cap, and inspect the 15 Amp blade fuse. Replace the fuse if the metal element is melted. If the fuse is intact, charge the battery.

Problem: The starter motor cranks the engine very slowly, but the engine fails to start and the battery quickly loses power.
Possible cause: The battery charge is low; battery terminals are corroded; engine oil viscosity is too high for cold temperatures.
Corrective action: Disconnect battery cables, negative black first, and clean corrosion from the posts with a wire brush. Apply dielectric grease, reconnect cables positive red first, and tighten terminal bolts. Charge the battery at 1.5 Amps. In cold weather, ensure crankcase is filled with SAE 5W-30 oil.

Problem: The battery inline 15 Amp fuse blows repeatedly.
Possible cause: Short circuit in the positive battery cable; incorrect battery connection.
Corrective action: Disconnect the negative black battery cable immediately. Inspect the entire length of the positive red battery cable for rubbing, pinching, or contact with hot engine parts. If insulation is worn through, wrap the damaged area with high-quality electrical tape or replace the cable assembly.

Problem: The remote start key fob does not initiate the starting sequence, but the electric start switch on the control panel functions normally.
Possible cause: The battery inside the remote start key fob is discharged; the remote key fob is out of its seventy-five foot operating range; the remote key fob has lost its programming connection to the receiver module; the electric start switch is in the off position.
Corrective action: Ensure the electric start switch on the control panel is turned to the run position to power the internal wireless receiver. Move closer to the generator, keeping within the seventy-five foot operating range. Replace the remote start key fob battery with a fresh three-volt CR2032 button cell. If the remote fob is still unresponsive, perform the manual pairing procedure.

### Safety Sensors and Protection System Problems

Problem: The CO Sentinel indicator light on the control panel flashes red, and the engine shuts down or will not start.
Possible cause: The CO Sentinel carbon monoxide sensor module has detected dangerous concentrations of carbon monoxide gas around the generator; the sensor module is experiencing an internal fault.
Corrective action: Move away from the generator immediately and allow the surrounding area to ventilate with fresh air for ten minutes. Relocate the generator to an open, outdoor area at least twenty feet away from any occupied building. Turn the electric start switch to the off position, then attempt to restart the engine.

Problem: The engine starts and runs for a few seconds, then shuts down abruptly without any indicator lights flashing.
Possible cause: The engine oil level is extremely low, causing the low-oil shutdown float-switch sensor to ground the ignition; the generator is operating on an incline.
Corrective action: Place the generator on a flat, level surface. Let the engine cool for five minutes, remove the oil fill cap, and wipe the dipstick clean. Insert the dipstick into the fill neck without threading. If the oil level is low, add SAE 10W-30 engine oil until it reaches the upper limit mark, then reinstall the cap.

Problem: The engine runs normally under load, but the digital multimeter display flashes or displays erratic characters.
Possible cause: The digital multimeter display has experienced internal electronic damage; the electrical connection harness is loose; electromagnetic interference from a non-resistor spark plug is disrupting the display.
Corrective action: Shut down the engine and disconnect the spark plug wire. Verify that the spark plug is a genuine Ridgeline F7TC resistor-type plug. If the plug is correct, verify that wire connectors on the back of the multimeter display are tight. If the display remains garbled, replace the meter.
