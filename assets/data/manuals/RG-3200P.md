## 1. Important Safety Instructions

### California Proposition 65 and Emissions Compliance
WARNING: This product contains or emits chemicals known to the State of California to cause cancer and birth defects or other reproductive harm. Engine exhaust, some of its constituents, and certain vehicle components contain or emit these chemicals. Avoid inhaling exhaust, do not idle the engine except as necessary, service your equipment in a well-ventilated area, and wear gloves or wash your hands frequently when servicing your equipment. For more information regarding state-mandated health advisories and environmental regulations, consult local authorities.

The engine installed on this Ridgeline Outdoor Power equipment is certified by the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and, where applicable, the California Air Resources Board (CARB) to meet strict emission standards for spark-ignited utility engines. Maintenance, replacement, or repair of the emission control devices and systems may be performed by any nonroad engine repair establishment or individual. However, warranty repairs must be performed by an authorized Ridgeline Outdoor Power service dealer. The emission control system consists of the carburetor, the ignition system, and the fuel tank, lines, cap, and carbon canister (where applicable). Do not alter or modify the engine in any way that would increase emissions or bypass these safety controls.

### Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Hazards
WARNING: Carbon monoxide is an odorless, colorless, and extremely toxic gas produced by internal combustion engines. Inhaling carbon monoxide can cause brain damage, severe illness, or death within minutes of exposure. Never operate the engine indoors or in enclosed spaces, even if doors and windows are open. Operating this equipment in garages, sheds, basements, or any other confined structure is strictly prohibited. Only operate the equipment outdoors, far away from open windows, doors, ventilation intakes, or other openings that could allow carbon monoxide gas to drift inside occupied structures.

Symptoms of carbon monoxide poisoning include headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness, sleepiness, confusion, and disorientation. If you or anyone else exhibits any of these symptoms while operating this pressure washer, immediately shut off the engine, move to fresh outdoor air, and seek emergency medical attention. Ensure that all persons operating this equipment are aware of the hazards of carbon monoxide and trained in proper outdoor positioning of the machine to prevent exhaust gas accumulation.

### Fuel Handling Safety and Fire Prevention
WARNING: Gasoline is highly flammable, volatile, and explosive. Liquid gasoline and its vapors can ignite from spark, static electricity, hot exhaust parts, or open flames, causing catastrophic fire or explosion, severe personal injury, or property damage.

Observe the following safety precautions during fuel handling and engine operation:
1. Store gasoline only in containers specifically designed and approved for fuel storage. Keep fuel containers out of reach of children in a cool, dry, well-ventilated location.
2. Always shut off the engine and allow it to cool completely for at least two minutes before removing the fuel cap and refueling.
3. Refuel the fuel tank outdoors in an open, well-ventilated area. Do not refuel indoors, in garages, or near ignition sources such as pilot lights.
4. Do not smoke or permit open flames or sparks near the fuel tank during refueling.
5. Avoid overfilling the fuel tank. Fill the tank only to approximately one inch below the bottom of the filler neck to allow space for fuel expansion. Securely tighten the fuel tank cap immediately after refueling.
6. Clean up any spilled fuel immediately using clean, dry rags. Move the pressure washer at least ten feet away from the fueling site before starting the engine.
7. Inspect the fuel system regularly for signs of leakage or deterioration. Replace any damaged components before operating.
8. Never use gasoline containing more than ten percent ethanol (E10), as this can damage the engine fuel system and void the product warranty. Do not use methanol or diesel fuel.
9. Avoid prolonged skin contact with gasoline or inhalation of fuel vapors. If gasoline is spilled on clothing, change clothes immediately.
10. Ensure the fuel shut-off valve is closed whenever the engine is not running and during transport.

### General Operation and Safety Rules for Pressure Washers
WARNING: This high-pressure gas pressure washer is capable of causing severe fluid injection injuries, deep lacerations, and property damage. Always exercise extreme caution during operation, maintenance, and setup.

Training and Preparation:
1. Read, understand, and follow all instructions on the machine and in this manual before attempting to assemble, operate, or service the pressure washer. Keep this manual in a safe place for future reference.
2. Familiarize yourself thoroughly with all controls and their proper operation. Know how to stop the engine and disengage the pressure quickly.
3. Never allow children under the age of sixteen to operate this pressure washer. Only responsible, trained adults should be permitted to operate the machine.
4. Wear appropriate personal protective equipment, including heavy boots with slip-resistant soles, during operation. Avoid loose-fitting clothing that can become entangled in rotating parts.
5. Always wear safety glasses or eye shields during operation and while performing adjustments or repairs to protect against back-spray and thrown debris.
6. Thoroughly inspect the area where the pressure washer is to be used. Remove all welcome mats, toys, loose boards, wires, and other foreign objects that could be propelled or damaged.
7. Adjust the pressure washer setup to clear gravel or crushed rock surfaces to prevent gravel from being picked up and projected by the high-pressure stream.
8. Wear adequate hearing protection to reduce the risk of noise-induced hearing loss from the 208cc OHV engine.

Safe Operation Practices:
1. Never put hands, feet, or body parts near the pump or high-pressure spray nozzle. Contact with the high-pressure water stream can cause severe subcutaneous fluid injection.
2. The spray gun trigger safety lock, located on the handle, must be engaged when starting the engine and whenever the operator leaves the operating position.
3. The high-pressure hose must be fully uncoiled and free of kinks when starting the engine and when the machine is not actively spraying.
4. Always operate the pressure washer from the operator's position directly behind the handles. Maintain a firm grip on the handles.
5. Exercise extreme caution when operating on or crossing gravel drives, walks, or roads. Stay alert for hidden hazards.
6. If the pressure washer strikes a foreign object, stop the engine, disconnect the spark plug wire, and inspect for damage. Repair any damage before restarting.
7. If the pressure washer starts to vibrate abnormally, stop the engine, disconnect the spark plug wire, and inspect immediately for the cause. Vibration is generally a warning of damage.
8. Stop the engine and disconnect the spark plug wire whenever you leave the operating position, before unclogging the nozzles, and before making repairs.
9. Walk slowly and carefully on slopes. Do not operate on steep slopes. Exercise caution when changing direction on slopes to prevent tipping.
10. Do not overload the machine capacity by spraying at too fast a rate. Adjust the nozzle spray pattern and distance to match cleaning conditions.
11. Never operate at high pressure on slippery surfaces. Use the quick-connect nozzles with caution and look behind to avoid falling.
12. Disengage power to the axial cam pump when transporting or not actively cleaning.
13. Never operate without good visibility. Ensure the work area is clear.
14. Ensure the spray gun trigger safety lock is engaged to prevent accidental discharge in cold conditions. Do not operate if you cannot feel temperature changes.
15. Never attempt to make any adjustments while the engine is running, except where specifically recommended.

### High-Pressure Hose and Spray Gun Safety
WARNING: The high-pressure hose and spray gun are critical safety components. Misdirecting the high-pressure spray can cause severe injury from fluid injection.

1. Never direct the high-pressure spray toward people, animals, vehicles, buildings, windows, or property that could be damaged.
2. Always adjust the spray nozzle selection and verify the spray gun safety lock is engaged from the operator's position before starting the engine.
3. Never attempt to adjust the spray nozzle or quick-connect couplings by reaching directly in front of the nozzle. Always use the spray gun safety lock.
4. If the high-pressure hose becomes clogged or damaged, disengage the pump, stop the engine, remove the key, and disconnect the spark plug wire.
5. Always use a clean-out tool or wire brush to clear a clogged nozzle. Never use your hands or feet to clear a clogged nozzle or hose.
6. Keep bystanders, children, and pets at least seventy-five feet away from the pressure washer and high-pressure hose during operation.

### Spray Nozzle and Thermal Relief Valve Safety
CAUTION: Spray nozzles and the pump thermal relief valve are critical safety components. Improper handling of nozzles or the thermal relief valve can lead to injury or pump damage.

1. Ensure quick-connect nozzles are securely locked to the end of the spray wand before operating. Check nozzle connections regularly.
2. Proceed slowly, letting the high-pressure stream cut into the dirt. Do not push the spray wand aggressively close to fragile surfaces.
3. Keep hands, face, and clothing away from the thermal relief valve, as it has a discharge port designed to vent extremely hot water.
4. Maintain the thermal relief valve in its proper operating condition to prevent accidental hot water contact.

### Children and Bystander Safety
WARNING: Tragic accidents can occur if the operator is not alert to the presence of children. Children are often attracted to the pressure washer. Never assume they will remain where last seen.

1. Keep children out of the washing area and under the watchful care of a responsible adult other than the operator.
2. Be alert and turn the pressure washer off if a child or bystander enters the immediate area of operation.

4. Use extreme care when approaching blind corners, shrubs, trees, or other objects that may block your view.

### Maintenance, Service, and Storage Safety
WARNING: Improper maintenance or unauthorized modifications to the pressure washer can result in serious injury, fire, carbon monoxide poisoning, or equipment failure.

1. Before performing maintenance, cleaning, or repairs, stop the engine, remove the safety ignition key, and disconnect the spark plug wire.

To facilitate mobility, you must install the two solid, puncture-resistant 10-inch polyurethane never-flat wheels to the axle brackets located at the lower rear of the main frame.

3. Tilt the main frame of the pressure washer forward so that it rests safely on its front rubber feet and the lower frame bumper.

4. Align the wheel axle bore with the mounting sleeve on the lower left frame bracket. The protruding bearing shoulder must face inward toward the frame.

7. Insert a cotter clip through the axle pin hole until it snaps firmly around the axle pin.
8. Repeat steps 4 through 7 on the right side of the frame bracket to install the second solid wheel.
9. Carefully lower the pressure washer back down until both never-flat wheels and the front rubber feet rest firmly on the floor. Verify that both wheels rotate freely.

### Handlebar Assembly and Nozzle Storage Panel
The two-piece frame design requires the secure installation of the upper handlebar assembly onto the lower frame tubes to ensure structural rigidity and isolate vibration.
1. Locate the upper handlebar assembly, which features the padded operator handle, the nozzle storage panel, the spray gun holster, and the high-pressure hose storage hook.

7. Hand-tighten both star knobs firmly by turning them clockwise until the upper handlebar is secured.

8. Locate the five color-coded quick-connect spray nozzles from the accessory tray.
9. Push each nozzle into its designated rubber storage grommet on the handlebar panel. Press the nozzles firmly downward until the collar of the nozzle sits flat against the rubber surface. Organize the nozzles in order of spray angle from left to right: Red (0°), Yellow (15°), Green (25°), White (40°), and Black (Soap).

### Connecting the Spray Gun, Spray Wand, and High-Pressure Hose
To ensure leak-free operation under the extreme hydraulic pressure of 3,200 PSI, all high-pressure connections must be clean, unmarred, and tightened to their exact mechanical stops. Refer to section 2. Specifications for connection details.
1. Remove any plastic protective shipping caps from the inlet and outlet fittings of the pump, the spray gun, and the ends of the 25-foot high-pressure hose. Inspect all threads and internal rubber O-rings.
WARNING: Never operate the pressure washer with damaged, worn, or missing O-rings. High-pressure water can easily blow past damaged seals, resulting in severe fluid injection injuries.
2. Thread the male end of the 21-inch metal spray wand into the female outlet port (M22 fitting) at the front of the spray gun. Turn the connection collar clockwise by hand until it is fully engaged.
3. Hand-tighten the spray wand collar firmly. Do not use wrenches or pliers to tighten this fitting, as excessive torque can crack internal plastic components or strip the threads.
4. Locate the 25-foot high-pressure hose. Uncoil the hose completely to remove any twists or kinks. Operating a kinked hose under high pressure can cause the hose structure to fail.
5. Align one of the M22 female threaded hose connections with the male inlet fitting at the bottom of the spray gun handle.
6. Push the hose fitting into the gun inlet and thread the collar clockwise by hand until tight.
7. Verify that there is no axial play or loose movement at the connection.
8. Align the second female connection of the hose with the high-pressure outlet fitting on the direct-drive axial cam pump.
9. Push the hose fitting onto the pump outlet and hand-tighten the collar until snug. Ensure that the hose does not drape over or come into contact with the hot engine muffler.
10. Store the fully connected spray gun and wand assembly in the integrated holster on the handlebar. Loop the high-pressure hose neatly over the integrated hose storage hook.

### Onboard Detergent Tank Hose Connection
The RG-3200P features an onboard 0.8-gallon detergent tank. To enable chemical draw when utilizing the black soap nozzle, the siphon tube must be properly connected.
1. Locate the clear plastic chemical siphon tube pre-attached to the bottom outlet of the onboard detergent tank.
2. Route the clear plastic tube under the engine platform, ensuring it is clear of any hot engine components or the rotating recoil starter cover.
3. Locate the brass chemical injection barb on the outlet side of the pump, adjacent to the M22 high-pressure hose outlet fitting.
4. Push the open end of the clear plastic siphon tube firmly over the brass chemical injection barb. Ensure the tube is pushed completely past the raised ridges of the barb so that it forms an airtight seal.
CAUTION: Do not use any tools to crimp or tighten the siphon tube onto the barb. If the tube is loose, a small zip-tie may be placed over the connection to prevent air leaks, which would stall chemical drawing.
5. Verify that the siphon tube is not kinked, flattened, or pinched beneath the engine mounting plate. An air leak or a kink in this tube will prevent the pump from drawing detergent.

### Engine Oil Pre-Start Preparation
WARNING: This engine is shipped from the factory without oil in the crankcase. Operating the 208cc OHV engine without adding the proper type and amount of engine oil will cause immediate, catastrophic mechanical failure and void the warranty. You must fill the crankcase with the specified engine oil before attempting to start the engine.

1. Position the pressure washer on a flat, level, and solid horizontal surface. Filling the engine on an incline or uneven surface will result in an inaccurate oil level reading.
2. Clean the area surrounding the yellow engine oil fill cap/dipstick, located at the lower rear of the engine block, using a clean, dry rag to prevent dirt from falling into the crankcase.
3. Turn the yellow oil fill cap/dipstick counterclockwise and pull it straight out of the crankcase fill neck.
4. Wipe the metal dipstick blade completely dry.
5. Insert the provided angled oil funnel into the oil fill neck.
6. Slowly pour the entire contents of the provided 20-fluid ounce (0.6-liter) bottle of premium SAE 10W-30 engine oil into the oil funnel. Allow several minutes for the oil to flow completely down and settle.
7. Remove the oil funnel and insert the clean dipstick straight back into the oil fill neck. Push it completely in and turn it clockwise until it is fully threaded.
8. Unscrew the yellow oil fill cap/dipstick and pull it out to inspect the oil level on the dipstick blade.
9. Verify that the oil level is at the "FULL" mark (or the upper limit of the crosshatched pattern) on the dipstick. The oil level must never be below the "LOW" mark.
CAUTION: Overfilling the crankcase can cause excessive smoke, spark plug fouling, oil aeration, and oil to enter the air filter housing. Underfilling will trigger the low-oil shutdown sensor.
10. Re-insert the dipstick and hand-tighten the yellow oil fill cap clockwise. Clean up any oil spills or drips on the frame or engine block using a dry rag before proceeding.

### Fueling the Engine Fuel Tank
WARNING: Gasoline is highly volatile, flammable, and explosive. Observe all safety guidelines outlined in "Fuel Handling Safety and Fire Prevention" in Section 1 before adding fuel to the tank. Always refuel outdoors in a well-ventilated area, with the engine shut off and cooled for at least two minutes.

1. Ensure the pressure washer is positioned on a level, stable, outdoor surface, far away from any open flames, sparks, pilot lights, or dry vegetation.
2. Clean the area around the black fuel tank cap on top of the fuel tank to prevent dirt, water, or debris from contaminating the fuel system.
3. Turn the black fuel tank cap counterclockwise and remove it from the tank filler neck. Set the cap down on a clean, dry surface.
4. Inspect the internal fuel strainer cup inside the filler neck. Ensure it is clean, correctly seated, and free of any debris. Never pour fuel directly into the tank if the fuel strainer is missing.
5. Using an approved clean fuel container, slowly pour fresh, clean, unleaded gasoline (minimum 87 octane, with a maximum of 10 percent ethanol content by volume) into the fuel tank.
6. Stop filling when the fuel level reaches approximately one inch below the bottom of the fuel filler neck to allow space for fuel expansion.
WARNING: Do not overfill the fuel tank. Gasoline expands significantly as the engine heats up. Overfilling can cause fuel to spill onto a hot engine or muffler, resulting in an immediate fire or explosion hazard.
7. Re-install the black fuel tank cap and turn it clockwise. Continue turning until you hear several distinct clicks, indicating that the cap is fully sealed and locked.
8. Wipe away any spilled fuel immediately using dry rags. Move the pressure washer at least ten feet away from the fueling location before starting the engine.

### Connection to Water Source
The pump is designed to operate under continuous water feed. Running the pump dry, even for a few seconds, will cause extreme friction heat, ruin the high-pressure water seals, and score the stainless steel plungers, voiding the pump warranty.
1. Verify that the garden hose water supply is capable of delivering a continuous flow rate of at least 5.0 GPM at a steady pressure of 20 to 80 PSI. Do not attempt to use a rain barrel or bucket, as the pump is not self-priming.
2. Inspect the female garden hose inlet coupling on the pump. Ensure the internal wire mesh inlet screen is clean, intact, and seated flat inside the coupling to prevent debris from entering.
WARNING: Never operate the pressure washer without the wire mesh inlet screen in place. Debris entering the pump will damage the check valves and cause catastrophic pump failure.
3. Run water through your garden hose for 30 seconds before connecting it to the pressure washer to clear any trapped air, dirt, or debris out of the line.
4. Thread the male end of the garden hose into the female garden hose inlet coupling on the pump. Tighten the connection collar clockwise by hand until it is completely snug. Ensure the garden hose is not cross-threaded.
5. Turn the water supply valve completely on. Check the connection at the pump inlet for any leaks.
6. Before starting the engine, you must purge all trapped air from the water system. Disengage the spray gun trigger safety lock.
7. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger. Water will flow out of the spray wand at low municipal pressure. Continue holding the trigger until a steady, unbroken stream of water flows from the wand, free of air bubbles.
8. Release the trigger and engage the trigger safety lock. The pressure washer is now fully assembled and primed for immediate operation.

## 4. Operation

Operating the Ridgeline Outdoor Power RG-3200P Gas Pressure Washer safely and effectively requires a thorough understanding of its operational controls, start-up procedures, hydraulic characteristics, and surface-specific cleaning methodologies. This section provides detailed instructions designed to maximize the performance of your 208cc OHV engine and direct-drive axial cam pump while maintaining strict compliance with safety regulations to prevent physical injury or equipment damage.

### Pre-Operation Inspection Checklist
Before starting the pressure washer engine, the operator must perform a comprehensive pre-operation inspection. This routine ensures that all critical subsystems are in safe operating condition, fluid levels are within specification, and the hydraulic circuit is structurally sound. Do not attempt to start or operate the machine if any component is damaged, leaking, or improperly configured.

1. Check the Unit Placement: Position the pressure washer on a flat, level, and solid horizontal surface outdoors. The machine must be operated on level ground to ensure proper gravity splash lubrication of the internal engine components and to prevent the low-oil shutdown sensor from prematurely stopping the engine. Ensure the unit is placed in a well-ventilated outdoor area, at least 20 feet away from any building windows, doors, or ventilation intakes, to prevent toxic carbon monoxide gas from accumulating or entering occupied structures.
2. Inspect for Fuel and Oil Leaks: Conduct a visual inspection of the entire engine block, carburetor, fuel lines, fuel tank, and undercarriage of the unit. Check for any wetness, residue, or the distinct odor of gasoline or engine oil. If a leak is detected, do not start the engine. Clean up all spills and have the unit serviced by an authorized Ridgeline Outdoor Power dealer before further use.
3. Verify the Engine Oil Level: Remove the yellow engine oil fill cap/dipstick, wipe the blade clean with a lint-free cloth, re-insert the dipstick fully into the fill neck, and remove it again to read the oil level. The oil level must sit precisely at the "FULL" mark or within the crosshatched region of the dipstick. If the oil level is low, add premium SAE 10W-30 engine oil in small increments using a clean funnel, as detailed in Section 3, until the proper level is achieved. Securely hand-tighten the dipstick.
4. Check the Fuel Supply: Check the fuel level in the fuel tank. If refueling is necessary, ensure the engine is off and has cooled for at least two minutes. Refuel outdoors using fresh, clean, unleaded gasoline (minimum 87 octane, maximum 10 percent ethanol content), leaving at least one inch of airspace below the filler neck for fuel expansion. Securely tighten the black fuel tank cap until it clicks multiple times.
5. Inspect the High-Pressure Hose: Uncoil the 25-foot high-pressure hose completely and inspect its entire length. Look for physical abnormalities, including deep cuts, severe abrasions, localized bulges, kinks, soft spots, or exposed wire braiding. Check that the metal M22 threaded fittings are clean and that the internal elastomeric O-rings are present, fully seated, and free of cracks or dry rot. 
WARNING: Never attempt to use a high-pressure hose that is damaged, kinked, or shows signs of wear. Under the extreme hydraulic pressure of 3,200 PSI, a compromised hose can rupture catastrophically, discharging a high-velocity stream of water capable of causing severe fluid injection injuries or deep lacerations.
6. Verify Spray Gun and Wand Integrity: Check that the 21-inch metal spray wand is threaded securely into the ergonomic spray gun handle's M22 female outlet port. Inspect the spray gun assembly for cracks or structural failures. Test the trigger mechanism to ensure it moves smoothly and returns to its neutral position when released. Engage the red spray gun trigger safety lock located on the underside of the trigger handle. The trigger must not be able to depress when the safety lock is in the active position.
7. Inspect the Pump Water Inlet Screen: Examine the female garden hose inlet coupling on the direct-drive axial cam pump. Use a flashlight to verify that the internal stainless steel wire mesh inlet screen is clean, seated flat, and free of mineral scale, algae, or debris. If the screen is dirty, remove it, flush it with clean water, and reinstall it. If the screen is damaged or missing, do not connect the water supply.
8. Check the Never-Flat Wheels and Hardware: Ensure that both 10-inch polyurethane never-flat wheels are securely attached to the axle pins with their respective flat washers and cotter clips. Verify that the two plastic star knobs securing the upper handlebar assembly are tight and that the rubber storage grommets on the nozzle storage panel are intact.

### Water Supply Requirements and Hose Connections
The direct-drive axial cam pump installed on the RG-3200P relies on a continuous, uninterrupted flow of pressurized municipal water to lubricate and cool its internal valves, seals, and stainless steel plungers. Insufficient water supply will cause immediate cavitation, which induces intense vibration, loss of discharge pressure, and catastrophic thermal damage to the pump's hydraulic head.

To ensure proper pump operation, the water source must meet the following technical criteria:
*   Water Volume and Flow Rate: The source must deliver a minimum flow rate of 5.0 GPM. This is double the pump's rated discharge of 2.5 GPM, providing an adequate hydraulic margin to prevent cavitation during operation.
*   Source Pressure: The supply pressure must be regulated between a minimum of 20 PSI and a maximum of 80 PSI. Do not connect the pressure washer to water sources with pressures exceeding 80 PSI, as excessive inlet pressure can damage the low-pressure seals inside the pump.
*   Water Temperature: The inlet water temperature must not exceed 100 degrees Fahrenheit (38 degrees Celsius). Never connect this pressure washer to a hot water tap or residential hot water system. Warm water degrades the life of the pump's internal seals and accelerates thermal loading.
*   Supply Hose Length and Diameter: Use a heavy-duty, reinforced garden hose with a minimum internal diameter of 5/8 inch (16 millimeters). The supply garden hose should not exceed 50 feet (15.2 meters) in length to minimize frictional pressure drops and restrictional flow losses.

To connect the water supply to the pressure washer, follow this procedure:
1. Ensure the municipal water faucet is fully closed.
2. Uncoil your 5/8-inch garden hose completely to remove any twists, loops, or kinks.
3. Connect the female end of the garden hose to your municipal water faucet and tighten it securely.
4. Turn the municipal water faucet on fully for 30 seconds to flush out any loose rust, soil, scale, or trapped air from the interior of the hose. Once the water runs completely clear, shut off the faucet.
5. Align the male threaded brass fitting of the clean garden hose with the female 3/4-inch garden hose inlet coupling on the axial cam pump.
6. Thread the garden hose into the pump inlet clockwise by hand until it is tight and fully seated against the wire mesh inlet screen. Ensure the connection is not cross-threaded, which would allow air to draw into the pump during operation. Do not use wrenches or pliers, as over-tightening can crack the aluminum alloy pump housing.
7. Turn the municipal water faucet on fully. Inspect the inlet connection for water leaks. If water drips from the connection, shut off the faucet, disconnect the garden hose, verify that the wire mesh screen is seated flat, and reconnect the hose.

### Priming the Pump and Purging Air
Before starting the 208cc OHV engine, the entire high-pressure hydraulic circuit—including the axial cam pump, the 25-foot hose, the spray gun, and the 21-inch wand—must be primed with water and purged of all trapped air. Starting the engine with air pockets in the pump head or hoses creates a condition known as hydraulic shock or "water hammer." This shock wave can fracture the internal ceramic-coated plungers, blow out the high-pressure seals, and cause the engine to kick back violently when you pull the recoil starter.

CAUTION: Running the direct-drive axial cam pump dry, or operating the engine before purging all air from the water circuit, will cause immediate friction-induced heat build-up. This will destroy the high-pressure water seals and score the pump pistons within seconds, voiding the product warranty.

To safely purge air and prime the pump, execute the following steps in sequence:
1. Ensure the engine fuel valve is in the closed position and the ignition switch is set to the OFF position.
2. Confirm that the municipal garden hose is connected to the pump inlet and that the water supply valve is opened completely.
3. Make sure the 25-foot high-pressure hose is securely connected to the pump's M22 outlet fitting and the spray gun's M22 inlet fitting.
4. Remove any spray nozzles from the 1/4-inch brass quick-connect coupler at the tip of the 21-inch spray wand. Operating with an open wand allows air and debris to exit the system with minimal restriction.
5. Point the spray wand in a safe direction, away from yourself, bystanders, pets, and any electrical sources.
6. Disengage the red trigger safety lock on the spray gun handle.
7. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger completely. Trapped air will begin escaping from the open wand, followed by a sputtering, inconsistent mixture of water and air.
8. Continue holding the spray gun trigger depressed until a solid, steady, and unbroken stream of low-pressure water flows continuously from the tip of the wand for at least 15 seconds.
9. Release the spray gun trigger to halt the water flow.
10. Engage the red trigger safety lock. The hydraulic circuit is now fully primed, and the pressure washer is ready for the engine starting procedure.

### Starting the Engine (Cold and Warm Starts)
The 208cc OHV engine features a reliable transistorized magneto ignition system and an automatic compression release mechanism. However, because the engine is directly coupled to the axial cam pump, pulling the recoil starter rope rotates both the engine crankshaft and the pump pistons simultaneously. If water pressure has built up inside the pump head, the resistance can make the starter rope extremely difficult to pull, and it may snap back violently, causing hand or arm injury. Squeezing the spray gun trigger to relieve backpressure during the starting sequence is a critical requirement.

WARNING: Never start or run the engine indoors or in a poorly ventilated area. Engine exhaust contains carbon monoxide, an odorless, colorless, and highly toxic gas that can cause unconsciousness, brain damage, or death within minutes. Only operate the engine outdoors, far from doors, windows, and ventilation intakes.

To start a cold engine (an engine that has not been run recently, or has cooled completely):
1. Complete all pre-operation checks, connect the water supply, and prime the pump as described in the preceding sections.
2. Locate the fuel shut-off valve lever beneath the air filter housing on the side of the engine. Slide the fuel valve lever to the far right (ON) position to allow fuel to flow into the carburetor float bowl.
3. Locate the choke lever, situated directly above the fuel valve lever. Slide the choke lever to the far left (CHOKE) position. This restricts airflow into the carburetor, enriching the fuel mixture for a cold start.
4. Locate the engine ignition switch (rocker switch) on the side of the engine block. Flip the switch to the ON (I) position.
5. Standing behind the unit, grasp the spray gun handle firmly with your right hand. Disengage the red trigger safety lock.
6. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger fully with your right hand to relieve any hydrostatic pressure that has built up in the pump. Point the wand in a safe direction. Keep the trigger squeezed throughout the engine pull-start process.
7. Use your left hand to grasp the plastic recoil starter handle. Pull the starter rope slowly until you feel increased mechanical resistance. This indicates the engine piston is reaching the top of its compression stroke.
8. Once resistance is felt, pull the starter rope rapidly and firmly in a single, continuous, upward and outward motion. Do not let go of the starter handle at the end of the pull; guide the rope slowly back into the recoil housing to prevent damage to the starter spring or pulley.
9. If the engine fires but does not continue to run, repeat steps 7 and 8.
10. As soon as the engine starts and begins to idle smoothly, gradually slide the choke lever to the far right (RUN) position over a period of 5 to 10 seconds. The engine speed is governed at a fixed 3,600 RPM; do not attempt to adjust the governor settings.
11. Release the spray gun trigger once the engine is idling smoothly, and immediately engage the red trigger safety lock to prevent accidental high-pressure water discharge.

To start a warm engine (an engine that has been shut down for less than 15 minutes and is still at operating temperature):
1. Turn the engine ignition switch to the ON (I) position.
2. Verify that the fuel shut-off valve is in the ON position.
3. Ensure the choke lever is placed in the RUN position. Do not apply choke to a warm engine, as this will flood the combustion chamber and prevent the engine from starting.
4. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger to relieve hydraulic pressure.
5. Pull the recoil starter handle rapidly and firmly until the engine starts.
6. Once running, release the trigger and engage the spray gun safety lock.

### Selecting and Utilizing the Quick-Connect Nozzles
The Ridgeline Outdoor Power RG-3200P is supplied with five color-coded, quick-connect spray nozzles that are stored on the handlebar panel. Each nozzle features a unique internal orifice size and spray angle designed to modify the velocity and shape of the high-pressure water stream. Selecting the correct nozzle is critical to achieving effective cleaning results while preventing permanent damage to the target surface.

The characteristics and intended applications of each nozzle are detailed below:
*   0-Degree Red Nozzle (Pinpoint Stream): This nozzle delivers a highly concentrated, non-fanning, pencil-thin stream of water at maximum impact velocity. It covers a very small surface area and is capable of gouging wood, stripping paint, and fracturing concrete.
    *   *Applications*: Removing baked-on mud from heavy industrial machinery, clearing rust from steel structural beams, or removing silicone or concrete splatter from metal surfaces.
    *   *Warning*: Never use this nozzle on wood, siding, asphalt shingles, vehicles, or soft masonry. Keep the nozzle at least 24 inches away from any surface.
*   15-Degree Yellow Nozzle (Chisel Stream): This nozzle produces a narrow, high-impact fan of water that acts like a scraper. It is designed for aggressive surface preparation and stripping.
    *   *Applications*: Stripping peeling paint, stain, or varnish from solid wood decks and fences; cleaning grease, oil, and heavy grime from concrete driveways, brick walkways, and stone retaining walls.
    *   *Caution*: Use with extreme care on softwood decking and siding; keep the tip moving continuously to prevent scarring.
*   25-Degree Green Nozzle (Flushing Stream): This nozzle produces a medium-width fan of water that provides an optimal balance between impact pressure and surface coverage. It is the most versatile and commonly used nozzle for residential cleaning.
    *   *Applications*: Washing mold, mildew, and dirt from vinyl, aluminum, or fiber-cement siding; sweeping leaves, lawn clippings, and light dirt from patios and concrete garage floors; cleaning gutters and brickwork.
*   40-Degree White Nozzle (Wash Stream): This nozzle generates a wide, low-impact fan of water. It distributes the 3,200 PSI over a much larger surface area, making it safe for delicate and painted surfaces.
    *   *Applications*: Washing automobiles, motorcycles, boats, and RVs; cleaning outdoor furniture, windows, and glass patio doors; rinsing light dust or pollen from painted wood, stucco, or delicate masonry.
*   Black Soap Nozzle (Chemical Application): This nozzle has a very large internal orifice that drops the overall system operating pressure significantly. The low pressure creates a vacuum (venturi effect) in the pump's chemical injection barb, allowing the siphon tube to draw detergent from the onboard tank.
    *   *Applications*: Siphoning and applying pressure washer-approved soaps, detergents, and degreasers onto dirty surfaces before high-pressure rinsing.
    *   *Note*: The chemical injection siphon will only function when this black low-pressure soap nozzle is installed on the spray wand.

To safely install or change a quick-connect nozzle:
1. Release the spray gun trigger and engage the red trigger safety lock on the handle.
2. Point the spray wand down toward the ground in a safe direction.
3. Pull back the outer brass collar of the 1/4-inch quick-connect coupler on the tip of the wand using your thumb and forefinger.
4. Insert the hex shank of the desired color-coded nozzle straight into the coupler. Press the nozzle firmly inward until it bottom out.
5. Release the brass collar, allowing it to spring forward into its locked position. You should hear a distinct click as the collar snaps forward.
6. Pull firmly on the nozzle to verify that it is securely locked in place. If the nozzle slides out, pull the collar back, push the nozzle in deeper, and re-engage the collar.
WARNING: Never attempt to change a nozzle while the spray gun trigger is squeezed or while the water system is pressurized. Always engage the spray gun safety lock. A partially locked or loose nozzle will be violently launched from the wand under 3,200 PSI of water pressure, creating a high-velocity projectile that can cause severe eye injury or skin puncture.

### Operating the Onboard Detergent Tank and Chemical Siphon
The RG-3200P is equipped with an integrated 0.8-gallon (3.0-liter) onboard detergent tank. The chemical injection system utilizes a venturi siphon to mix concentrated detergent with the water stream at a calibrated draw ratio of approximately 12:1 to 15:1. This system allows you to apply cleaning chemicals directly to surfaces, dramatically improving the efficiency of the washing process.

Observe the following safety guidelines and operational procedures when using detergents:
*   Approved Chemicals Only: Use only biodegradable, non-toxic detergents and soaps specifically formulated and labeled for use in high-pressure washers. These chemicals are designed not to degrade the polyurethane and stainless steel seals inside the pump.
WARNING: Never use household liquid bleach, ammonia, highly acidic chemicals, strong alkaline solutions, organic solvents, flammable liquids, or abrasive powders in this detergent tank. Utilizing unapproved chemicals can cause toxic gas release, chemical burns, or permanent chemical corrosion of the pump head, which will void the warranty.
*   Detergent Dilution: Carefully read the mixing instructions on the detergent bottle. Many commercial concentrates must be diluted with clean water before being poured into the onboard tank to ensure smooth, consistent siphoning.
*   Detergent Application Procedure:
    1. Pour the prepared, diluted detergent into the 0.8-gallon onboard detergent tank. Securely thread the cap onto the tank.
    2. Verify that the clear plastic chemical siphon tube is firmly connected to the brass barb on the pump outlet, as described in Section 3.
    3. Install the color-coded Black Soap Nozzle into the 1/4-inch quick-connect coupler on the spray wand. The siphon will not function with any other nozzle.
    4. Disengage the spray gun trigger safety lock.
    5. Point the wand at the target surface and squeeze the trigger. Within 10 to 15 seconds, detergent will be drawn through the clear tube and will begin discharging from the nozzle as a low-pressure, soapy spray.
    6. Apply the detergent to the surface starting from the bottom of the work area and working upward. Applying soap from the bottom up prevents dirty streaks from forming and burning into the dry surface.
    7. Allow the detergent to remain on the surface for 3 to 5 minutes to break down dirt and grime. Do not allow the detergent to dry completely on the surface, as this can cause permanent staining or damage to paint and siding.
    8. Once the chemical has set, release the trigger, engage the safety lock, and replace the black soap nozzle with the appropriate high-pressure nozzle (e.g., Green 25° or White 40°) to rinse the area.
*   System Flushing Procedure:
    To prevent dry soap residue, chemical crystals, or sticky film from clogging the internal ball-check valve in the brass venturi injector, you must flush the chemical siphon system after every use.
    1. Turn off the engine and allow the system to cool.
    2. Empty any remaining detergent from the onboard tank.
    3. Fill the detergent tank with approximately one quart of clean, lukewarm water.
    4. Install the black soap nozzle, start the engine using the proper starting procedure, and squeeze the spray gun trigger.
    5. Spray the clean water through the soap nozzle for 2 minutes. This flushes the chemical tube, siphon screen, and pump injection port with fresh water.
    6. Shut down the engine, turn off the water supply, and depressurize the system.

### Safe Spraying Guidelines, Distance, and Sweep Speed
The immense hydraulic energy generated by the RG-3200P is highly effective for cleaning, but it can also be incredibly destructive if misapplied. The force of the high-pressure water stream can erode masonry, gouge wood fibers, slice through tire sidewalls, and shatter window glass. Understanding how to manage kickback force, spray distance, and sweeping speed is essential for safe and damage-free operation.

*   Managing Kickback Force: When you squeeze the spray gun trigger, a backward reactive force is generated immediately as high-pressure water exits the nozzle. This is called kickback, and it can cause the spray wand to jerk upward or sideways, potentially throwing the operator off balance or causing the wand to strike the face or body.
    *   *Operator Stance*: Always stand on a stable, level surface. Do not operate the pressure washer from a ladder, scaffolding, step stool, or any elevated platform that does not have secure handrails. Keep both feet planted firmly, slightly wider than shoulder-width apart.
    *   *Two-Handed Grip*: Grip the spray gun handle firmly with your dominant hand, wrapping your fingers securely around the trigger and handle grip. Place your non-dominant hand on the insulated grip sleeve of the metal spray wand. Never operate the pressure washer with one hand.
*   Spray Distance and Angle: The impact pressure exerted on a surface decreases exponentially as the nozzle is moved farther away. Conversely, bringing the nozzle too close to a surface concentrated the energy on a small point and can cause instant damage.
    *   *Initial Distance*: Always begin spraying with the nozzle positioned at least 3 to 4 feet away from the target surface. Squeeze the trigger and slowly bring the nozzle closer, stopping when you observe that the water is effectively removing the dirt without damaging the material.
    *   *Angled Spraying*: Do not hold the nozzle perpendicular (90 degrees) to the surface being washed. Pointing the spray stream directly at a surface drives dirt and water deep into the pores of the material and can cause surface erosion. Instead, hold the wand at a 45-degree angle relative to the surface. This creates a chiseling action that lifts dirt up and sweeps it away to the side, minimizing direct impact force.
*   Sweeping Motion: Keep the nozzle moving continuously across the surface. Do not hold the high-pressure stream stationary over a single point, as this can quickly cut into wood siding, concrete sealers, or paint. Use a steady, overlapping, side-to-side sweeping motion. Maintain a consistent distance from the surface throughout the entire sweep to ensure an even, uniform clean without dark or light "striping" marks.

### Thermal Relief Valve System Operation and Idle Limitations
When the engine of the RG-3200P is running at its governed speed of 3,600 RPM but the operator has released the spray gun trigger, the pump enters a bypass mode. In this mode, the pump's internal bypass valve continuously recirculates the same small volume of water inside the pump head instead of discharging it. 

As the pistons continue to stroke, friction from the moving parts and the compression of the water generate intense heat. If this bypass state continues for more than two minutes, the water inside the pump head will quickly reach temperatures exceeding 140 degrees Fahrenheit (60 degrees Celsius). This extreme heat will melt the internal polymer seals, damage the check valves, and crack the ceramic-coated pistons.

To protect the pump from catastrophic thermal failure, a specialized thermal relief valve is threaded into the underside of the pump body.
*   Activation Threshold: When the water temperature inside the pump head reaches approximately 140 degrees Fahrenheit (60 degrees Celsius), the thermal relief valve opens automatically.
*   Discharge Behavior: Once open, the valve discharges a small stream of extremely hot water from its lower drain port onto the ground beneath the pressure washer frame. This allows fresh, cold municipal water from your garden hose to enter the pump head, immediately cooling the internal components.
*   Automatic Reset: As soon as the internal water temperature drops below the thermal threshold, the valve closes automatically and seals the system.
*   Safety Warning:
WARNING: Keep your hands, feet, and face clear of the thermal relief valve's drain port located under the pump housing. The water discharged from this valve is scalding hot and can cause severe burns on contact. Do not touch the valve or the surrounding metal pump housing during or immediately after engine operation.
*   Idle Limitation Rule: The thermal relief valve is intended as an emergency fail-safe device only. It is not designed to handle frequent, prolonged cycling. 
CAUTION: Never allow the pressure washer engine to run for more than two consecutive minutes without squeezing the spray gun trigger to discharge water. If you must stop washing to move hoses, clear debris, or take a break for longer than two minutes, you must shut off the engine immediately. This practice prevents pump overheating, extends seal life, and conserves fuel.

### Shutting Down the Pressure Washer and System Depressurization
When you have finished your cleaning task, or when you need to perform maintenance or store the machine, you must follow a strict shutdown and depressurization procedure. Simply turning off the engine does not remove the high-pressure water trapped within the 25-foot hose and the pump head. This trapped hydrostatic pressure makes it impossible to disconnect the hoses and represents a major safety hazard if the trigger is pulled accidentally.

To shut down and completely depressurize the system:
1. Release the spray gun trigger and engage the red trigger safety lock.
2. Allow the engine to run at idle for 15 to 30 seconds to allow the combustion chamber and exhaust system to cool gradually.
3. Locate the red engine ignition switch on the engine block and flip it to the OFF (O) position. The engine will stop rotating.
4. Locate the fuel shut-off valve lever and slide it to the far left (OFF) position. This cuts off fuel flow to the carburetor and prevents fuel pooling during transport or storage.
5. Walk to your municipal water faucet and turn the water supply valve completely off.
6. Return to the pressure washer operating position and grasp the spray gun handle.
7. Disengage the red trigger safety lock.
8. Point the spray wand in a safe direction and squeeze the trigger. Hold the trigger depressed until all trapped water and high-pressure air have been completely discharged. The water will transition from a high-pressure burst to a low-pressure trickle, and then stop completely.
9. Verify that the system is fully depressurized by letting go of the trigger, waiting 5 seconds, and squeezing it once more. No water should discharge.
10. Engage the red trigger safety lock.
11. Disconnect the garden hose from the pump inlet by turning the connection collar counterclockwise. Residual water will drain from the hose and pump inlet.
12. Disconnect the high-pressure hose from the M22 pump outlet and the spray gun handle inlet by unscrewing the threaded collars counterclockwise.
13. Drain all remaining water from the high-pressure hose, the spray gun, and the metal wand before coiling the hose and storing the components on their respective frame hooks.

### High Altitude and Extreme Environmental Operation
Operating the RG-3200P gas pressure washer under extreme environmental conditions, such as high altitudes or extreme ambient temperatures, requires specific adjustments and precautions to maintain optimal engine performance and prevent mechanical wear.

*   High Altitude Operation: At altitudes above 3,000 feet (914 meters), the atmospheric air density is significantly lower. This reduction in oxygen concentration alters the carburetor's air-fuel mixture, causing the engine to run "rich" (too much fuel, not enough air).
    *   *Symptoms of Unadjusted Altitude Operation*: Rough engine idling, excessive black smoke from the muffler, spark plug fouling, high fuel consumption, difficulty starting, and a severe reduction in engine horsepower, which can cause the pump discharge pressure to drop below 3,200 PSI.
    *   *Corrective Action*: If you regularly operate this pressure washer at altitudes above 3,000 feet, you must have an authorized Ridgeline Outdoor Power service dealer install a high-altitude carburetor jet kit. This kit adjusts the fuel metering nozzle to match the lower air density.
    *   *Caution*: If an engine modified for high-altitude use is operated at altitudes below 3,000 feet, the carburetor will supply a "lean" mixture (too much air, not enough fuel). This can cause the engine to overheat, run erratically, and sustain severe internal cylinder or valve damage. Ensure the original carburetor jetting is reinstalled when returning the unit to low-altitude environments.
*   High Ambient Temperature Operation: Operating the unit in temperatures exceeding 100 degrees Fahrenheit (38 degrees Celsius) accelerates the thermal loading of both the engine and the pump.
    *   Ensure the engine's cooling fins and recoil starter cover are completely clear of dust, leaves, and debris, as air restriction will cause rapid engine overheating.
    *   Check engine oil levels more frequently. High temperatures degrade oil viscosity faster, which can lead to increased oil consumption and friction.
    *   Be aware that the thermal relief valve will activate much faster under high ambient heat, as the incoming municipal water source is also warmer and provides less cooling margin.
*   Near-Freezing and Cold Weather Operation: Operating the pressure washer in temperatures approaching 32 degrees Fahrenheit (0 degrees Celsius) carries a severe risk of water freezing inside the pump head or hoses.
    *   If water freezes inside the axial cam pump, the ice expands. The immense pressure generated by expanding ice can crack the aluminum pump manifold, shatter the stainless steel pistons, and split the brass fittings.
    *   Do not operate the machine if the ambient temperature is below 40 degrees Fahrenheit (4.4 degrees Celsius).
    *   If you must use the machine in cold conditions, never leave the unit sitting with water in the pump when the engine is shut off. Immediately drain all hoses and perform the cold-weather storage preparation (using a pump saver fluid) as outlined in the storage section of this manual.

The extreme environments in which pressure washers operate—characterized by high vibration, elevated mechanical loads, chemical exposure, and constant water contact—place significant stress on both the 208cc OHV utility engine and the direct-drive axial cam pump. ### Engine Maintenance Procedures
The 208cc OHV engine installed on your pressure washer requires precise lubricating, air filtration, and electrical service to maintain its emissions compliance and rated power.

#### Engine Oil Inspection and Change
Engine oil lubricates the high-speed internal components of your engine, including the piston, connecting rod, and overhead valves. Over time, engine oil degrades due to heat, fuel dilution, and combustion blow-by, losing its lubricating viscosity and carrying suspended metal wear particles. Changing the oil at the prescribed intervals is the most effective way to prevent engine seizing and component wear.

WARNING: Engine oil may contain harmful chemical compounds that can cause skin irritation or skin cancer upon prolonged contact. Exhaust parts and engine oil are extremely hot immediately after operation and can cause severe thermal burns. Allow the engine to cool for at least fifteen minutes before draining the oil. Wear chemical-resistant gloves and protective eyewear when handling used engine oil.

To change the engine oil, execute the following procedure:
1. Position the pressure washer on a flat, level, and solid horizontal surface. Ensure the workspace is well-ventilated and free of any ignition sources.
2. Start the engine in accordance with the starting instructions in "Starting the Engine (Cold and Warm Starts)" in Section 4 and allow it to run for three to five minutes. Warming the engine oil decreases its viscosity, allowing it to drain more rapidly and carry away a greater volume of suspended carbon and metallic contaminants.
3. Shut off the engine, turn off the water supply, and depressurize the system completely.
4. Disconnect the spark plug wire and tuck it securely away from the spark plug terminal.
5. Clean the area surrounding the yellow engine oil fill cap/dipstick and the lower oil drain bolt located at the base of the engine cylinder block to prevent dirt or debris from entering the crankcase.
6. Position a suitable oil drain pan (minimum 1.0-quart capacity) directly beneath the oil drain bolt.
7. Using a 10mm wrench or socket, turn the oil drain bolt counterclockwise to loosen it. Slowly remove the bolt and the copper sealing washer.
8. Tilt the pressure washer slightly backward on its 10-inch wheels to guide the draining oil into the pan. Allow the engine oil to drain completely until the flow transitions to a slow drip.
9. Wipe the oil drain bolt, the copper sealing washer, and the engine drain port threads clean with a lint-free cloth. Inspect the copper sealing washer for compression cracks or flattening; replace the washer if it is damaged.
10. Thread the oil drain bolt back into the engine block. Turn it clockwise by hand to avoid cross-threading, then tighten it securely using a wrench to 10 to 12 foot-pounds (13 to 16 Newton-meters) of torque.
11. Remove the yellow engine oil fill cap/dipstick by turning it counterclockwise. Wipe the dipstick blade dry with a clean cloth.
12. Insert the plastic angled funnel provided in the accessory kit into the oil fill neck.
13. Slowly pour 20 fluid ounces (0.6 liters) of clean, fresh SAE 10W-30 engine oil into the crankcase. Refer to "Engine Specifications" in Section 2 for approved oil specifications.
14. Remove the funnel. Insert the dipstick straight back into the fill neck. Push it completely in and thread it clockwise until it is fully tightened.
15. Unscrew the dipstick and pull it straight out to read the oil level. Verify that the oil level rests exactly at the "FULL" mark or the top of the crosshatched region on the dipstick blade. If the level is below the "LOW" mark, add oil in 1-ounce increments and re-check.
16. Reinstall and tighten the dipstick. Clean up all oil drips or spills on the engine block, frame, and floor. Dispose of the used engine oil at an authorized municipal recycling facility in accordance with local regulations.

#### Air Filter Cleaning and Replacement
The air filtration system prevents airborne dust, abrasive dirt, and pollen from entering the carburetor and the engine cylinder, where they would cause rapid piston ring and cylinder bore wear. Operating the engine with a clogged, dirty, or torn air filter restricts combustion airflow, resulting in a rich air-fuel mixture, carbon-fouled spark plugs, exhaust smoke, high fuel consumption, and reduced engine power.

CAUTION: Never run the engine without the air filter element installed. Raw, unfiltered air will draw dirt and abrasive particles into the carburetor and combustion chamber, causing immediate, irreversible engine wear and voiding the manufacturer warranty.

To service the dual-element air filter, follow these steps:
1. Clean the exterior of the air filter housing cover, located on the side of the engine above the carburetor, to prevent loose dirt from falling into the intake when the cover is removed.
2. Depress the upper and lower locking tabs on the air filter cover, or unscrew the cover fastener depending on configuration, and pull the cover off the housing base.
3. Carefully remove the air filter assembly from the housing. The assembly consists of an outer foam pre-cleaner sleeve wrapped around a pleated paper core.
4. Separate the foam pre-cleaner sleeve from the paper filter core.
5. Inspect both filter elements. If the paper core is excessively dirty, clogged with dust, or torn, it must be replaced with an original equipment manufacturer paper filter element. Do not attempt to wash or blow dirt out of the paper core, as this can tear the delicate paper fibers.
6. Wash the foam pre-cleaner sleeve in warm water mixed with a mild household liquid soap. Squeeze the foam gently to dissolve dirt and oil. Do not twist, wring, or pull the foam, as this will tear the material.
7. Rinse the foam sleeve thoroughly in clean water to remove all soap residue, then squeeze it dry in a clean, lint-free towel. Allow the foam to air-dry completely.
8. Once the foam sleeve is dry, apply 5 to 10 drops of clean engine oil (SAE 10W-30) to the foam. Squeeze the foam repeatedly to distribute the oil evenly. Squeeze the foam in a clean paper towel to remove all excess oil; the foam should be slightly damp with oil, not dripping or saturated.
9. Slide the cleaned foam pre-cleaner sleeve back over the paper filter core.
10. Wipe any dust or grease out of the plastic air filter housing base and cover using a damp cloth. Do not allow dirt to enter the air intake throat leading to the carburetor.
11. Reinstall the air filter assembly into the housing base, ensuring it is seated flat and aligned with the registration tabs.
12. Snap the air filter cover back onto the housing until the locking tabs click firmly into place. Verify that the cover is completely sealed around its entire perimeter.

#### Spark Plug Service and Gap Calibration
The spark plug ignites the compressed air-fuel mixture within the combustion chamber. Over time, the spark plug electrodes wear down, and carbon or oil residues deposit on the insulator tip, leading to engine misfires, rough idling, starting difficulties, and loss of cleaning pressure.

To service and calibrate the F7RTC spark plug:
1. Disconnect the spark plug wire and boot from the spark plug terminal. Clean the spark plug base area to remove any dirt or grit.
2. Using the spark plug wrench provided in your tool kit, turn the spark plug counterclockwise to loosen and unscrew it from the engine cylinder head.
3. Inspect the spark plug. If the ceramic insulator is cracked, chipped, or damaged, or if the electrodes are severely worn or corroded, replace the spark plug with a new F7RTC (or equivalent) plug immediately.
4. Examine the color of the spark plug insulator tip. A healthy, properly firing engine will produce a light tan or grey color. A black, oily deposit indicates a rich fuel mixture, excessive idling, or overfilled engine oil. A stark white insulator indicates a lean mixture, high operating temperatures, or engine overheating.
5. Use a wire brush to clean any light carbon deposits from the electrodes. Do not use sandblasting or abrasive grinding tools to clean the spark plug, as loose grit can enter the cylinder and ruin the engine.
6. Measure the electrode gap using a wire feeler gauge or spark plug gap tool. Refer to "Engine Specifications" in Section 2 for the exact clearance limit.
7. The electrode gap must measure between 0.028 and 0.031 inches (0.7 to 0.8 millimeters). If adjustment is necessary, carefully bend the ground electrode (the outer bent metal piece) closer to or farther from the center electrode until the gap matches the specification. Never tap or press on the center electrode, as this can crack the ceramic insulator core.
8. Ensure the spark plug crush washer is in place on the spark plug threads. Wipe the cylinder head threads clean with a rag.
9. Thread the spark plug into the cylinder head by hand. Turn it clockwise until it is finger-tight to prevent cross-threading.
10. Using the spark plug wrench, tighten the plug to its final torque. If installing a new spark plug, tighten it 1/2 turn past finger-tight to crush the sealing washer. If reinstalling a used spark plug, tighten it 1/8 to 1/4 turn past finger-tight to secure it.
11. If utilizing a torque wrench, tighten the spark plug to 18 to 22 foot-pounds (24 to 30 Newton-meters).
12. Reattach the spark plug wire boot firmly onto the spark plug terminal. You must feel it snap into place over the threaded stud.

#### Spark Arrestor Inspection and Cleaning
The exhaust muffler is equipped with a USDA Forest Service-approved spark arrestor screen. The spark arrestor prevents hot, burning carbon particles from being ejected from the engine exhaust, reducing the risk of starting a forest or brush fire. In many jurisdictions, operating an internal combustion utility engine without an effective, clean spark arrestor is a violation of environmental safety laws.

To clean the spark arrestor screen:
1. Ensure the engine and exhaust muffler have cooled completely (at least thirty minutes).
2. Locate the metal muffler guard shielding the exhaust outlet.
3. Using a socket wrench, remove the mounting screws securing the muffler guard and pull the guard off the muffler.
4. Locate the spark arrestor retention screw or clip at the exhaust discharge nozzle. Remove the screw.
5. Carefully slide the stainless steel spark arrestor screen out of the muffler assembly.
6. Inspect the spark arrestor screen for holes, physical damage, or tears. If the screen is damaged or burnt through, it must be replaced immediately.
7. Use a fine-wire brass brush to clean carbon deposits from the screen. Brush gently to avoid tearing or distorting the wire mesh. The screen must be completely free of carbon blockages to ensure proper exhaust flow.
8. Slide the clean spark arrestor screen back into the exhaust outlet nozzle. Align the screw holes.
9. Reinstall and tighten the retention screw.
10. Reattach the muffler guard and secure it with the mounting screws.

#### Engine Governor Limits and Calibration
WARNING: Do not alter, bypass, or tamper with the engine governor spring, linkages, or speed adjustment screws. The governor is factory-set to control the engine speed at a maximum of 3,600 RPM under load. Running the engine at speeds exceeding 3,600 RPM increases the risk of catastrophic pump rupture, engine rod failure, or flying metal shards, and voids the warranty. If the engine speed is unstable, contact an authorized service dealer.

### Pump and Hydraulic System Maintenance
The direct-drive axial cam pump is the high-pressure heart of your machine. While the pump oil chamber is sealed and maintenance-free, the water-handling components require regular care to prevent wear and pressure loss.

#### Water Inlet Screen Inspection
The stainless steel wire mesh screen located inside the female garden hose inlet coupling is the only barrier protecting the pump's precision internal valves and plungers from damage by sand, silt, pipe scale, or debris.
1. Turn off the water supply and disconnect the garden hose from the pump inlet.
2. Examine the screen inside the inlet coupling.
3. If the screen is dirty, carefully pull it out of the coupling using a pair of needle-nose pliers. Be careful not to tear the mesh.
4. Flush both sides of the screen with clean water to rinse away trapped debris. Inspect the screen for tears, holes, or corrosion. If the screen is damaged, replace it immediately with an original equipment manufacturer part.
5. Reinsert the clean inlet screen into the pump coupling, pushing it flat until it is fully seated against the internal stop.

#### High-Pressure Spray Nozzle Maintenance
Mineral scale, silt, and algae can build up inside the tiny precision orifices of the color-coded quick-connect nozzles, causing flow restriction, excessive pump backpressure, and erratic spray patterns.
1. Shut off the engine and turn off the water supply faucet.
2. Relieve all trapped water pressure in the hose and gun by squeezing the trigger.
3. Engage the red trigger safety lock on the spray gun handle.
4. Pull back the brass collar on the 21-inch wand's quick-connect coupler and remove the nozzle.
5. Locate the nozzle clean-out tool (a stiff, thin wire) provided in the accessory kit. Alternatively, a straightened metal paperclip can be used.
6. Insert the wire tool into the nozzle's central orifice and slide it back and forth to loosen any mineral deposits, sand, or debris.
7. Flush the nozzle with water by running clean water backward through the spray tip (from the front discharge side toward the rear hex shank side) to wash out the freed debris.
8. Inspect the nozzle orifice. If it is clear, reinstall the nozzle onto the wand and test the spray pattern.

WARNING: Never attempt to clean a nozzle while the water system is pressurized or while the engine is running. A nozzle launched under pressure represents a major projectile hazard. Always engage the spray gun safety lock.

#### Hose, Spray Gun, and Wand O-Ring Lubrication
The high-pressure connections on the 25-foot hose, the ergonomic spray gun handle, and the metal wand rely on elastomeric O-rings to form watertight seals under 3,200 PSI of pressure. Friction from threading and unthreading connections can wear, dry, or tear these O-rings, leading to high-pressure leaks.
1. Inspect all hose connections and O-rings before each use.
2. Apply a thin coating of high-quality, water-resistant silicone grease directly to the O-rings and threaded fittings on a seasonal basis. This lubricates the seals, prevents dry rot, and makes threading connections significantly easier.
3. If an O-ring is worn, flattened, or leaking, remove it carefully using a small non-marring plastic pick and install a replacement O-ring of the identical size and durometer rating.

### Onboard Detergent Tank and Chemical Siphon Maintenance
Soap residues, chemicals, and dried detergents can crystallize inside the clear plastic siphon tube, the bottom outlet of the onboard tank, and the internal check-ball mechanism of the pump's brass venturi injector. Clogged ports prevent the system from drawing soap when using the Black Soap Nozzle.
1. After each use of the chemical siphon system, execute the flushing procedure detailed in "Operating the Onboard Detergent Tank and Chemical Siphon" in Section 4.
2. Periodically, inspect the clear plastic chemical siphon tube for kinks, splits, or hardening. Replace the tube if it is cracked or leaking air.
3. Seasonally, empty the 0.8-gallon onboard detergent tank completely. Pour a mixture of warm water and household vinegar (1:1 ratio) into the tank and run the system with the black soap nozzle for five minutes. This dissolves stubborn mineral scale and soap buildup.
4. Rinse the tank thoroughly with clean water afterward.

### Short-Term Storage (1 to 4 Weeks)
If you do not plan to operate your pressure washer for more than seven days but less than thirty days, observe the following short-term storage precautions:
1. Perform the full shutdown and system depressurization procedure detailed in "Shutting Down the Pressure Washer and System Depressurization" in Section 4.
2. Ensure the engine fuel shut-off valve is in the OFF position to prevent gasoline from leaking past the carburetor float valve.
3. Wipe down the entire pressure washer with a damp cloth to remove dirt, grease, and cleaning residues. Inspect the unit for loose bolts or damaged parts.
4. Store the pressure washer in a dry, covered, and secure location out of reach of children. Do not store the unit near open flames, boilers, water heaters, or other appliances equipped with pilot lights or spark-emitting electric motors.
5. Cover the unit with a breathable protective tarp to shield it from dust and moisture. Do not use a non-breathable plastic sheet, as this traps condensation and promotes corrosion.

### Long-Term Off-Season Storage (Over 4 Weeks)
When storing the pressure washer for winter or for any extended period exceeding thirty days, you must take comprehensive preservation measures. Untreated gasoline degrades rapidly, forming sticky varnishes, gums, and acid compounds that will clog the carburetor jets and ruin the fuel system. Water left inside the pump will cause internal oxidation, galvanic corrosion between stainless steel and aluminum, and catastrophic freeze damage if temperatures drop.

WARNING: Gasoline is highly flammable and explosive. Do not drain the fuel tank or carburetor near open flames, sparks, pilot lights, heaters, or in enclosed unventilated spaces. Emptying fuel must be performed outdoors away from all ignition sources. Wear chemical-resistant gloves, safety goggles, and handle fuel in approved containers. Observe all instructions in "Fuel Handling Safety and Fire Prevention" in Section 1.

#### Fuel System Stabilization and Draining
To protect the engine fuel system during long-term storage, choose one of the following two stabilization methods:

*Method A: Fuel Stabilizer Treatment (Recommended)*
1. Fill the fuel tank to approximately 90 percent capacity with fresh, clean unleaded gasoline.
2. Add a high-quality commercial fuel stabilizer compound to the tank. Follow the mixing ratio specified on the stabilizer bottle.
3. Start the engine and run it outdoors for ten minutes. This allows the stabilized fuel mixture to completely displace the untreated fuel inside the fuel lines, fuel shut-off valve, carburetor bowl, and jets.
4. Shut down the engine, turn off the water supply, and depressurize the system. Store the unit with the fuel tank full of stabilized fuel. This minimizes air space in the tank, reducing condensation and fuel oxidation.

*Method B: Draining the Fuel System completely*
1. Position the unit outdoors and place an approved gasoline container beneath the engine carburetor.
2. Close the fuel shut-off valve.
3. Locate the carburetor float bowl drain bolt, situated on the lower angled side of the carburetor float bowl.
4. Using a wrench, remove the carburetor drain bolt. Be careful not to lose the sealing washer.
5. Open the fuel shut-off valve. Gasoline will begin draining from the fuel tank through the carburetor and into the container. Allow the tank to drain completely empty.
6. Once the fuel tank is empty, allow any remaining fuel inside the carburetor float bowl to drain out.
7. Reinstall the carburetor drain bolt and its sealing washer, tightening securely.
8. Pull the recoil starter handle three to four times to clear any remaining residual fuel out of the engine combustion chamber.

#### Engine Cylinder and Valve Preservation
To prevent the cylinder bore, piston rings, and valve faces from rusting or seizing during storage:
1. Remove the spark plug as detailed in the Spark Plug section.
2. Pour one tablespoon (approximately 0.5 fluid ounces or 15 milliliters) of clean engine oil (SAE 10W-30) directly into the spark plug cylinder hole.
3. Place a clean, dry rag over the spark plug hole to catch any oil spray.
4. Pull the recoil starter handle slowly three to five times. This rotates the crankshaft and distributes a thin, protective film of engine oil across the cylinder wall, piston crown, and valve seats.
5. Reinstall and tighten the F7RTC spark plug. Do not connect the spark plug wire.
6. Pull the recoil starter handle slowly until you feel increased mechanical resistance. This indicates the engine is on its compression stroke, which closes both the intake and exhaust valves. Closing the valves seals the combustion chamber and prevents moisture from entering.

#### Pump Winterization and Freeze Protection
Residual water trapped inside the chambers of the direct-drive axial cam pump is the single greatest hazard to the equipment during cold weather storage. If the temperature drops below 32 degrees Fahrenheit (0 degrees Celsius), the water will freeze, expand, and fracture the pump manifold. Even in warm climates, stagnant water inside the pump head causes corrosion, lime scale buildup, and can stick the internal valves.

CAUTION: Always protect the pump from freezing by treating it with Ridgeline Pump Saver fluid or an approved non-toxic RV antifreeze before storing. Failure to winterize the pump will result in catastrophic freeze damage that is not covered under the product warranty. Do not use automotive engine antifreeze, as it is highly toxic and will damage the internal rubber seals of the pump.

To winterize and protect the pump:
1. Ensure the pressure washer is completely shut down, the water supply is disconnected, and all hoses are removed from the pump.
2. Obtain a can of Ridgeline Pump Saver fluid (or equivalent pressurized pump lubricant/antifreeze).
3. Read and follow all instructions printed on the pump saver canister.
4. Thread the pump saver canister's brass hose adapter directly onto the pump's 3/4-inch garden hose female inlet coupling. Tighten by hand until snug.
5. Push down on the canister nozzle to discharge the pressurized aerosol contents into the pump.
6. While holding the nozzle down to feed the fluid, use your other hand to slowly pull the recoil starter handle two to three times. Pulling the recoil starter rotates the pump pistons, drawing the protective lubricant and antifreeze through the internal check valves and galleries.
7. Continue spraying the fluid into the pump inlet until the fluid begins discharging from the M22 high-pressure pump outlet fitting. The fluid emerging from the outlet should be the same color as the fluid inside the canister (typically pink or green), indicating all residual water has been displaced.
8. Disconnect the pump saver canister from the pump inlet. Wipe up any overspray or drips on the frame and pump housing. The pump is now fully winterized and protected against rust, corrosion, drying seals, and freezing temperatures.

### Removing the Equipment from Storage
To safely recommission your pressure washer and prepare it for operation after an extended storage period:
1. Move the pressure washer outdoors to a well-ventilated, level area.
2. Clean any dust, cobwebs, or debris from the engine cooling fins, the recoil starter housing, the muffler, and the pump body.
3. Inspect all fuel lines, the carburetor, and the engine block for signs of deterioration, cracks, or leaks.
4. Remove the spark plug, verify the electrode gap (0.028 to 0.031 inches), clean or replace the plug, and reinstall it securely.
5. Check the engine oil level using the yellow dipstick. If the oil was not changed before storage, change it now.
6. Check the fuel tank. If the tank was stored empty, fill it with fresh, clean unleaded gasoline. If stabilized fuel was stored in the tank for over six months, drain the tank and refill it with fresh gasoline.
7. Connect the garden hose to the pump inlet. Before starting the engine, run water through the pump for one minute with the high-pressure hose disconnected. This flushes out any residual pump saver fluid and verifies that the pump rotates freely without sticking.
8. Connect the high-pressure hose, spray gun, and wand. Prime the system and purge all trapped air as detailed in "Priming the Pump and Purging Air" in Section 4. Perform a complete pre-operation check before starting the engine.

## 6. Troubleshooting

### Engine Subsystem Troubleshooting

Problem: Engine will not start.
Possible cause: Engine ignition switch is OFF; fuel shut-off valve is closed; choke lever is in RUN on a cold engine; engine oil level is low, triggering the low-oil shutdown sensor; spark plug wire is disconnected; spark plug is dirty or improperly gapped; stale fuel in the tank; hydrostatic pressure is built up in the pump.
Corrective action:
1. Turn the ignition switch to the ON (I) position.
2. Slide the fuel shut-off valve lever to the far right (ON) position.
3. Slide the choke lever to the far left (CHOKE) position for cold starts.
4. Verify the oil level is at the "FULL" mark on the yellow dipstick; add SAE 10W-30 engine oil as needed to disengage the low-oil shutdown sensor.
5. Press the spark plug wire boot firmly onto the F7RTC spark plug terminal.
6. Remove the spark plug, clean carbon deposits, check the gap (0.028 to 0.031 inches), and replace if cracked.
7. Drain old fuel and refill with fresh unleaded gasoline.
8. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger to relieve trapped water pressure before pulling the recoil starter handle.

Problem: Engine starts but runs rough, misfires, or idles erratically.
Possible cause: Choke lever left in CHOKE; dirty air filter element; fouled spark plug; water or dirt in the fuel system; carburetor jetting is restricted.
Corrective action:
1. Gradually move the choke lever to the RUN position as the engine warms.
2. Inspect and clean the air filter foam sleeve as outlined in Section 5; replace the paper core if clogged.
3. Clean and regap the F7RTC spark plug to 0.028–0.031 inches.
4. Drain the carburetor float bowl using the drain bowl to clear sediment, then refill the fuel tank with fresh gasoline.
5. Clean the fuel sediment cup under the fuel valve. For persistent issues, contact an authorized Ridgeline Outdoor Power dealer.
6. Inspect the black fuel tank cap vent holes. Clear any debris to prevent vacuum lock in the fuel tank.

Problem: Engine runs but stalls or shuts down unexpectedly during operation.
Possible cause: Engine is low on oil, triggering the low-oil shutdown sensor; empty fuel tank; clogged fuel line; dirty cooling fins.
Corrective action:
1. Position the pressure washer on level ground and verify the oil level is at the "FULL" mark on the yellow dipstick; add oil if low.
2. Check the fuel level in the tank; refuel outdoors with fresh gasoline if empty.
3. Inspect fuel lines for kinks. If blocked, contact an authorized service dealer.
4. Shut off the engine, allow it to cool, and clean dirt from the cylinder cooling fins and recoil starter cover.

Problem: Engine lacks power or bogs down when the spray gun trigger is squeezed.
Possible cause: Clogged spark arrestor screen; spark plug gap is too wide; carburetor jetting is restricted; clogged high-pressure spray nozzle.
Corrective action:
1. Verify that the engine is running at its full governed speed of 3,600 RPM.
2. Remove the muffler guard, slide out the spark arrestor screen, and clean carbon blockages with a brass wire brush.
3. Inspect and reset the spark plug gap to 0.028–0.031 inches.
4. Clean the quick-connect nozzle orifice using the wire clean-out tool to eliminate hydraulic restriction.

Problem: Engine produces excessive black smoke from the exhaust muffler.
Possible cause: Choke lever is partially closed; air filter element is saturated with dirt or oil; carburetor float is stuck or leaking.
Corrective action:
1. Push the choke lever fully to the far right (RUN) position.
2. Clean the air filter foam sleeve and replace the paper core if restricted.
3. Tap the carburetor float bowl gently with a soft mallet to free a stuck float; have the carburetor serviced if it continues.

Problem: Recoil starter rope is extremely hard to pull or snaps back violently.
Possible cause: High hydrostatic water pressure is trapped in the pump head and high-pressure hose; engine cylinder is flooded with fuel.
Corrective action:
1. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger to release trapped pressure before pulling the recoil starter handle.
2. Remove the F7RTC spark plug and pull the recoil starter rope several times to expel excess fuel, then reinstall the plug and start again.

### Pump and Hydraulic Subsystem Troubleshooting

Problem: Pump does not build pressure or produces very low discharge pressure.
Possible cause: Incorrect nozzle installed; municipal water supply flow rate is low; inlet water screen is clogged; garden hose is kinked; trapped air in pump.
Corrective action:
1. Install a high-pressure nozzle (0, 15, 25, or 40-degree); do not use the black soap nozzle.
2. Verify the water supply provides at least 5.0 GPM at 20 to 80 PSI.
3. Disconnect the garden hose and clean the stainless steel wire mesh inlet screen inside the pump coupling.
4. Straighten the garden hose to remove all kinks or flat spots.
5. Squeeze the spray gun trigger to purge trapped air until a steady stream of low-pressure water flows.

Problem: Pump pressure fluctuates, pulses, or is inconsistent.
Possible cause: Municipal water supply is restricted; inlet water screen is blocked; spray nozzle is partially clogged; air leak at pump inlet connection.
Corrective action:
1. Confirm the garden hose has a minimum internal diameter of 5/8 inch and is no longer than 50 feet.
2. Clean the inlet screen and clear any obstruction from the nozzle orifice using the wire clean-out tool.
3. Tighten the garden hose connection at the pump inlet to ensure an airtight seal.

Problem: Thermal relief valve is continuously discharging hot water.
Possible cause: Engine has been idling without spraying for over two minutes; relief valve is dirty or stuck open; relief valve is defective.
Corrective action:
1. Squeeze the spray gun trigger immediately to discharge heated water and draw cool water into the pump head. Do not idle the engine over two minutes.
2. Shut off the engine, turn off the water, allow the unit to cool, and flush the pump with clean water.
3. If the valve leaks cold water when the machine is first started, replace the valve.

Problem: Water leaks from the high-pressure hose connection at the pump or gun.
Possible cause: Threaded M22 collar is loose; internal high-pressure rubber O-ring is worn, damaged, or missing; connection threads are stripped.
Corrective action:
1. Shut down the engine, relieve pressure, and hand-tighten the M22 collar clockwise.
2. Inspect and replace the rubber O-ring at the tip of the connector if worn, applying water-resistant silicone grease to the seal.
3. Replace the 25-foot high-pressure hose if the metal threads are stripped or cross-threaded.

Problem: Water leaks from the garden hose connection at the pump inlet.
Possible cause: Garden hose coupling is loose or cross-threaded; inlet hose washer is worn or missing; inlet coupling is cracked.
Corrective action:
1. Unthread the garden hose, align the threads carefully, and hand-tighten the connection collar clockwise until snug.
2. Inspect the female inlet fitting. Ensure the rubber hose washer is present and seated flat behind the wire mesh screen; replace if cracked.
3. Replace the pump inlet fitting if cracked.

Problem: Spray nozzle is ejected from the wand or leaks water around the coupler.
Possible cause: Brass quick-connect collar was not fully locked; nozzle hex shank is worn; coupler ball bearings are dirty.
Corrective action:
1. Pull back the quick-connect collar, push the nozzle firmly until it bottoms out, and release the collar. Verify that the collar snaps completely forward. Pull the nozzle to check the lock.
2. Replace the color-coded nozzle if the hex shank is worn.
3. Clean and lubricate the coupler sleeve with silicone grease.

### Detergent and Accessory Subsystem Troubleshooting

Problem: Detergent is not siphoning or mixing with the water stream.
Possible cause: High-pressure nozzle is installed; clear plastic chemical siphon tube is disconnected; siphon tube is kinked; siphon inlet filter screen is clogged; detergent is too thick; check-valve is stuck.
Corrective action:
1. Install the color-coded Black Soap Nozzle. The chemical siphon system will not draw detergent under high pressure.
2. Connect the clear plastic chemical siphon tube firmly to the brass chemical injection barb on the pump outlet.
3. Clear any kinks or pinches along the siphon tube.
4. Clean the siphon screen at the bottom of the onboard detergent tank.
5. Dilute the detergent with water.
6. Press the internal ball-check valve inside the brass barb with a toothpick to free a stuck check-ball, then flush with warm water.

Problem: Clear plastic chemical siphon tube is leaking detergent or drawing air.
Possible cause: Siphon tube is split, cracked, or hardened due to chemical exposure; tube is loose on the brass barb fitting.
Corrective action:
1. Replace the clear plastic chemical siphon tube with a new chemical-resistant silicone tube of identical dimensions.
2. Push the tube completely over the brass chemical injection barb and secure it with a small zip-tie to ensure an airtight seal.

Problem: High-pressure hose is stiff, kinked, or shows localized blistering.
Possible cause: Hose was stored with water under freezing conditions; hose was dragged over sharp corners; hose touched the hot engine muffler.
Corrective action:
1. Discard the damaged hose immediately. Never attempt to repair high-pressure hoses. Replace with an original Ridgeline Outdoor Power 3,200 PSI-rated replacement hose.
2. Always uncoil the 25-foot high-pressure hose before starting the engine and keep it clear of the hot exhaust muffler during operation.

Problem: Water drips steadily from the weep hole on the underside of the pump whenever the spray gun trigger is released, although spraying pressure is normal.
Possible cause: The pump's low-pressure seals (seal kit RP-LS-32) are worn; the weep hole is designed to vent water that passes a worn seal so it does not reach the crankcase.
Corrective action: Stop the engine, relieve pressure by squeezing the trigger, remove the four pump manifold bolts, replace the low-pressure seals with kit RP-LS-32, apply the supplied silicone grease to the plungers, reassemble, and confirm the weep hole stays dry.
