## 1. Important Safety Instructions

### California Proposition 65 and Emissions Compliance
WARNING: This product contains or emits chemicals known to the State of California to cause cancer and birth defects or other reproductive harm. Engine exhaust, some of its constituents, and certain vehicle components contain or emit these chemicals. Avoid inhaling exhaust, do not idle the engine except as necessary, service your equipment in a well-ventilated area, and wear gloves or wash your hands frequently when servicing your equipment. For more information regarding state-mandated health advisories and environmental regulations, consult local authorities.

The engine installed on this Ridgeline Outdoor Power equipment is certified by the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and, where applicable, the California Air Resources Board (CARB) to meet strict emission standards for spark-ignited utility engines. Maintenance, replacement, or repair of the emission control devices and systems may be performed by any nonroad engine repair establishment or individual. However, warranty repairs must be performed by an authorized Ridgeline Outdoor Power service dealer. The emission control system consists of the carburetor, the ignition system, and the fuel tank, lines, cap, and carbon canister (where applicable). Do not alter or modify the engine in any way that would increase emissions or bypass these safety controls.

### Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Hazards
WARNING: Carbon monoxide is an odorless, colorless, and extremely toxic gas produced by internal combustion engines. Inhaling carbon monoxide can cause brain damage, severe illness, or death within minutes of exposure. Never operate the engine indoors or in enclosed spaces, even if doors and windows are open. Operating this equipment in garages, sheds, basements, or any other confined structure is strictly prohibited. Only operate the equipment outdoors, far away from open windows, doors, ventilation intakes, or other openings that could allow carbon monoxide gas to drift inside occupied structures.

Symptoms of carbon monoxide poisoning include headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness, sleepiness, confusion, and disorientation. If you or anyone else exhibits any of these symptoms while operating this pressure washer, immediately shut off the engine, move to fresh outdoor air, and seek emergency medical attention. Ensure that all persons operating this equipment are aware of the hazards of carbon monoxide and trained in proper outdoor positioning of the machine to prevent exhaust gas accumulation.

### Fuel Handling Safety and Fire Prevention
WARNING: Gasoline is highly flammable, volatile, and explosive. Liquid gasoline and its vapors can ignite from spark, static electricity, hot exhaust parts, or open flames, causing catastrophic fire or explosion, severe personal injury, or property damage.

Observe the following safety precautions during fuel handling and engine operation:
1. Store gasoline only in containers specifically designed and approved for fuel storage. Keep fuel containers out of reach of children in a cool, dry, well-ventilated location.
2. Always shut off the engine and allow it to cool completely for at least two minutes before removing the fuel cap and refueling.
3. Refuel the fuel tank outdoors in an open, well-ventilated area. Do not refuel indoors, in garages, or near ignition sources such as pilot lights.
4. Do not smoke or permit open flames or sparks near the fuel tank during refueling.
5. Avoid overfilling the fuel tank. Fill the tank only to approximately one inch below the bottom of the filler neck to allow space for fuel expansion. Securely tighten the fuel tank cap immediately after refueling.
6. Clean up any spilled fuel immediately using clean, dry rags. Move the pressure washer at least ten feet away from the fueling site before starting the engine.
7. Inspect the fuel system regularly for signs of leakage or deterioration. Replace any damaged components before operating.
8. Never use gasoline containing more than ten percent ethanol (E10), as this can damage the engine fuel system and void the product warranty. Do not use methanol or diesel fuel.
9. Avoid prolonged skin contact with gasoline or inhalation of fuel vapors. If gasoline is spilled on clothing, change clothes immediately.
10. Ensure the fuel shut-off valve is closed whenever the engine is not running and during transport.

### General Operation and Safety Rules for Pressure Washers
WARNING: This high-pressure gas pressure washer is capable of causing severe fluid injection injuries, deep lacerations, and property damage. Always exercise extreme caution during operation, maintenance, and setup.

Training and Preparation:
1. Read, understand, and follow all instructions on the machine and in this manual before attempting to assemble, operate, or service the pressure washer. Keep this manual in a safe place for future reference.
2. Familiarize yourself thoroughly with all controls and their proper operation. Know how to stop the engine and disengage the pressure quickly.
3. Never allow children under the age of sixteen to operate this pressure washer. Only responsible, trained adults should be permitted to operate the machine.
4. Wear appropriate personal protective equipment, including heavy boots with slip-resistant soles, during operation. Avoid loose-fitting clothing that can become entangled in rotating parts.
5. Always wear safety glasses or eye shields during operation and while performing adjustments or repairs to protect against back-spray and thrown debris.
6. Thoroughly inspect the area where the pressure washer is to be used. Remove all welcome mats, toys, loose boards, wires, and other foreign objects that could be propelled or damaged.
7. Adjust the pressure washer setup to clear gravel or crushed rock surfaces to prevent gravel from being picked up and projected by the high-pressure stream.
8. Wear adequate hearing protection to reduce the risk of noise-induced hearing loss from the 208cc OHV engine.

Safe Operation Practices:
1. Never put hands, feet, or body parts near the pump or high-pressure spray nozzle. Contact with the high-pressure water stream can cause severe subcutaneous fluid injection.
2. The spray gun trigger safety lock, located on the handle, must be engaged when starting the engine and whenever the operator leaves the operating position.
3. The high-pressure hose must be fully uncoiled and free of kinks when starting the engine and when the machine is not actively spraying.
4. Always operate the pressure washer from the operator's position directly behind the handles. Maintain a firm grip on the handles.
5. Exercise extreme caution when operating on or crossing gravel drives, walks, or roads. Stay alert for hidden hazards.
6. If the pressure washer strikes a foreign object, stop the engine, disconnect the spark plug wire, and inspect for damage. Repair any damage before restarting.
7. If the pressure washer starts to vibrate abnormally, stop the engine, disconnect the spark plug wire, and inspect immediately for the cause. Vibration is generally a warning of damage.
8. Stop the engine and disconnect the spark plug wire whenever you leave the operating position, before unclogging the nozzles, and before making repairs.
9. Walk slowly and carefully on slopes. Do not operate on steep slopes. Exercise caution when changing direction on slopes to prevent tipping.
10. Do not overload the machine capacity by spraying at too fast a rate. Adjust the nozzle spray pattern and distance to match cleaning conditions.
11. Never operate at high pressure on slippery surfaces. Use the quick-connect nozzles with caution and look behind to avoid falling.
12. Disengage power to the axial cam pump when transporting or not actively cleaning.
13. Never operate without good visibility. Ensure the work area is clear.
14. Ensure the spray gun trigger safety lock is engaged to prevent accidental discharge in cold conditions. Do not operate if you cannot feel temperature changes.
15. Never attempt to make any adjustments while the engine is running, except where specifically recommended.

### High-Pressure Hose and Spray Gun Safety
WARNING: The high-pressure hose and spray gun are critical safety components. Misdirecting the high-pressure spray can cause severe injury from fluid injection.

1. Never direct the high-pressure spray toward people, animals, vehicles, buildings, windows, or property that could be damaged.
2. Always adjust the spray nozzle selection and verify the spray gun safety lock is engaged from the operator's position before starting the engine.
3. Never attempt to adjust the spray nozzle or quick-connect couplings by reaching directly in front of the nozzle. Always use the spray gun safety lock.
4. If the high-pressure hose becomes clogged or damaged, disengage the pump, stop the engine, remove the key, and disconnect the spark plug wire.
5. Always use a clean-out tool or wire brush to clear a clogged nozzle. Never use your hands or feet to clear a clogged nozzle or hose.
6. Keep bystanders, children, and pets at least seventy-five feet away from the pressure washer and high-pressure hose during operation.

### Spray Nozzle and Thermal Relief Valve Safety
CAUTION: Spray nozzles and the pump thermal relief valve are critical safety components. Improper handling of nozzles or the thermal relief valve can lead to injury or pump damage.

1. Ensure quick-connect nozzles are securely locked to the end of the spray wand before operating. Check nozzle connections regularly.
2. Proceed slowly, letting the high-pressure stream cut into the dirt. Do not push the spray wand aggressively close to fragile surfaces.
3. Keep hands, face, and clothing away from the thermal relief valve, as it has a discharge port designed to vent extremely hot water.
4. Maintain the thermal relief valve in its proper operating condition to prevent accidental hot water contact.

### Children and Bystander Safety

4. Use extreme care when approaching blind corners, shrubs, trees, or other objects that may block your view.

### Maintenance, Service, and Storage Safety
WARNING: Improper maintenance or unauthorized modifications to the pressure washer can result in serious injury, fire, carbon monoxide poisoning, or equipment failure.

1. Before performing maintenance, cleaning, or repairs, stop the engine, remove the safety ignition key, and disconnect the spark plug wire.

### Pneumatic Wheel Installation
To facilitate mobility, you must install the two pneumatic 13-inch wheels to the axle brackets located at the lower rear of the main frame.
1. Locate the two wheels, steel axle pins, flat washers, and cotter clips from the hardware package.
2. Ensure the engine fuel valve is in the closed position and the spark plug wire is disconnected.
3. Tilt the main frame forward to rest safely on its front feet and the lower frame bumper.
CAUTION: Ensure the machine is stabilized. Do not allow the unit to tip over, which can damage the shroud or carburetor.
4. Align the wheel axle bore with the mounting sleeve on the lower left frame bracket. The bearing shoulder must face inward toward the frame.
5. Insert an axle pin from the outside of the wheel through the frame axle sleeve.
6. Slide a flat washer over the inner tip of the axle pin.
7. Insert a cotter clip through the axle pin hole until it snaps.
8. Repeat steps 4 through 7 on the right frame bracket.
9. Lower the pressure washer back down until both wheels and front rubber feet rest firmly on the floor. Verify that both wheels rotate freely.

### Handlebar Assembly and Nozzle Storage Panel
The two-piece frame design requires the secure installation of the upper handlebar assembly onto the lower frame tubes to ensure structural rigidity and isolate vibration.
1. Locate the upper handlebar assembly.
2. Slide the open ends of the upper handlebar tubes down over the lower frame tubes. Ensure that the curved operator handle sweeps backward, away from the engine shroud.
3. Push the handlebar downward until the mounting holes are aligned.
4. Locate the two carriage bolts (M8 size) and two plastic star knobs.
5. Insert the carriage bolts from the inside of the frame tubes, pushing them outward. The square shoulder must seat completely into the square portion of the inner mounting hole.
6. Thread the plastic star knobs onto the protruding ends of the carriage bolts.
7. Hand-tighten both star knobs clockwise until the upper handlebar is secured.
WARNING: Do not operate the pressure washer with loose knobs. Excessive vibration can cause loose knobs to back off, leading to a structural failure of the handle.
8. Locate the five color-coded quick-connect spray nozzles from the accessory tray.
9. Push each nozzle into its designated rubber storage grommet on the handlebar panel. Organize the nozzles in order of spray angle from left to right: Red (0 degree), Yellow (15 degree), Green (25 degree), White (40 degree), and Black (Soap).

### Connecting the Spray Gun, Spray Wand, and High-Pressure Hose
To ensure leak-free operation under 4,000 PSI, all high-pressure connections must be clean, unmarred, and tightened to their exact mechanical stops. Refer to section 2. Specifications for connection details.
1. Remove protective shipping caps from the inlet and outlet fittings of the pump, spray gun, and the ends of the 50-foot high-pressure hose. Inspect all threads and internal rubber O-rings.
WARNING: Never operate with damaged, worn, or missing O-rings. High-pressure water can blow past seals, resulting in fluid injection injuries.
2. Thread the male end of the 21-inch spray wand into the female outlet port (M22 fitting) of the spray gun. Turn the collar clockwise by hand until fully engaged.
3. Hand-tighten the wand collar firmly. Do not use tools, as excessive torque can crack components or strip threads.
4. Locate the 50-foot steel-braided high-pressure hose. Uncoil it completely to remove any twists or kinks.
5. Align one end of the hose with the inlet fitting at the bottom of the spray gun.
6. Push the hose fitting into the gun inlet and thread the collar clockwise by hand until tight.
7. Verify that there is no play or loose movement.
8. Align the other end of the hose with the high-pressure outlet fitting on the triplex plunger pump.
9. Push the hose fitting onto the pump outlet and hand-tighten the collar until snug. Ensure the hose does not touch the hot engine muffler.
10. Store the spray gun and wand in the integrated holster. Loop the hose over the storage hook.

### Onboard Detergent Tank Hose Connection
The RG-4000P features an onboard 1.0-gallon detergent tank. To enable chemical draw when utilizing the black soap nozzle, the siphon tube must be properly connected.
1. Locate the clear plastic chemical siphon tube pre-attached to the bottom outlet of the onboard detergent tank.
2. Route the clear plastic tube under the engine platform, ensuring it is clear of any hot engine components or the rotating recoil starter cover.
3. Locate the brass chemical injection barb on the outlet side of the pump, adjacent to the M22 high-pressure hose outlet fitting.
4. Push the open end of the clear plastic siphon tube firmly over the brass chemical injection barb. Ensure the tube is pushed completely past the raised ridges of the barb so that it forms an airtight seal.
CAUTION: Do not use any tools to crimp or tighten the siphon tube onto the barb. If the tube is loose, a small zip-tie may be placed over the connection to prevent air leaks, which would stall chemical drawing.
5. Verify that the siphon tube is not kinked, flattened, or pinched beneath the engine mounting plate. An air leak or a kink in this tube will prevent the pump from drawing detergent.

### Engine Oil Pre-Start Preparation
WARNING: This engine is shipped without oil. Operating the 306cc OHV engine without engine oil will cause immediate mechanical failure and void the warranty. Fill the crankcase with SAE 10W-30 oil before attempting to start.
1. Position the pressure washer on a flat, level surface.
2. Clean the area surrounding the yellow oil fill cap/dipstick at the lower rear of the engine block.
3. Turn the yellow oil fill cap/dipstick counterclockwise and pull it straight out.
4. Wipe the metal dipstick blade dry.
5. Insert the provided angled oil funnel into the oil fill neck.
6. Slowly pour the entire contents of the 37-fluid ounce (1.1-liter) bottle of SAE 10W-30 engine oil into the oil funnel.
7. Remove the funnel and insert the dipstick back into the fill neck. Thread it clockwise until fully tightened.
8. Unscrew the yellow oil fill cap/dipstick and pull it out to check the level.
9. Verify that the oil level is at the "FULL" mark (or the upper limit of the crosshatched pattern) on the dipstick. The level must never be below the "LOW" mark.
CAUTION: Overfilling the crankcase can cause smoke, plug fouling, or oil to enter the air filter. Underfilling will trigger the low-oil shutdown sensor.
10. Re-insert the dipstick and hand-tighten the cap clockwise. Wipe up any spills before proceeding.

### Fueling the Engine Fuel Tank
WARNING: Gasoline is highly volatile and explosive. Observe all safety guidelines outlined in "Fuel Handling Safety and Fire Prevention" in Section 1. Refuel outdoors, with the engine cooled for at least two minutes.
1. Position the pressure washer on a level, stable, outdoor surface, away from any ignition sources.
2. Clean the area around the black fuel tank cap.
3. Turn the cap counterclockwise and remove it from the tank filler neck.
4. Inspect the internal fuel strainer cup. Ensure it is clean and correctly seated. Never pour fuel directly into the tank if the strainer is missing.
5. Using an approved clean fuel container, slowly pour fresh, unleaded gasoline (minimum 87 octane, maximum 10 percent ethanol) into the fuel tank.
6. Stop filling when the level reaches approximately one inch below the bottom of the filler neck to allow space for fuel expansion.
WARNING: Do not overfill. Gasoline expands as the engine heats up. Overfilling can cause fuel to spill onto hot engine components, causing a fire or explosion.
7. Re-install the fuel tank cap and turn it clockwise until you hear distinct clicks, indicating it is locked.
8. Wipe away any spilled fuel immediately. Move the pressure washer at least ten feet away from the fueling location before starting.

### Connection to Water Source
The pump is designed to operate under continuous water feed. Running the pump dry, even for a few seconds, will cause extreme friction heat, ruin the high-pressure seals, and score the plungers, voiding the pump warranty.
1. Verify that the garden hose water supply is capable of delivering at least 7.0 GPM at a steady pressure of 20 to 80 PSI. The pump is not self-priming.
2. Inspect the female garden hose inlet coupling on the pump. Ensure the internal wire mesh inlet screen is clean, intact, and seated flat inside the coupling.
WARNING: Never operate without the wire mesh inlet screen in place. Debris entering the pump will damage check valves and cause pump failure.
3. Run water through your garden hose for 30 seconds before connecting it to clear any trapped air, dirt, or debris.
4. Thread the male end of the garden hose into the female garden hose inlet coupling on the pump. Tighten the collar clockwise by hand until snug.
5. Turn the water supply valve completely on. Check the inlet connection for leaks.
6. Purge all trapped air from the water system before starting the engine. Disengage the spray gun safety lock.
7. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger. Continue holding the trigger until a steady, unbroken stream of water flows from the wand.
8. Release the trigger and engage the safety lock. The pressure washer is now fully assembled and primed for operation.

Section 10: Shutting Down the Pressure Washer and System Depressurization (Budget: 200 words)
"When you have finished your cleaning task, or when you need to perform maintenance or store the machine, you must follow a strict shutdown and depressurization procedure. Simply turning off the engine does not remove the high-pressure water trapped within the 50-foot hose and the pump head. This trapped hydrostatic pressure makes it impossible to disconnect the hoses and represents a major safety hazard if the trigger is pulled accidentally." (66 words)

"To shut down and completely depressurize the system:
1. Release the trigger and engage the red trigger safety lock.
2. Allow the engine to run at idle for 15 to 30 seconds to allow the combustion chamber and exhaust system to cool gradually.
3. Locate the red engine ignition switch on the engine block and flip it to the OFF position. The engine will stop rotating.
4. Locate the fuel shut-off valve lever and slide it to the far left (OFF) position. This cuts off fuel flow to the carburetor and prevents fuel pooling during transport or storage.
5. Walk to your municipal water faucet and turn the water supply valve completely off.
6. Return to the pressure washer operating position and grasp the spray gun handle.
7. Disengage the red trigger safety lock.
8. Point the spray wand in a safe direction and squeeze the trigger. Hold the trigger depressed until all trapped water and high-pressure air have been completely discharged.
9. Verify that the system is fully depressurized by letting go of the trigger, waiting 5 seconds, and squeezing it once more. No water should discharge.
10. Engage the red trigger safety lock.
11. Disconnect the garden hose from the pump inlet by turning the connection collar counterclockwise. Residual water will drain from the hose and pump inlet.
12. Disconnect the high-pressure hose from the M22 pump outlet and the spray gun handle inlet by unscrewing the threaded collars counterclockwise.
13. Drain all remaining water from the high-pressure hose, the spray gun, and the metal wand before coiling the hose and storing the components on their respective frame hooks." (249 words)

## 5. Maintenance and Storage

Regular, systematic maintenance is essential for the reliable operation, long-term durability, and structural safety of your Ridgeline Outdoor Power RG-4000P Gas Pressure Washer. The high vibration, elevated mechanical loads, chemical exposure, and constant water contact of operation place significant stress on both the 306cc OHV engine and the direct-drive triplex plunger pump. Adhering to the scheduled maintenance intervals and procedural instructions detailed in this section preserves cleaning power, prevents costly breakdowns, maintains emissions compliance, and protects operators from hazards.

WARNING: Servicing a running or hot pressure washer can cause severe injury, shock, fluid injection, or death. Before performing any maintenance, position the unit on a level surface, turn the engine switch OFF, slide the fuel valve to OFF, turn off the water faucet, and squeeze the spray gun trigger to relieve all hydrostatic pressure. Allow the engine, muffler, and pump to cool for thirty minutes. Disconnect and secure the spark plug wire to prevent accidental starting.

### Maintenance Schedule
Perform the following service items at specified intervals. Under dusty, high-load, or wet conditions, service the unit more frequently to maintain emissions certification, pump efficiency, and structural safety.

Before each use: Check engine and pump oil levels
Before each use: Inspect hose, spray gun, wand, and water inlet screen
Before each use: Clean engine cooling fins and recoil starter housing
Before each use: Inspect complete unit for fuel, oil, or water leaks
Before each use: Verify spark plug wire is secure
First 5 hours: Change engine oil
First 50 hours: Change pump oil
Every 25 hours: Clean engine air filter foam element
Every 50 hours: Change engine oil

### Engine Maintenance Procedures
The 306cc OHV utility engine requires precise lubricating, air filtration, and electrical service to maintain its EPA and CARB emissions compliance, preserve its power output under heavy pumping loads, and ensure reliable starting.

#### Engine Oil Inspection and Change
Engine oil degrades over time from heat and contamination, losing viscosity. Regular oil changes prevent engine seizing and premature wear.

WARNING: Used engine oil contains harmful compounds. Hot oil and engine parts can cause severe burns. Allow the engine to cool for fifteen minutes. Wear chemical-resistant gloves and protective eyewear.

To change the engine oil:
1. Position the pressure washer on a level surface.
2. Run the engine for three minutes to warm the oil, then shut it off, turn off the water, and depressurize the system.
3. Disconnect and secure the spark plug wire.
4. Clean around the yellow oil fill cap/dipstick and the drain bolt.
5. Place a drain pan beneath the drain bolt.
6. Remove the 10mm drain bolt and copper washer, and tilt the unit backward on its 13-inch wheels to drain the oil.
7. Reinstall the drain bolt and washer, tightening to 10 to 12 foot-pounds.
8. Remove the yellow engine oil fill cap/dipstick, insert a clean plastic funnel into the fill neck, and slowly pour 37 fluid ounces (1.1 liters) of SAE 10W-30 engine oil into the crankcase. Do not overfill.
9. Verify the oil level is at the FULL mark, replace the dipstick, and clean up spills.

#### Air Filter Cleaning and Replacement
The air filter prevents dust from entering the carburetor and cylinder. Operating with a clogged filter restricts airflow, causing a rich mixture, exhaust smoke, and power loss.

CAUTION: Never run the engine without the air filter installed. Unfiltered air draws abrasive dirt into the engine, causing immediate cylinder wear and voiding the warranty.

To service the dual-element air filter:
1. Clean the air filter cover exterior, then depress the tabs and remove the cover.
2. Remove the filter assembly and separate the foam sleeve from the paper core.
3. Replace the paper core if dirty or torn. Do not wash the paper core.
4. Wash the foam sleeve in warm, soapy water, squeeze gently, rinse, and squeeze dry in a clean towel.
5. Apply 5 to 10 drops of SAE 10W-30 engine oil to the dry foam, squeeze to distribute, and blot excess oil.
6. Slide the foam sleeve over the paper core, reinstall the assembly, and secure the cover.

#### Spark Plug Service and Gap Calibration
The spark plug ignites the air-fuel mixture. Electrode wear and carbon deposits lead to misfires, starting difficulties, and loss of cleaning pressure.

To service and calibrate the F7RTC spark plug:
1. Disconnect the plug wire boot and clean the spark plug base area.
2. Remove the spark plug counterclockwise using the provided wrench.
3. Inspect the plug. Replace if the insulator is cracked or electrodes are corroded. Clean carbon with a wire brush.
4. Measure the gap with a feeler gauge. It must measure between 0.028 and 0.031 inches (0.7 to 0.8 millimeters).
5. Adjust the gap by bending the ground electrode.
6. Thread the plug in by hand, then tighten with the wrench to 18 to 22 foot-pounds. Reconnect the wire boot.

#### Spark Arrestor Inspection and Cleaning
The muffler contains a USDA Forest Service-approved spark arrestor screen to prevent hot carbon particles from being ejected.

To clean the spark arrestor screen:
1. Allow the muffler to cool, then remove the muffler guard.
2. Remove the retention screw and slide the spark arrestor screen out of the exhaust nozzle.
3. Replace the screen if torn or damaged. Clean carbon with a brass wire brush.
4. Reinstall the screen, tighten the retention screw, and secure the muffler guard.

#### Engine Governor Limits and Calibration
WARNING: Do not alter or tamper with the engine governor. The governor is factory-set to control the engine speed at a maximum of 3,600 RPM under load. Exceeding 3,600 RPM increases the risk of catastrophic pump rupture, engine failure, or flying shards, and voids the warranty. If engine speed is unstable, contact an authorized service dealer.

### Pump and Hydraulic System Maintenance
The direct-drive triplex plunger pump is the high-pressure heart of your machine. Regular lubrication and water-handling component care are essential for maintaining water pressurization.

#### Pump Oil Inspection and Change
The triplex pump requires SAE 75W-90 non-detergent pump oil to lubricate internal crankcase components. Condensation and wear debris contaminate the oil, requiring periodic replacement.

To inspect the oil, position the unit on a level surface and check the rear sight glass. The oil level must align with the red center dot. If low, discolored, or milky, service immediately.

To change the pump oil:
1. Shut down the unit, depressurize the system, and disconnect the spark plug wire.
2. Clean around the top breather cap and the lower drain plug.
3. Place a container under the drain plug and remove the breather cap.
4. Remove the drain plug, drain pump oil completely, and reinstall the plug securely.
5. Slowly pour clean SAE 75W-90 non-detergent pump oil into the pump fill port. Monitor the sight glass constantly and stop pouring as soon as the oil level reaches the red indicator dot (approximately 15 fluid ounces or 0.44 liters).
6. Reinstall the breather cap and wipe up spills.

#### Water Inlet Screen Inspection
The wire mesh screen inside the female garden hose inlet protects the pump check valves and plungers from damage by abrasive debris.

To service the inlet screen:
1. Turn off the water, disconnect the garden hose, and inspect the screen inside the inlet coupling.
2. Carefully pull the screen out using needle-nose pliers.
3. Flush both sides with clean water. Replace if torn or corroded.
4. Reinsert the screen flat against the internal stop.

#### High-Pressure Spray Nozzle Maintenance
Mineral scale and debris can build up inside the tiny orifices of the color-coded nozzles, causing flow restriction, excessive backpressure, and erratic spray.

To clean the spray nozzles:
1. Shut off the engine, turn off the water, squeeze the trigger to relieve pressure, and engage the safety lock.
2. Remove the nozzle from the 21-inch wand.
3. Slide the nozzle clean-out tool through the central orifice to loosen deposits.
4. Flush the nozzle backward with clean water, then reinstall it.

WARNING: Never clean a nozzle while the water system is pressurized or while the engine is running to avoid projectile hazards. Always engage the gun safety lock.

#### Hose, Spray Gun, and Wand O-Ring Lubrication
High-pressure connections on the 50-foot hose, gun, and 21-inch wand rely on elastomeric O-rings to form watertight seals under 4,000 PSI. Friction from threading can wear or tear seals, causing leaks.

To service the O-rings:
1. Inspect all connections and O-rings before each use.
2. Apply water-resistant silicone grease to O-rings and threaded fittings seasonally to prevent dry rot and ease assembly.
3. Replace worn O-rings using a non-marring pick to install a replacement seal of identical size.

### Onboard Detergent Tank and Chemical Siphon Maintenance
Dried detergent can crystallize inside the siphon tube, tank outlet, and pump venturi injector, preventing chemical draw when using the black soap nozzle.

To service the chemical siphon system:
1. Flush the system by running clean water through the siphon tube after each use.
2. Inspect the tube for kinks or cracks; replace if leaking air.
3. Seasonally empty the 1.0-gallon tank, add a 1:1 warm water and vinegar mixture, run with the black nozzle for five minutes to dissolve scale, then flush with clean water.

### Short-Term Storage (1 to 4 Weeks)
If you do not plan to operate your pressure washer for seven to thirty days:
1. Perform the complete shutdown and depressurization procedure.
2. Slide the engine fuel shut-off valve lever to the OFF position.
3. Clean the unit with a damp cloth and check for loose hardware.
4. Store the unit in a dry, covered location away from open flames, pilot lights, or electric motors.
5. Cover with a breathable protective tarp; do not use a plastic sheet which traps moisture.

### Long-Term Off-Season Storage (Over 4 Weeks)
When storing the pressure washer for over thirty days, take comprehensive preservation measures. Untreated fuel degrades rapidly, forming varnishes that clog carburetor jets. Water left in the pump causes oxidation and freeze damage.

WARNING: Gasoline is highly flammable. Do not drain fuel near open flames or in enclosed spaces. Empty fuel outdoors using chemical-resistant gloves and safety goggles. Store fuel in approved containers.

#### Fuel System Stabilization and Draining
To protect the fuel system, select one of these stabilization methods:

*Method A: Fuel Stabilizer Treatment (Recommended)*
1. Fill the fuel tank to 90 percent capacity with fresh unleaded gasoline and add fuel stabilizer.
2. Run the engine outdoors for ten minutes to distribute stabilized fuel through the fuel lines and carburetor.
3. Shut down the engine, turn off the water, and depressurize. Store with the fuel tank full.

*Method B: Draining the Fuel System completely*
1. Place an approved gasoline container beneath the carburetor float bowl outdoors.
2. Close the fuel valve, remove the carburetor drain bolt, and open the fuel valve to drain the tank and carburetor.
3. Reinstall the drain bolt and washer. Pull the recoil starter handle three times to clear cylinder fuel.

#### Engine Cylinder and Valve Preservation
To prevent the cylinder, piston rings, and valves from rusting during storage:
1. Remove the spark plug and pour one tablespoon (15 milliliters) of clean engine oil into the cylinder.
2. Cover the hole with a rag and pull the recoil starter slowly five times to coat the cylinder walls.
3. Reinstall the F7RTC spark plug; do not connect the plug wire.
4. Pull the recoil starter slowly until resistance is felt to close the valves and seal the combustion chamber.

#### Pump Winterization and Freeze Protection
Water trapped inside the triplex pump is the greatest storage hazard. Freeze expansion below 32 degrees Fahrenheit (0 degrees Celsius) will fracture the brass pump manifold. Even in warm climates, stagnant water causes internal corrosion.

CAUTION: Always protect the pump from freezing using Ridgeline Pump Saver fluid or non-toxic RV antifreeze. Failure to winterize will cause freeze damage that voids the warranty. Do not use toxic automotive antifreeze.

To winterize the pump:
1. Shut down the unit, disconnect the water, and remove all hoses.
2. Thread the pump saver canister brass adapter onto the pump 3/4-inch garden hose inlet.
3. Discharge the pressurized canister contents into the pump.
4. While spraying, slowly pull the recoil starter handle three times to distribute fluid through check valves.
5. Spray until fluid discharges from the high-pressure M22 outlet. Disconnect canister and wipe spills.

### Removing the Equipment from Storage
To recommission the pressure washer after storage:
1. Move the unit outdoors to a level, well-ventilated area.
2. Clean dust and debris from the cooling fins, recoil starter, muffler, and pump housing.
3. Inspect the fuel lines, carburetor, and engine block for leaks or damage.
4. Remove the spark plug, verify the electrode gap is between 0.028 and 0.031 inches (0.7 to 0.8 millimeters), clean any carbon deposits, or replace with a new F7RTC plug, and reinstall it securely.
5. Check the engine oil level. Change the oil if it was not serviced before storage.
6. Fill the fuel tank with fresh unleaded gasoline.
7. Connect the garden hose to the pump inlet and run water for one minute with the high-pressure hose disconnected to flush pump saver fluid.
8. Connect all hoses, guns, and wands. Purge trapped air and perform a pre-operation check.

## 6. Troubleshooting

### Engine Subsystem Troubleshooting

Problem: Engine will not start.
Possible cause: Engine ignition switch is OFF; fuel shut-off valve is closed; choke lever is in RUN on a cold engine; engine oil level is low, triggering the low-oil shutdown sensor; spark plug wire is disconnected; spark plug is dirty or improperly gapped; stale fuel in the tank; hydrostatic pressure is built up in the pump.
Corrective action:
1. Turn the ignition switch to the ON (I) position.
2. Slide the fuel shut-off valve lever to the far right (ON) position.
3. Slide the choke lever to the far left (CHOKE) position for cold starts.
4. Verify the oil level is at the "FULL" mark on the yellow dipstick; add SAE 10W-30 engine oil as needed to disengage the low-oil shutdown sensor.
5. Press the spark plug wire boot firmly onto the F7RTC spark plug terminal.
6. Remove the spark plug, clean carbon deposits, check the gap (0.028 to 0.031 inches), and replace if cracked.
7. Drain old fuel and refill with fresh unleaded gasoline.
8. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger to relieve trapped water pressure before pulling the recoil starter handle.

Problem: Engine starts but runs rough, misfires, or idles erratically.
Possible cause: Choke lever left in CHOKE; dirty air filter element; fouled spark plug; water or dirt in the fuel system; carburetor jetting is restricted.
Corrective action:
1. Gradually move the choke lever to the RUN position as the engine warms.
2. Inspect and clean the air filter foam sleeve as outlined in Section 5; replace the paper core if clogged.
3. Clean and regap the F7RTC spark plug to 0.028–0.031 inches.
4. Drain the carburetor float bowl using the drain bowl to clear sediment, then refill the fuel tank with fresh gasoline.
5. Clean the fuel sediment cup under the fuel valve. For persistent issues, contact an authorized Ridgeline Outdoor Power dealer.
6. Inspect the black fuel tank cap vent holes. Clear any debris to prevent vacuum lock in the fuel tank.

Problem: Engine runs but stalls or shuts down unexpectedly during operation.
Possible cause: Engine is low on oil, triggering the low-oil shutdown sensor; empty fuel tank; clogged fuel line; dirty cooling fins.
Corrective action:
1. Position the pressure washer on level ground and verify the oil level is at the "FULL" mark on the yellow dipstick; add oil if low.
2. Check the fuel level in the tank; refuel outdoors with fresh gasoline if empty.
3. Inspect fuel lines for kinks. If blocked, contact an authorized service dealer.
4. Shut off the engine, allow it to cool, and clean dirt from the cylinder cooling fins and recoil starter cover.

Problem: Engine lacks power or bogs down when the spray gun trigger is squeezed.
Possible cause: Clogged spark arrestor screen; spark plug gap is too wide; carburetor jetting is restricted; clogged high-pressure spray nozzle.
Corrective action:
1. Verify that the engine is running at its full governed speed of 3,600 RPM.
2. Remove the muffler guard, slide out the spark arrestor screen, and clean carbon blockages with a brass wire brush.
3. Inspect and reset the spark plug gap to 0.028–0.031 inches.
4. Clean the quick-connect nozzle orifice using the wire clean-out tool to eliminate hydraulic restriction.

Problem: Engine produces excessive black smoke from the exhaust muffler.
Possible cause: Choke lever is partially closed; air filter element is saturated with dirt or oil; carburetor float is stuck or leaking.
Corrective action:
1. Push the choke lever fully to the far right (RUN) position.
2. Clean the air filter foam sleeve and replace the paper core if restricted.
3. Tap the carburetor float bowl gently with a soft mallet to free a stuck float; have the carburetor serviced if it continues.

Problem: Recoil starter rope is extremely hard to pull or snaps back violently.
Possible cause: High hydrostatic water pressure is trapped in the pump head and high-pressure hose; engine cylinder is flooded with fuel.
Corrective action:
1. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger to release trapped pressure before pulling the recoil starter handle.
2. Remove the F7RTC spark plug and pull the recoil starter rope several times to expel excess fuel, then reinstall the plug and start again.

### Pump and Hydraulic Subsystem Troubleshooting

Problem: Pump does not build pressure or produces very low discharge pressure.
Possible cause: Incorrect nozzle installed; municipal water supply flow rate is low; inlet water screen is clogged; garden hose is kinked; trapped air in pump.
Corrective action:
1. Install a high-pressure nozzle (0, 15, 25, or 40-degree); do not use the black soap nozzle.
2. Verify the water supply provides at least 7.0 GPM at 20 to 80 PSI.
3. Disconnect the garden hose and clean the stainless steel wire mesh inlet screen inside the pump coupling.
4. Straighten the garden hose to remove all kinks or flat spots.
5. Squeeze the spray gun trigger to purge trapped air until a steady stream of low-pressure water flows.

Problem: Pump pressure fluctuates, pulses, or is inconsistent.
Possible cause: Municipal water supply is restricted; inlet water screen is blocked; spray nozzle is partially clogged; air leak at pump inlet connection.
Corrective action:
1. Confirm the garden hose has a minimum internal diameter of 5/8 inch and is no longer than 50 feet.
2. Clean the inlet screen and clear any obstruction from the nozzle orifice using the wire clean-out tool.
3. Tighten the garden hose connection at the pump inlet to ensure an airtight seal.

Problem: Thermal relief valve is continuously discharging hot water.
Possible cause: Engine has been idling without spraying for over two minutes; relief valve is dirty or stuck open; relief valve is defective.
Corrective action:
1. Squeeze the spray gun trigger immediately to discharge heated water and draw cool water into the pump head. Do not idle the engine over two minutes.
2. Shut off the engine, turn off the water, allow the unit to cool, and flush the pump with clean water.
3. If the valve leaks cold water when the machine is first started, replace the valve.

Problem: Water leaks from the high-pressure hose connection at the pump or gun.
Possible cause: Threaded M22 collar is loose; internal high-pressure rubber O-ring is worn, damaged, or missing; connection threads are stripped.
Corrective action:
1. Shut down the engine, relieve pressure, and hand-tighten the M22 collar clockwise.
2. Inspect and replace the rubber O-ring at the tip of the connector if worn, applying water-resistant silicone grease to the seal.
3. Replace the 50-foot high-pressure hose if the metal threads are stripped or cross-threaded.

Problem: Water leaks from the garden hose connection at the pump inlet.
Possible cause: Garden hose coupling is loose or cross-threaded; inlet hose washer is worn or missing; inlet coupling is cracked.
Corrective action:
1. Unthread the garden hose, align the threads carefully, and hand-tighten the connection collar clockwise until snug.
2. Inspect the female inlet fitting. Ensure the rubber hose washer is present and seated flat behind the wire mesh screen; replace if cracked.
3. Replace the pump inlet fitting if cracked.

Problem: Spray nozzle is ejected from the wand or leaks water around the coupler.
Possible cause: Brass quick-connect collar was not fully locked; nozzle hex shank is worn; coupler ball bearings are dirty.
Corrective action:
1. Pull back the quick-connect collar, push the nozzle firmly until it bottoms out, and release the collar. Verify that the collar snaps completely forward. Pull the nozzle to check the lock.
2. Replace the color-coded nozzle if the hex shank is worn.
3. Clean and lubricate the coupler sleeve with silicone grease.

Problem: The oil in the triplex pump's sight glass has turned milky or cloudy white, and the pump runs noticeably louder.
Possible cause: Water has passed the plunger seals into the pump crankcase and emulsified with the pump oil; the milky appearance in the sight glass is the first sign of seal wear on the triplex pump.
Corrective action: Stop the engine, drain the pump crankcase through the drain plug, replace the plunger seals, refill with 12 ounces of Ridgeline RP-PO-40 pump oil to the center of the sight glass, and check the sight glass again after one hour of use.

### Detergent and Accessory Subsystem Troubleshooting

Problem: Detergent is not siphoning or mixing with the water stream.
Possible cause: High-pressure nozzle is installed; clear plastic chemical siphon tube is disconnected; siphon tube is kinked; siphon inlet filter screen is clogged; detergent is too thick; check-valve is stuck.
Corrective action:
1. Install the color-coded Black Soap Nozzle. The chemical siphon system will not draw detergent under high pressure.
2. Connect the clear plastic chemical siphon tube firmly to the brass chemical injection barb on the pump outlet.
3. Clear any kinks or pinches along the siphon tube.
4. Clean the siphon screen at the bottom of the onboard detergent tank.
5. Dilute the detergent with water.
6. Press the internal ball-check valve inside the brass barb with a toothpick to free a stuck check-ball, then flush with warm water.

Problem: Clear plastic chemical siphon tube is leaking detergent or drawing air.
Possible cause: Siphon tube is split, cracked, or hardened due to chemical exposure; tube is loose on the brass barb fitting.
Corrective action:
1. Replace the clear plastic chemical siphon tube with a new chemical-resistant silicone tube of identical dimensions.
2. Push the tube completely over the brass chemical injection barb and secure it with a small zip-tie to ensure an airtight seal.

Problem: High-pressure hose is stiff, kinked, or shows localized blistering.
Possible cause: Hose was stored with water under freezing conditions; hose was dragged over sharp corners; hose touched the hot engine muffler.
Corrective action:
1. Discard the damaged hose immediately. Never attempt to repair high-pressure hoses. Replace with an original Ridgeline Outdoor Power 4,000 PSI-rated replacement hose.
2. Always uncoil the 50-foot high-pressure hose before starting the engine and keep it clear of the hot exhaust muffler during operation.
