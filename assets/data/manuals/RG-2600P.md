## 1. Important Safety Instructions

### California Proposition 65 and Emissions Compliance
WARNING: This product contains or emits chemicals known to the State of California to cause cancer and birth defects or other reproductive harm. Engine exhaust, some of its constituents, and certain vehicle components contain or emit these chemicals. Avoid inhaling exhaust, do not idle the engine except as necessary, service your equipment in a well-ventilated area, and wear gloves or wash your hands frequently when servicing your equipment. For more information regarding state-mandated health advisories and environmental regulations, consult local authorities.

The engine installed on this Ridgeline Outdoor Power equipment is certified by the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and, where applicable, the California Air Resources Board (CARB) to meet strict emission standards for spark-ignited utility engines. Maintenance, replacement, or repair of the emission control devices and systems may be performed by any nonroad engine repair establishment or individual. However, warranty repairs must be performed by an authorized Ridgeline Outdoor Power service dealer. The emission control system consists of the carburetor, the ignition system, and the fuel tank, lines, cap, and carbon canister (where applicable). Do not alter or modify the engine in any way that would increase emissions or bypass these safety controls.

### Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Hazards
WARNING: Carbon monoxide is an odorless, colorless, and extremely toxic gas produced by internal combustion engines. Inhaling carbon monoxide can cause brain damage, severe illness, or death within minutes of exposure. Never operate the engine indoors or in enclosed spaces, even if doors and windows are open. Operating this equipment in garages, sheds, basements, or any other confined structure is strictly prohibited. Only operate the equipment outdoors, far away from open windows, doors, ventilation intakes, or other openings that could allow carbon monoxide gas to drift inside occupied structures.

Symptoms of carbon monoxide poisoning include headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness, sleepiness, confusion, and disorientation. If you or anyone else exhibits any of these symptoms while operating this pressure washer, immediately shut off the engine, move to fresh outdoor air, and seek emergency medical attention. Ensure that all persons operating this equipment are aware of the hazards of carbon monoxide and trained in proper outdoor positioning of the machine to prevent exhaust gas accumulation.

### Fuel Handling Safety and Fire Prevention
WARNING: Gasoline is highly flammable, volatile, and explosive. Liquid gasoline and its vapors can ignite from spark, static electricity, hot exhaust parts, or open flames, causing catastrophic fire or explosion, severe personal injury, or property damage.

Observe the following safety precautions during fuel handling and engine operation:
1. Store gasoline only in containers specifically designed and approved for fuel storage. Keep fuel containers out of reach of children in a cool, dry, well-ventilated location.
2. Always shut off the engine and allow it to cool completely for at least two minutes before removing the fuel cap and refueling.
3. Refuel the fuel tank outdoors in an open, well-ventilated area. Do not refuel indoors, in garages, or near ignition sources such as pilot lights.
4. Do not smoke or permit open flames or sparks near the fuel tank during refueling.
5. Avoid overfilling the fuel tank. Fill the tank only to approximately one inch below the bottom of the filler neck to allow space for fuel expansion. Securely tighten the fuel tank cap immediately after refueling.
6. Clean up any spilled fuel immediately using clean, dry rags. Move the pressure washer at least ten feet away from the fueling site before starting the engine.
7. Inspect the fuel system regularly for signs of leakage or deterioration. Replace any damaged components before operating.
8. Never use gasoline containing more than ten percent ethanol (E10), as this can damage the engine fuel system and void the product warranty. Do not use methanol or diesel fuel.
9. Avoid prolonged skin contact with gasoline or inhalation of fuel vapors. If gasoline is spilled on clothing, change clothes immediately.
10. Ensure the fuel shut-off valve is closed whenever the engine is not running and during transport.

### General Operation and Safety Rules for Pressure Washers
WARNING: This high-pressure gas pressure washer is capable of causing severe fluid injection injuries, deep lacerations, and property damage. Always exercise extreme caution during operation, maintenance, and setup.

Training and Preparation:
1. Read, understand, and follow all instructions on the machine and in this manual before attempting to assemble, operate, or service the pressure washer. Keep this manual in a safe place for future reference.
2. Familiarize yourself thoroughly with all controls and their proper operation. Know how to stop the engine and disengage the pressure quickly.
3. Never allow children under the age of sixteen to operate this pressure washer. Only responsible, trained adults should be permitted to operate the machine.
4. Wear appropriate personal protective equipment, including heavy boots with slip-resistant soles, during operation. Avoid loose-fitting clothing that can become entangled in rotating parts.
5. Always wear safety glasses or eye shields during operation and while performing adjustments or repairs to protect against back-spray and thrown debris.
6. Thoroughly inspect the area where the pressure washer is to be used. Remove all welcome mats, toys, loose boards, wires, and other foreign objects that could be propelled or damaged.
7. Adjust the pressure washer setup to clear gravel or crushed rock surfaces to prevent gravel from being picked up and projected by the high-pressure stream.
8. Wear adequate hearing protection to reduce the risk of noise-induced hearing loss from the 208cc OHV engine.

Safe Operation Practices:
1. Never put hands, feet, or body parts near the pump or high-pressure spray nozzle. Contact with the high-pressure water stream can cause severe subcutaneous fluid injection.
2. The spray gun trigger safety lock, located on the handle, must be engaged when starting the engine and whenever the operator leaves the operating position.
3. The high-pressure hose must be fully uncoiled and free of kinks when starting the engine and when the machine is not actively spraying.
4. Always operate the pressure washer from the operator's position directly behind the handles. Maintain a firm grip on the handles.
5. Exercise extreme caution when operating on or crossing gravel drives, walks, or roads. Stay alert for hidden hazards.
6. If the pressure washer strikes a foreign object, stop the engine, disconnect the spark plug wire, and inspect for damage. Repair any damage before restarting.
7. If the pressure washer starts to vibrate abnormally, stop the engine, disconnect the spark plug wire, and inspect immediately for the cause. Vibration is generally a warning of damage.
8. Stop the engine and disconnect the spark plug wire whenever you leave the operating position, before unclogging the nozzles, and before making repairs.
9. Walk slowly and carefully on slopes. Do not operate on steep slopes. Exercise caution when changing direction on slopes to prevent tipping.
10. Do not overload the machine capacity by spraying at too fast a rate. Adjust the nozzle spray pattern and distance to match cleaning conditions.
11. Never operate at high pressure on slippery surfaces. Use the quick-connect nozzles with caution and look behind to avoid falling.
12. Disengage power to the axial cam pump when transporting or not actively cleaning.
13. Never operate without good visibility. Ensure the work area is clear.
14. Ensure the spray gun trigger safety lock is engaged to prevent accidental discharge in cold conditions. Do not operate if you cannot feel temperature changes.
15. Never attempt to make any adjustments while the engine is running, except where specifically recommended.

2. Always adjust the spray nozzle selection and verify the spray gun safety lock is engaged from the operator's position before starting the engine.

5. Always use a clean-out tool or wire brush to clear a clogged nozzle. Never use your hands or feet to clear a clogged nozzle or hose.
6. Keep bystanders, children, and pets at least seventy-five feet away from the pressure washer and high-pressure hose during operation.

### Spray Nozzle and Thermal Relief Valve Safety

1. Ensure quick-connect nozzles are securely locked to the end of the spray wand before operating. Check nozzle connections regularly.
2. Proceed slowly, letting the high-pressure stream cut into the dirt. Do not push the spray wand aggressively close to fragile surfaces.

### Children and Bystander Safety

### Never-Flat Wheel Installation
To facilitate mobility, you must install the two solid, puncture-resistant 10-inch polyurethane never-flat wheels to the axle brackets located at the lower rear of the main frame.
1. Locate the two 10-inch wheels, steel axle pins, flat washers, and hitch-pin cotter clips from the hardware package.
2. Before beginning assembly, ensure the fuel valve is closed and the spark plug wire is disconnected.
3. Tilt the main frame forward so that it rests safely on its front rubber feet and frame bumper.
CAUTION: Ensure the machine is stabilized in this position. Do not allow the unit to tip completely over, which can damage the engine shroud or carburetor.
4. Align the wheel axle bore with the mounting sleeve on the lower frame bracket. The protruding bearing shoulder must face inward toward the frame.
5. Insert a steel axle pin from the outside of the wheel through the frame axle sleeve.
6. Slide a flat washer over the inner tip of the axle pin and insert a cotter clip through the axle pin hole.
7. Repeat on the opposite side to install the second wheel.
8. Lower the pressure washer until both never-flat wheels and the front feet rest firmly on the floor. Verify that wheels rotate freely.

### Handlebar Assembly and Nozzle Storage Panel
The two-piece frame design requires the secure installation of the upper handlebar assembly onto the lower frame tubes to ensure structural rigidity and isolate vibration.
1. Locate the upper handlebar assembly, which features the padded operator handle, the nozzle storage panel, the spray gun holster, and the high-pressure hose storage hook.
2. Slide the open ends of the upper handlebar tubes down over the protruding ends of the lower frame tubes. Ensure that the curved operator handle sweeps backward, away from the engine shroud.
3. Push the handlebar downward until the mounting holes are completely aligned.
4. Insert the two carriage bolts (M8 size) from the inside of the frame tubes, pushing them outward. The square shoulder under the carriage bolt head must seat completely into the square portion of the inner mounting hole.
5. Thread the plastic star knobs onto the protruding ends of the carriage bolts on the outside of the frame tubes and hand-tighten both star knobs firmly by turning them clockwise.
WARNING: Do not operate the pressure washer with loose or partially tightened star knobs. Excessive engine vibration can cause loose knobs to back off, leading to structural failure.
6. Locate the four color-coded quick-connect spray nozzles and push each nozzle into its designated rubber storage grommet on the handlebar panel. Organize the nozzles in order of spray angle from left to right: Red (0 degrees), Green (25 degrees), White (40 degrees), and Black (Soap).

### Connecting the Spray Gun, Spray Wand, and High-Pressure Hose
To ensure leak-free operation under the hydraulic pressure of 2,600 PSI, all high-pressure connections must be clean, unmarred, and tightened to their mechanical stops. Refer to section 2. Specifications for connection details.
1. Remove any plastic protective caps from the fittings of the pump, spray gun, and high-pressure hose. Inspect threads and internal rubber O-rings.
WARNING: Never operate the pressure washer with damaged, worn, or missing O-rings. High-pressure water can easily blow past damaged seals, resulting in severe fluid injection injuries.
2. Thread the male end of the 21-inch metal spray wand into the female outlet port (M22 fitting) at the front of the spray gun. Hand-tighten the collar clockwise until fully engaged. Do not use tools.
3. Uncoil the 25-foot high-pressure hose completely to remove any twists. Operating a kinked hose under high pressure can cause the hose structure to fail.
4. Align one of the M22 female connections with the male inlet fitting at the bottom of the spray gun handle. Push the hose fitting in and thread the collar clockwise until tight.
5. Align the second female connection with the high-pressure outlet fitting on the pump. Push the hose fitting onto the pump outlet and hand-tighten the collar until snug. Ensure the hose does not touch the hot engine muffler.
6. Store the fully connected spray gun and wand assembly in the holster on the handlebar. Loop the high-pressure hose over the storage hook.

### Detergent Siphon Tube Connection
The RG-2600P features a detergent siphon system to draw chemical cleaning solutions from an external container using a clear plastic siphon tube. To enable chemical draw when utilizing the black soap nozzle, the siphon tube must be properly connected.
1. Locate the clear plastic chemical siphon tube and pre-attached weighted filter screen.
2. Route the clear plastic tube under the engine platform, keeping it clear of hot engine components or the rotating recoil starter cover.
3. Locate the brass chemical injection barb on the outlet side of the pump, adjacent to the M22 high-pressure hose outlet fitting.
4. Push the open end of the clear plastic siphon tube firmly over the brass chemical injection barb past the raised ridges to form an airtight seal.
CAUTION: Do not use tools to tighten the siphon tube onto the barb. If the tube is loose, a small zip-tie may be placed over the connection to prevent air leaks, which would stall chemical drawing.
5. Place the weighted filter end of the siphon tube completely into an external container of approved pressure washer detergent. Ensure the siphon tube is not kinked.

### Engine Oil Pre-Start Preparation
WARNING: This engine is shipped from the factory without oil in the crankcase. Operating the 196cc OHV engine without adding the proper type and amount of engine oil will cause immediate, catastrophic mechanical failure and void the warranty. You must fill the crankcase with the specified engine oil before attempting to start the engine.
1. Position the pressure washer on a flat, level, and solid horizontal surface. Filling the engine on an incline will result in an inaccurate oil level reading.
2. Clean the area surrounding the yellow oil fill cap/dipstick, located at the lower rear of the engine block, with a dry rag to prevent dirt from falling into the crankcase.
3. Turn the yellow oil fill cap/dipstick counterclockwise and pull it straight out of the fill neck. Wipe the dipstick blade dry.
4. Insert the angled oil funnel into the oil fill neck and slowly pour the entire 20-fluid ounce (0.6-liter) bottle of premium SAE 10W-30 engine oil into the fill neck.
5. Remove the oil funnel and insert the clean dipstick straight back into the oil fill neck. Push it completely in and turn it clockwise until it is fully threaded.
6. Unscrew the dipstick and inspect the oil level. Verify that the oil level is at the FULL mark. The oil level must never be below the LOW mark.
CAUTION: Overfilling the crankcase can cause smoke, spark plug fouling, oil aeration, or air filter damage. Underfilling will trigger the low-oil shutdown sensor, preventing the engine from starting.
7. Re-insert the dipstick and hand-tighten the yellow cap clockwise. Clean up any oil spills on the frame or engine block before proceeding.

### Fueling the Engine Fuel Tank
WARNING: Gasoline is highly volatile, flammable, and explosive. Observe all safety guidelines outlined in Fuel Handling Safety and Fire Prevention in Section 1 before adding fuel to the tank. Always refuel outdoors in a well-ventilated area, with the engine shut off and cooled for at least two minutes.
1. Ensure the pressure washer is positioned on a level, stable, outdoor surface, far away from open flames, sparks, pilot lights, or dry vegetation.
2. Clean the area around the fuel tank cap to prevent dirt, water, or debris from contaminating the fuel system.
3. Turn the black fuel tank cap counterclockwise, remove it, and set it down on a clean surface.
4. Inspect the fuel strainer cup inside the filler neck to ensure it is clean and seated. Never pour fuel if the strainer is missing.
5. Slowly pour fresh, clean, unleaded gasoline (minimum 87 octane, with a maximum of 10 percent ethanol content by volume) into the fuel tank.
6. Stop filling when the fuel level reaches approximately one inch below the bottom of the fuel filler neck to allow space for fuel expansion. The tank capacity is 0.9 gallons (3.4 liters).
WARNING: Do not overfill the fuel tank. Gasoline expands significantly as the engine heats up during operation. Overfilling can cause fuel to spill onto a hot engine or muffler, resulting in fire or explosion.
7. Re-install the black fuel tank cap and turn it clockwise until you hear distinct clicks, indicating that the cap is fully sealed and locked.
8. Wipe away any spilled fuel immediately. Move the pressure washer at least ten feet away from the fueling location before starting the engine.

### Connection to Water Source
The pump is designed to operate under continuous water feed. Running the pump dry, even for a few seconds, will cause extreme friction heat, ruin the high-pressure water seals, and score the stainless steel plungers, voiding the pump warranty.
1. Verify that the garden hose water supply is capable of delivering a continuous flow rate of at least 4.6 GPM at a steady pressure of 20 to 80 PSI. Do not attempt to use a rain barrel or bucket, as the pump is not self-priming.
2. Inspect the female garden hose inlet coupling on the pump. Ensure the internal wire mesh inlet screen is clean, intact, and seated flat inside the coupling.
WARNING: Never operate the pressure washer without the wire mesh inlet screen in place. Debris entering the pump will damage the check valves and cause catastrophic pump failure.
3. Run water through your garden hose for 30 seconds before connecting it to the pressure washer to clear any trapped air, dirt, or debris out of the line.
4. Thread the male end of the garden hose into the female garden hose inlet coupling on the pump. Tighten the connection collar clockwise by hand until snug.
5. Turn the water supply valve completely on and check the inlet connection for leaks.
6. Before starting the engine, purge all trapped air from the water system. Disengage the spray gun trigger safety lock.
7. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger. Continue holding until a steady, unbroken stream of water flows from the wand, free of air bubbles.
8. Release the trigger and engage the trigger safety lock. The pressure washer is now fully assembled and primed for operation.

## 4. Operation

Operating the Ridgeline Outdoor Power RG-2600P Gas Pressure Washer safely and effectively requires a thorough understanding of its operational controls, start-up procedures, hydraulic characteristics, and surface-specific cleaning methodologies. This section provides detailed instructions designed to maximize the performance of your 196cc OHV engine and direct-drive axial cam pump while maintaining strict compliance with safety regulations to prevent physical injury or equipment damage.

### Pre-Operation Inspection Checklist
Before starting the pressure washer engine, the operator must perform a comprehensive pre-operation inspection. This routine ensures that all critical subsystems are in safe operating condition, fluid levels are within specification, and the hydraulic circuit is structurally sound. Do not attempt to start or operate the machine if any component is damaged, leaking, or improperly configured.

1. Check the Unit Placement: Position the pressure washer on a flat, level, and solid horizontal surface outdoors. The machine must be operated on level ground to ensure proper gravity splash lubrication of the internal engine components and to prevent the low-oil shutdown sensor from prematurely stopping the engine. Ensure the unit is placed in a well-ventilated outdoor area, at least 20 feet away from any building windows, doors, or ventilation intakes, to prevent toxic carbon monoxide gas from accumulating or entering occupied structures.
2. Inspect for Fuel and Oil Leaks: Conduct a visual inspection of the entire engine block, carburetor, fuel lines, fuel tank, and undercarriage of the unit. Check for any wetness, residue, or the distinct odor of gasoline or engine oil. If a leak is detected, do not start the engine. Clean up all spills and have the unit serviced by an authorized Ridgeline Outdoor Power dealer before further use.
3. Verify the Engine Oil Level: Remove the yellow engine oil fill cap/dipstick, wipe the blade clean with a lint-free cloth, re-insert the dipstick fully into the fill neck, and remove it again to read the oil level. The oil level must sit precisely at the FULL mark or within the crosshatched region of the dipstick. If the oil level is low, add premium SAE 10W-30 engine oil in small increments using a clean funnel until the proper level is achieved. Securely hand-tighten the dipstick.
4. Check the Fuel Supply: Check the fuel level in the fuel tank. If refueling is necessary, ensure the engine is off and has cooled for at least two minutes. Refuel outdoors using fresh, clean, unleaded gasoline (minimum 87 octane, maximum 10 percent ethanol content), leaving at least one inch of airspace below the filler neck for fuel expansion. Securely tighten the black fuel tank cap until it clicks multiple times.
5. Inspect the High-Pressure Hose: Uncoil the 25-foot high-pressure hose completely and inspect its entire length. Look for physical abnormalities, including deep cuts, severe abrasions, localized bulges, kinks, soft spots, or exposed wire braiding. Check that the metal M22 threaded fittings are clean and that the internal elastomeric O-rings are present, fully seated, and free of cracks or dry rot.
WARNING: Never attempt to use a high-pressure hose that is damaged, kinked, or shows signs of wear. Under the extreme hydraulic pressure of 2,600 PSI, a compromised hose can rupture catastrophically, discharging a high-velocity stream of water capable of causing severe fluid injection injuries or deep lacerations.
6. Verify Spray Gun and Wand Integrity: Check that the 21-inch metal spray wand is threaded securely into the ergonomic spray gun handle's M22 female outlet port. Inspect the spray gun assembly for cracks or structural failures. Test the trigger mechanism to ensure it moves smoothly and returns to its neutral position when released. Engage the red spray gun trigger safety lock located on the underside of the trigger handle. The trigger must not be able to depress when the safety lock is in the active position.
7. Inspect the Pump Water Inlet Screen: Examine the female garden hose inlet coupling on the direct-drive axial cam pump. Use a flashlight to verify that the internal stainless steel wire mesh inlet screen is clean, seated flat, and free of mineral scale, algae, or debris. If the screen is dirty, remove it, flush it with clean water, and reinstall it. If the screen is damaged or missing, do not connect the water supply.
8. Check the Never-Flat Wheels and Hardware: Ensure that both 10-inch polyurethane never-flat wheels are securely attached to the axle pins with their respective flat washers and cotter clips. Verify that the two plastic star knobs securing the upper handlebar assembly are tight and that the rubber storage grommets on the nozzle storage panel are intact.

### Water Supply Requirements and Hose Connections
The direct-drive axial cam pump installed on the RG-2600P relies on a continuous, uninterrupted flow of pressurized municipal water to lubricate and cool its internal valves, seals, and stainless steel plungers. Insufficient water supply will cause immediate cavitation, which induces intense vibration, loss of discharge pressure, and catastrophic thermal damage to the pump's hydraulic head.

To ensure proper pump operation, the water source must meet the following technical criteria:
- Water Volume and Flow Rate: The source must deliver a minimum flow rate of 4.6 GPM. This is double the pump's rated discharge of 2.3 GPM, providing an adequate hydraulic margin to prevent cavitation during operation.
- Source Pressure: The supply pressure must be regulated between a minimum of 20 PSI and a maximum of 80 PSI. Do not connect the pressure washer to water sources with pressures exceeding 80 PSI, as excessive inlet pressure can damage the low-pressure seals inside the pump.
- Water Temperature: The inlet water temperature must not exceed 100 degrees Fahrenheit (38 degrees Celsius). Never connect this pressure washer to a hot water tap or residential hot water system. Warm water degrades the life of the pump's internal seals and accelerates thermal loading.
- Supply Hose Length and Diameter: Use a heavy-duty, reinforced garden hose with a minimum internal diameter of 5/8 inch (16 millimeters). The supply garden hose should not exceed 50 feet (15.2 meters) in length to minimize frictional pressure drops and restrictional flow losses.

To connect the water supply to the pressure washer, follow this procedure:
1. Ensure the municipal water faucet is fully closed.
2. Uncoil your 5/8-inch garden hose completely to remove any twists, loops, or kinks.
3. Connect the female end of the garden hose to your municipal water faucet and tighten it securely.
4. Turn the municipal water faucet on fully for 30 seconds to flush out any loose rust, soil, scale, or trapped air from the interior of the hose. Once the water runs completely clear, shut off the faucet.
5. Align the male threaded brass fitting of the clean garden hose with the female 3/4-inch garden hose inlet coupling on the axial cam pump.
6. Thread the garden hose into the pump inlet clockwise by hand until it is tight and fully seated against the wire mesh inlet screen. Ensure the connection is not cross-threaded, which would allow air to draw into the pump during operation. Do not use wrenches or pliers, as over-tightening can crack the aluminum alloy pump housing.
7. Turn the municipal water faucet on fully. Inspect the inlet connection for water leaks. If water drips from the connection, shut off the faucet, disconnect the garden hose, verify that the wire mesh screen is seated flat, and reconnect the hose.

### Priming the Pump and Purging Air
Before starting the 196cc OHV engine, the entire high-pressure hydraulic circuit—including the axial cam pump, the 25-foot hose, the spray gun, and the 21-inch wand—must be primed with water and purged of all trapped air. Starting the engine with air pockets in the pump head or hoses creates a condition known as hydraulic shock or water hammer. This shock wave can fracture the internal stainless steel plungers, blow out the high-pressure seals, and cause the engine to kick back violently when you pull the recoil starter.

CAUTION: Running the direct-drive axial cam pump dry, or operating the engine before purging all air from the water circuit, will cause immediate friction-induced heat build-up. This will destroy the high-pressure water seals and score the pump pistons within seconds, voiding the product warranty.

To safely purge air and prime the pump, execute the following steps in sequence:
1. Ensure the engine fuel valve is in the closed position and the ignition switch is set to the OFF position.
2. Confirm that the municipal garden hose is connected to the pump inlet and that the water supply valve is opened completely.
3. Make sure the 25-foot high-pressure hose is securely connected to the pump's M22 outlet fitting and the spray gun's M22 inlet fitting.
4. Remove any spray nozzles from the 1/4-inch brass quick-connect coupler at the tip of the 21-inch spray wand. Operating with an open wand allows air and debris to exit the system with minimal restriction.
5. Point the spray wand in a safe direction, away from yourself, bystanders, pets, and any electrical sources.
6. Disengage the red trigger safety lock on the spray gun handle.
7. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger completely. Trapped air will begin escaping from the open wand, followed by a sputtering, inconsistent mixture of water and air.
8. Continue holding the spray gun trigger depressed until a solid, steady, and unbroken stream of low-pressure water flows continuously from the tip of the wand for at least 15 seconds.
9. Release the spray gun trigger to halt the water flow.
10. Engage the red trigger safety lock. The hydraulic circuit is now fully primed, and the pressure washer is ready for the engine starting procedure.

### Starting the Engine (Cold and Warm Starts)
The 196cc OHV engine features a reliable transistorized magneto ignition system and an automatic compression release mechanism. However, because the engine is directly coupled to the axial cam pump, pulling the recoil starter rope rotates both the engine crankshaft and the pump pistons simultaneously. If water pressure has built up inside the pump head, the resistance can make the starter rope extremely difficult to pull, and it may snap back violently, causing hand or arm injury. Squeezing the spray gun trigger to relieve backpressure during the starting sequence is a critical requirement.

WARNING: Never start or run the engine indoors or in a poorly ventilated area. Engine exhaust contains carbon monoxide, an odorless, colorless, and highly toxic gas that can cause unconsciousness, brain damage, or death within minutes of exposure. Only operate the engine outdoors, far from doors, windows, and ventilation intakes.

To start a cold engine (an engine that has not been run recently, or has cooled completely):
1. Complete all pre-operation checks, connect the water supply, and prime the pump as described in the preceding sections.
2. Locate the fuel shut-off valve lever beneath the air filter housing on the side of the engine. Slide the fuel valve lever to the far right (ON) position to allow fuel to flow into the carburetor float bowl.
3. Locate the choke lever, situated directly above the fuel valve lever. Slide the choke lever to the far left (CHOKE) position. This restricts airflow into the carburetor, enriching the fuel mixture for a cold start.
4. Locate the engine ignition switch (rocker switch) on the side of the engine block. Flip the switch to the ON (I) position.
5. Standing behind the unit, grasp the spray gun handle firmly with your right hand. Disengage the red trigger safety lock.
6. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger fully with your right hand to relieve any hydrostatic pressure that has built up in the pump. Point the wand in a safe direction. Keep the trigger squeezed throughout the engine pull-start process.
7. Use your left hand to grasp the plastic recoil starter handle. Pull the starter rope slowly until you feel increased mechanical resistance. This indicates the engine piston is reaching the top of its compression stroke.
8. Once resistance is felt, pull the starter rope rapidly and firmly in a single, continuous, upward and outward motion. Do not let go of the starter handle at the end of the pull; guide the rope slowly back into the recoil housing to prevent damage to the starter spring or pulley.
9. If the engine fires but does not continue to run, repeat steps 7 and 8.
10. As soon as the engine starts and begins to idle smoothly, gradually slide the choke lever to the far right (RUN) position over a period of 5 to 10 seconds. The engine speed is governed at a fixed 3,600 RPM; do not attempt to adjust the governor settings.
11. Release the spray gun trigger once the engine is idling smoothly, and immediately engage the red trigger safety lock to prevent accidental high-pressure water discharge.

To start a warm engine (an engine that has been shut down for less than 15 minutes and is still at operating temperature):
1. Turn the engine ignition switch to the ON (I) position.
2. Verify that the fuel shut-off valve is in the ON position.
3. Ensure the choke lever is placed in the RUN position. Do not apply choke to a warm engine, as this will flood the combustion chamber and prevent the engine from starting.
4. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger to relieve hydraulic pressure.
5. Pull the recoil starter handle rapidly and firmly until the engine starts.
6. Once running, release the trigger and engage the spray gun safety lock.

### Selecting and Utilizing the Quick-Connect Nozzles
The Ridgeline Outdoor Power RG-2600P is supplied with four color-coded, quick-connect spray nozzles that are stored on the handlebar panel. Each nozzle features a unique internal orifice size and spray angle designed to modify the velocity and shape of the high-pressure water stream. Selecting the correct nozzle is critical to achieving effective cleaning results while preventing permanent damage to the target surface.

The characteristics and intended applications of each nozzle are detailed below:
- 0-Degree Red Nozzle (Pinpoint Stream): This nozzle delivers a highly concentrated, non-fanning, pencil-thin stream of water at maximum impact velocity. It covers a very small surface area and is capable of gouging wood, stripping paint, and fracturing concrete.
  Applications: Removing baked-on mud from heavy industrial machinery, clearing rust from steel structural beams, or removing silicone or concrete splatter from metal surfaces.
  Warning: Never use this nozzle on wood, siding, asphalt shingles, vehicles, or soft masonry. Keep the nozzle at least 24 inches away from any surface.
- 25-Degree Green Nozzle (Flushing Stream): This nozzle produces a medium-width fan of water that provides an optimal balance between impact pressure and surface coverage. It is the most versatile and commonly used nozzle for residential cleaning.
  Applications: Washing mold, mildew, and dirt from vinyl, aluminum, or fiber-cement siding; sweeping leaves, lawn clippings, and light dirt from patios and concrete garage floors; cleaning gutters and brickwork.
- 40-Degree White Nozzle (Wash Stream): This nozzle generates a wide, low-impact fan of water. It distributes the 2,600 PSI over a much larger surface area, making it safe for delicate and painted surfaces.
  Applications: Washing automobiles, motorcycles, boats, and RVs; cleaning outdoor furniture, windows, and glass patio doors; rinsing light dust or pollen from painted wood, stucco, or delicate masonry.
- Black Soap Nozzle (Chemical Application): This nozzle has a very large internal orifice that drops the overall system operating pressure significantly. The low pressure creates a vacuum (venturi effect) in the pump's chemical injection barb, allowing the clear plastic siphon tube to draw detergent from an external container.
  Applications: Siphoning and applying pressure washer-approved soaps, detergents, and degreasers onto dirty surfaces before high-pressure rinsing.
  Note: The chemical injection siphon will only function when this black low-pressure soap nozzle is installed on the spray wand.

To safely install or change a quick-connect nozzle:
1. Release the spray gun trigger and engage the red trigger safety lock on the handle.
2. Point the spray wand down toward the ground in a safe direction.
3. Pull back the outer brass collar of the 1/4-inch quick-connect coupler on the tip of the wand using your thumb and forefinger.
4. Insert the hex shank of the desired color-coded nozzle straight into the coupler. Press the nozzle firmly inward until it bottoms out.
5. Release the brass collar, allowing it to spring forward into its locked position. You should hear a distinct click as the collar snaps forward.
6. Pull firmly on the nozzle to verify that it is securely locked in place. If the nozzle slides out, pull the collar back, push the nozzle in deeper, and re-engage the collar.
WARNING: Never attempt to change a nozzle while the spray gun trigger is squeezed or while the water system is pressurized. Always engage the spray gun safety lock. A partially locked or loose nozzle will be violently launched from the wand under 2,600 PSI of water pressure, creating a high-velocity projectile that can cause severe eye injury or skin puncture.

### Operating the Chemical Siphon Tube and External Detergent Supply
The RG-2600P features a detergent siphon system to draw chemical cleaning solutions from an external container using a clear plastic siphon tube. The chemical injection system utilizes a venturi siphon to mix concentrated detergent with the water stream at a calibrated draw ratio of approximately 12:1 to 15:1. This system allows you to apply cleaning chemicals directly to surfaces, dramatically improving the efficiency of the washing process.

Observe the following safety guidelines and operational procedures when using detergents:
- Approved Chemicals Only: Use only biodegradable, non-toxic detergents and soaps specifically formulated and labeled for use in high-pressure washers. These chemicals are designed not to degrade the polyurethane and stainless steel seals inside the pump.
WARNING: Never use household liquid bleach, ammonia, highly acidic chemicals, strong alkaline solutions, organic solvents, flammable liquids, or abrasive powders with this chemical siphon. Utilizing unapproved chemicals can cause toxic gas release, chemical burns, or permanent chemical corrosion of the pump head, which will void the warranty.
- Detergent Dilution: Carefully read the mixing instructions on the detergent container. Many commercial concentrates must be diluted with clean water in your external bucket or container before being siphoned to ensure smooth, consistent siphoning.
- Detergent Application Procedure:
  1. Prepare the diluted detergent in an external container or bucket of sufficient capacity for your cleaning task.
  2. Ensure the weighted filter screen end of the clear plastic chemical siphon tube is placed completely at the bottom of the external container, submerged in the detergent solution.
  3. Verify that the opposite end of the clear plastic chemical siphon tube is firmly connected to the brass chemical injection barb on the pump outlet.
  4. Install the color-coded Black Soap Nozzle into the 1/4-inch quick-connect coupler on the spray wand. The siphon will not function with any other nozzle.
  5. Disengage the spray gun trigger safety lock.
  6. Point the wand at the target surface and squeeze the trigger. Within 10 to 15 seconds, detergent will be drawn through the clear tube and will begin discharging from the nozzle as a low-pressure, soapy spray.
  7. Apply the detergent to the surface starting from the bottom of the work area and working upward. Applying soap from the bottom up prevents dirty streaks from forming and burning into the dry surface.
  8. Allow the detergent to remain on the surface for 3 to 5 minutes to break down dirt and grime. Do not allow the detergent to dry completely on the surface, as this can cause permanent staining or damage to paint and siding.
  9. Once the chemical has set, release the trigger, engage the safety lock, and replace the black soap nozzle with the appropriate high-pressure nozzle (such as the Green 25-degree or White 40-degree nozzle) to rinse the area.
- System Flushing Procedure:
  To prevent dry soap residue, chemical crystals, or sticky film from clogging the internal ball-check valve in the brass venturi injector, you must flush the chemical siphon system after every use.
  1. Turn off the engine and allow the system to cool.
  2. Remove the weighted filter end of the clear plastic siphon tube from the external detergent container.
  3. Submerge the weighted filter end completely in a container of clean, lukewarm water.
  4. Install the black soap nozzle, start the engine using the proper starting procedure, and squeeze the spray gun trigger.
  5. Spray the clean water through the soap nozzle for 2 minutes. This flushes the chemical tube, weighted filter screen, and pump injection port with fresh water.
  6. Shut down the engine, turn off the water supply, and depressurize the system.

### Safe Spraying Guidelines, Distance, and Sweep Speed
The immense hydraulic energy generated by the RG-2600P is highly effective for cleaning, but it can also be incredibly destructive if misapplied. The force of the high-pressure water stream can erode masonry, gouge wood fibers, slice through tire sidewalls, and shatter window glass. Understanding how to manage kickback force, spray distance, and sweeping speed is essential for safe and damage-free operation.

- Managing Kickback Force: When you squeeze the spray gun trigger, a backward reactive force is generated immediately as high-pressure water exits the nozzle. This is called kickback, and it can cause the spray wand to jerk upward or sideways, potentially throwing the operator off balance or causing the wand to strike the face or body.
  Operator Stance: Always stand on a stable, level surface. Do not operate the pressure washer from a ladder, scaffolding, step stool, or any elevated platform that does not have secure handrails. Keep both feet planted firmly, slightly wider than shoulder-width apart.
  Two-Handed Grip: Grip the spray gun handle firmly with your dominant hand, wrapping your fingers securely around the trigger and handle grip. Place your non-dominant hand on the insulated grip sleeve of the metal spray wand. Never operate the pressure washer with one hand.
- Spray Distance and Angle: The impact pressure exerted on a surface decreases exponentially as the nozzle is moved farther away. Conversely, bringing the nozzle too close to a surface concentrates the energy on a small point and can cause instant damage.
  Initial Distance: Always begin spraying with the nozzle positioned at least 3 to 4 feet away from the target surface. Squeeze the trigger and slowly bring the nozzle closer, stopping when you observe that the water is effectively removing the dirt without damaging the material.
  Angled Spraying: Do not hold the nozzle perpendicular (90 degrees) to the surface being washed. Pointing the spray stream directly at a surface drives dirt and water deep into the pores of the material and can cause surface erosion. Instead, hold the wand at a 45-degree angle relative to the surface. This creates a chiseling action that lifts dirt up and sweeps it away to the side, minimizing direct impact force. Always test on an inconspicuous area first before cleaning the entire surface.
- Sweeping Motion: Keep the nozzle moving continuously across the surface. Do not hold the high-pressure stream stationary over a single point, as this can quickly cut into wood siding, concrete sealers, or paint. Use a steady, overlapping, side-to-side sweeping motion. Maintain a consistent distance from the surface throughout the entire sweep to ensure an even, uniform clean without dark or light striping marks. Never use sweeping motions that spray directly upward beneath siding panels, as this can force water behind the cladding and cause mold or structural rot.

### Thermal Relief Valve System Operation and Idle Limitations
When the engine of the RG-2600P is running at its governed speed of 3,600 RPM but the operator has released the spray gun trigger, the pump enters a bypass mode. In this mode, the pump's internal bypass valve continuously recirculates the same small volume of water inside the pump head instead of discharging it.

As the pistons continue to stroke, friction from the moving parts and the compression of the water generate intense heat. If this bypass state continues for more than two minutes, the water inside the pump head will quickly reach temperatures exceeding 140 degrees Fahrenheit (60 degrees Celsius). This extreme heat will melt the internal polymer seals, damage the check valves, and crack the stainless steel pistons.

To protect the pump from catastrophic thermal failure, a specialized thermal relief valve is threaded into the pump body.
- Activation Threshold: When the water temperature inside the pump head reaches approximately 140 degrees Fahrenheit (60 degrees Celsius), the thermal relief valve opens automatically.
- Discharge Behavior: Once open, the valve discharges a small stream of extremely hot water from its lower drain port onto the ground beneath the pressure washer frame. This allows fresh, cold municipal water from your garden hose to enter the pump head, immediately cooling the internal components.
- Automatic Reset: As soon as the internal water temperature drops below the thermal threshold, the valve closes automatically and seals the system.
- Safety Warning:
WARNING: Keep your hands, feet, and face clear of the thermal relief valve's drain port located under the pump housing. The water discharged from this valve is scalding hot and can cause severe burns on contact. Do not touch the valve or the surrounding metal pump housing during or immediately after engine operation.
- Idle Limitation Rule: The thermal relief valve is intended as an emergency fail-safe device only. It is not designed to handle frequent, prolonged cycling.
CAUTION: Never allow the pressure washer engine to run for more than two consecutive minutes without squeezing the spray gun trigger to discharge water. If you must stop washing to move hoses, clear debris, or take a break for longer than two minutes, you must shut off the engine immediately. This practice prevents pump overheating, extends seal life, and conserves fuel.

### Shutting Down the Pressure Washer and System Depressurization
When you have finished your cleaning task, or when you need to perform maintenance or store the machine, you must follow a strict shutdown and depressurization procedure. Simply turning off the engine does not remove the high-pressure water trapped within the 25-foot hose and the pump head. This trapped hydrostatic pressure makes it impossible to disconnect the hoses and represents a major safety hazard if the trigger is pulled accidentally.

To shut down and completely depressurize the system:
1. Release the spray gun trigger and engage the red trigger safety lock.
2. Allow the engine to run at idle for 15 to 30 seconds to allow the combustion chamber and exhaust system to cool gradually.
3. Locate the red engine ignition switch on the engine block and flip it to the OFF (O) position. The engine will stop rotating.
4. Locate the fuel shut-off valve lever and slide it to the far left (OFF) position. This cuts off fuel flow to the carburetor and prevents fuel pooling during transport or storage.
5. Walk to your municipal water faucet and turn the water supply valve completely off.
6. Return to the pressure washer operating position and grasp the spray gun handle.
7. Disengage the red trigger safety lock.
8. Point the spray wand in a safe direction and squeeze the trigger. Hold the trigger depressed until all trapped water and high-pressure air have been completely discharged. The water will transition from a high-pressure burst to a low-pressure trickle, and then stop completely.
9. Verify that the system is fully depressurized by letting go of the trigger, waiting 5 seconds, and squeezing it once more. No water should discharge.
10. Engage the red trigger safety lock.
11. Disconnect the garden hose from the pump inlet by turning the connection collar counterclockwise. Residual water will drain from the hose and pump inlet.
12. Disconnect the high-pressure hose from the M22 pump outlet and the spray gun handle inlet by unscrewing the threaded collars counterclockwise.
13. Drain all remaining water from the high-pressure hose, the spray gun, and the metal wand before coiling the hose and storing the components on their respective frame hooks.

### High Altitude and Extreme Environmental Operation
Operating the RG-2600P gas pressure washer under extreme environmental conditions, such as high altitudes or extreme ambient temperatures, requires specific adjustments and precautions to maintain optimal engine performance and prevent mechanical wear.

- High Altitude Operation: At altitudes above 3,000 feet (914 meters), the atmospheric air density is significantly lower. This reduction in oxygen concentration alters the carburetor's air-fuel mixture, causing the engine to run rich (too much fuel, not enough air).
  Symptoms of Unadjusted Altitude Operation: Rough engine idling, excessive black smoke from the muffler, spark plug fouling, high fuel consumption, difficulty starting, and a severe reduction in engine horsepower, which can cause the pump discharge pressure to drop below 2,600 PSI.
  Corrective Action: If you regularly operate this pressure washer at altitudes above 3,000 feet, you must have an authorized Ridgeline Outdoor Power service dealer install a high-altitude carburetor jet kit. This kit adjusts the fuel metering nozzle to match the lower air density.
  Caution: If an engine modified for high-altitude use is operated at altitudes below 3,000 feet, the carburetor will supply a lean mixture (too much air, not enough fuel). This can cause the engine to overheat, run erratically, and sustain severe cylinder or valve damage. Ensure the original carburetor jetting is reinstalled when returning the unit to low-altitude environments.
- High Ambient Temperature Operation: Operating the unit in temperatures exceeding 100 degrees Fahrenheit (38 degrees Celsius) accelerates the thermal loading of both the engine and the pump. Ensure the engine's cooling fins are clear of debris to prevent overheating. Check oil levels frequently as high heat degrades viscosity. The thermal relief valve will activate faster because incoming water is warmer.
- Near-Freezing and Cold Weather Operation: Operating the pressure washer in temperatures approaching 32 degrees Fahrenheit (0 degrees Celsius) carries a severe risk of water freezing inside the pump head or hoses. If water freezes inside the axial cam pump, the ice expands. The immense pressure generated by expanding ice can crack the aluminum pump manifold, shatter the stainless steel pistons, and split the brass fittings. Do not operate the machine if the ambient temperature is below 40 degrees Fahrenheit (4.4 degrees Celsius). If you must use the machine in cold conditions, never leave the unit sitting with water in the pump when the engine is shut off. Immediately drain all hoses and perform the cold-weather storage preparation (using a pump saver fluid) as outlined in the storage section of this manual.

### Engine Maintenance Procedures
The 196cc OHV engine requires precise lubricating, air filtration, and electrical service to maintain its emissions compliance and power.

#### Engine Oil Inspection and Change
Engine oil degrades over time, losing viscosity and carrying metal particles. Changing oil at prescribed intervals prevents engine wear and seizing.

WARNING: Oil and exhaust are extremely hot immediately after operation. Allow the engine to cool for fifteen minutes before draining. Wear protective gloves and eyewear.

To change engine oil:
1. Position pressure washer on a flat, level, well-ventilated surface.
2. Run engine three to five minutes to warm oil, then shut down.
3. Turn off water supply and depressurize system.
4. Disconnect and secure spark plug wire.
5. Clean areas around oil fill cap/dipstick and drain bolt.
6. Place drain pan under base drain bolt.
7. Remove drain bolt and copper washer with 10mm wrench.
8. Tilt unit backward on 10-inch wheels to drain oil completely.
9. Clean drain bolt and washer. Replace washer if damaged.
10. Reinstall drain bolt, tightening to 10 to 12 foot-pounds.
11. Remove yellow oil fill cap/dipstick and wipe blade dry.
12. Insert clean funnel into oil fill neck.
13. Pour 20 fluid ounces (0.6 liters) of SAE 10W-30 oil into crankcase.
14. Remove funnel, insert dipstick, and tighten clockwise.
15. Unscrew dipstick to verify oil level is at FULL mark.
16. Reinstall dipstick, clean spills, and recycle used oil.

#### Air Filter Cleaning and Replacement
The air filter prevents dust from entering the carburetor and cylinder. Operating with a clogged filter restricts airflow, causing a rich air-fuel mixture, carbon-fouled spark plugs, exhaust smoke, and reduced power.

CAUTION: Never run the engine without the air filter installed. Unfiltered air will draw abrasive dirt into the carburetor, causing permanent engine damage and voiding the warranty.

To service the dual-element air filter:
1. Clean exterior of air filter cover.
2. Depress cover locking tabs and remove cover.
3. Remove air filter assembly (foam sleeve and paper core).
4. Separate foam sleeve from paper core.
5. Replace paper core if dirty or torn. Do not wash or blow dirt from it.
6. Wash foam sleeve in warm, soapy water, squeezing gently.
7. Rinse, squeeze dry in a clean towel, and air-dry completely.
8. Apply 5 to 10 drops of SAE 10W-30 engine oil to dry foam and squeeze to distribute. Remove excess oil with paper towel.
9. Slide foam sleeve back over paper core.
10. Clean housing base and cover with damp cloth.
11. Reinstall assembly into housing base, ensuring flat seating.
12. Snap cover back onto base.

#### Spark Plug Service and Gap Calibration
The spark plug ignites the air-fuel mixture. Over time, electrodes wear and carbon builds up, causing misfires, starting difficulties, and pressure loss.

To service the F7RTC spark plug:
1. Disconnect spark plug wire boot and clean plug base area.
2. Remove spark plug with a spark plug wrench.
3. Replace plug if insulator is cracked or electrodes are worn.
4. Tan insulator indicates normal operation; black indicates rich mixture, white indicates overheating.
5. Clean carbon from electrodes with wire brush. Do not use abrasives.
6. Measure gap with wire feeler gauge.
7. Verify gap is 0.028 to 0.031 inches (0.7 to 0.8 mm). Adjust ground electrode.
8. Verify crush washer is in place and clean threads.
9. Thread plug in by hand clockwise to avoid cross-threading.
10. Tighten plug: 1/2 turn past finger-tight for new, or 1/8 to 1/4 turn for used.
11. Tighten plug to 18 to 22 foot-pounds.
12. Reattach spark plug wire boot firmly.

#### Spark Arrestor Inspection and Cleaning
The muffler features a USDA Forest Service-approved spark arrestor screen that prevents burning carbon particles from being ejected. Operating without a clean spark arrestor is illegal in many jurisdictions.

To clean the screen:
1. Allow engine and muffler to cool completely.
2. Remove screws securing metal muffler guard and remove it.
3. Remove spark arrestor retention screw from exhaust nozzle.
4. Slide spark arrestor screen out of muffler.
5. Inspect screen and replace if torn or damaged.
6. Gently clean carbon deposits with fine-wire brass brush.
7. Slide clean screen back into exhaust nozzle.
8. Reinstall and tighten retention screw.
9. Secure muffler guard with mounting screws.

#### Engine Governor Limits and Calibration
WARNING: Do not tamper with engine governor spring, linkages, or speed screws. The governor is factory-set for a maximum speed of 3,600 RPM under load. Running the engine faster increases risks of pump rupture, engine failure, or flying metal shards, and voids the warranty. If speed is unstable, contact an authorized service dealer.

### Pump and Hydraulic System Maintenance
The direct-drive axial cam pump is the high-pressure heart of your machine. While the pump oil chamber is sealed and maintenance-free, the water-handling components require regular care to prevent wear and pressure loss.

#### Water Inlet Screen Inspection
The wire mesh screen inside the pump's water inlet coupling protects internal valves from damage by debris.
1. Turn off water supply and disconnect garden hose.
2. Examine screen inside coupling.
3. If dirty, pull it out carefully using needle-nose pliers.
4. Flush both sides with clean water. Replace if torn or corroded.
5. Reinsert screen flat until fully seated.

#### High-Pressure Spray Nozzle Maintenance
Scale, silt, and algae can build up inside nozzle orifices, causing flow restriction, pump backpressure, and erratic spray patterns.
1. Shut off engine, turn off water supply, and squeeze trigger to relieve pressure.
2. Engage trigger safety lock.
3. Pull back brass collar on wand's coupler and remove nozzle.
4. Use nozzle clean-out tool or a straightened paperclip.
5. Insert wire tool into orifice and slide back and forth to loosen debris.
6. Flush nozzle with water backward through spray tip.
7. Reinstall nozzle and verify connection.

WARNING: Never clean a nozzle while the system is pressurized or engine is running. Always engage spray gun safety lock.

#### Hose, Spray Gun, and Wand O-Ring Lubrication
Connections on the 25-foot hose, spray gun, and metal wand rely on O-rings to form seals under 2,600 PSI of pressure. Friction can wear, dry, or tear O-rings, causing leaks.
1. Inspect connections and O-rings before each use.
2. Apply water-resistant silicone grease to O-rings and fittings seasonally to prevent dry rot.
3. If an O-ring is damaged or leaking, remove it with a small plastic pick and install a replacement of identical size.

### Chemical Siphon Tube and Filter Screen Maintenance
Soap residues can crystallize inside the clear plastic siphon tube, weighted filter screen, and the internal check-ball mechanism of the pump's brass venturi injector, preventing the system from drawing soap when using the Black Soap Nozzle.
1. After each use of chemical siphon, execute flushing procedure in Section 4.
2. Inspect clear siphon tube for kinks, splits, or hardening. Replace if cracked or leaking air.
3. Seasonally, empty external detergent container. Submerge weighted filter end in warm water and household vinegar mixture (1:1 ratio) and run system with black soap nozzle for five minutes to dissolve scale and soap buildup.
4. Rinse tube and filter screen thoroughly with clean water.

### Short-Term Storage (1 to 4 Weeks)
If you do not plan to operate your pressure washer for more than seven days but less than thirty days, observe these short-term storage precautions:
1. Perform full shutdown and system depressurization procedure detailed in Section 4.
2. Ensure engine fuel shut-off valve is in OFF position.
3. Wipe down entire pressure washer with damp cloth to remove dirt and residues. Inspect unit for loose bolts or damaged parts.
4. Store pressure washer in dry, covered, and secure location out of reach of children. Do not store near open flames, boilers, water heaters, or other appliances with pilot lights or spark-emitting electric motors.
5. Cover unit with breathable protective tarp. Do not use non-breathable plastic sheet, which traps condensation and promotes corrosion.

### Long-Term Off-Season Storage (Over 4 Weeks)
When storing the pressure washer for winter or any extended period exceeding thirty days, you must take comprehensive preservation measures. Untreated gasoline degrades rapidly, forming sticky varnishes, gums, and acid compounds that will clog carburetor jets and ruin the fuel system. Water left inside the pump will cause internal oxidation, galvanic corrosion, and catastrophic freeze damage if temperatures drop.

WARNING: Gasoline is highly flammable and explosive. Do not drain the fuel tank or carburetor near open flames, sparks, pilot lights, heaters, or in enclosed unventilated spaces. Emptying fuel must be performed outdoors away from all ignition sources. Wear chemical-resistant gloves, safety goggles, and handle fuel in approved containers. Observe all instructions in Section 1.

#### Fuel System Stabilization and Draining
Choose one of these methods to protect the fuel system during storage:

Method A: Fuel Stabilizer Treatment (Recommended)
1. Fill fuel tank to 90 percent capacity with fresh unleaded gasoline.
2. Add commercial fuel stabilizer, following mixing ratio on the bottle.
3. Run engine outdoors for ten minutes to circulate stabilized fuel through carburetor.
4. Shut down engine, turn off water, depressurize, and store with full tank.

Method B: Draining the Fuel System completely
1. Place approved gasoline container beneath carburetor outdoors.
2. Close fuel shut-off valve.
3. Remove carburetor drain bolt and washer with wrench.
4. Open fuel shut-off valve to drain tank and carburetor completely.
5. Reinstall and tighten drain bolt and washer securely.
6. Pull recoil starter three to four times to clear residual fuel from cylinder.

#### Engine Cylinder and Valve Preservation
To prevent cylinder wall and valve faces from rusting during storage:
1. Remove spark plug.
2. Pour one tablespoon (15 milliliters) of SAE 10W-30 engine oil into cylinder hole.
3. Place clean rag over spark plug hole to catch oil spray.
4. Pull recoil starter slowly three to five times to distribute oil.
5. Reinstall and tighten F7RTC spark plug. Leave plug wire disconnected.
6. Pull recoil starter slowly until resistance is felt, closing valves to seal combustion chamber.

#### Pump Winterization and Freeze Protection
Water trapped inside the pump is the greatest hazard during storage. If the temperature drops below 32 degrees Fahrenheit (0 degrees Celsius), water will freeze, expand, and fracture the pump manifold. Stagnant water also causes corrosion and sticks internal valves.

CAUTION: Protect pump from freezing by treating it with Ridgeline Pump Saver fluid or non-toxic RV antifreeze before storing. Freeze damage is not covered under warranty. Do not use toxic automotive antifreeze, which damages internal seals.

To winterize the pump:
1. Ensure unit is shut down, water supply disconnected, and hoses removed.
2. Obtain Ridgeline Pump Saver fluid canister.
3. Thread canister's adapter onto pump's female garden hose inlet.
4. Push nozzle to discharge pressurized fluid into pump.
5. Squeezing nozzle, slowly pull recoil starter two to three times to circulate fluid.
6. Spray until pink or green fluid discharges from pump's M22 outlet.
7. Disconnect canister and wipe up overspray.

### Removing the Equipment from Storage
To safely recommission your pressure washer after storage:
1. Move unit outdoors to a well-ventilated, level area.
2. Clean dust or debris from cooling fins, recoil housing, muffler, and pump.
3. Inspect fuel lines, carburetor, and engine block for leaks.
4. Remove spark plug, verify 0.028 to 0.031 inch gap, clean or replace, and reinstall.
5. Check engine oil level; change if dirty or not done before storage.
6. Fill fuel tank with fresh gasoline. Replace fuel stored over six months.
7. Connect garden hose and run water for one minute with high-pressure hose disconnected to flush pump saver.
8. Connect high-pressure hose, gun, and wand. Prime system as detailed in Section 4. Perform pre-operation checks.

## 6. Troubleshooting

### Engine Subsystem Troubleshooting

Problem: Engine will not start.
Possible cause: Engine ignition switch is OFF; fuel valve is closed; choke lever is in RUN on a cold engine; low oil level; spark plug wire is disconnected; spark plug is fouled or improperly gapped; stale fuel; hydrostatic pressure is built up in the pump.
Corrective action:
1. Turn the ignition switch to the ON (I) position.
2. Slide the fuel shut-off valve lever to the far right (ON) position.
3. Slide the choke lever to the far left (CHOKE) position for cold starts.
4. Verify the oil level is at the FULL mark on the yellow dipstick; add SAE 10W-30 engine oil as needed.
5. Press the spark plug wire boot firmly onto the F7RTC spark plug terminal.
6. Remove the spark plug, clean carbon deposits, check the gap (0.028 to 0.031 inches), and replace if cracked.
7. Drain old fuel and refill with fresh unleaded gasoline.
8. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger to relieve trapped water pressure before pulling the recoil starter handle.

Problem: Engine starts but runs rough, misfires, or idles erratically.
Possible cause: Choke lever left in CHOKE; dirty air filter element; fouled spark plug; water or dirt in the fuel system; carburetor jetting is restricted.
Corrective action:
1. Gradually move the choke lever to the RUN position as the engine warms.
2. Inspect and clean the air filter foam sleeve as outlined in Section 5; replace the paper core if clogged.
3. Clean and regap the F7RTC spark plug to 0.028 to 0.031 inches.
4. Drain the carburetor float bowl using the drain bowl to clear sediment, then refill the fuel tank with fresh gasoline.
5. Clean the fuel sediment cup under the fuel valve.
6. Clear any debris from the black fuel tank cap vent holes.

Problem: Engine runs but stalls or shuts down unexpectedly during operation.
Possible cause: Engine is low on oil, triggering the low-oil shutdown sensor; empty fuel tank; clogged fuel line; dirty cooling fins.
Corrective action:
1. Position the pressure washer on level ground and verify the oil level is at the FULL mark on the yellow dipstick; add oil if low.
2. Check the fuel level in the tank; refuel outdoors with fresh gasoline if empty.
3. Inspect fuel lines for kinks or blockages.
4. Shut off the engine, allow it to cool, and clean dirt from the cylinder cooling fins and recoil starter cover.

Problem: Engine lacks power or bogs down when the spray gun trigger is squeezed.
Possible cause: Clogged spark arrestor screen; spark plug gap is too wide; carburetor jetting is restricted; clogged high-pressure spray nozzle.
Corrective action:
1. Verify that the engine is running at its full governed speed of 3,600 RPM.
2. Remove the muffler guard and clean the spark arrestor screen with a brass wire brush.
3. Inspect and reset the spark plug gap to 0.028 to 0.031 inches.
4. Clean the quick-connect nozzle orifice using the wire clean-out tool to eliminate hydraulic restriction.

Problem: Engine produces excessive black smoke from the exhaust muffler.
Possible cause: Choke lever is partially closed; air filter element is saturated with dirt or oil; carburetor float is stuck or leaking.
Corrective action:
1. Push the choke lever fully to the far right (RUN) position.
2. Clean the air filter foam sleeve and replace the paper core if restricted.
3. Tap the carburetor float bowl gently with a soft mallet to free a stuck float; have the carburetor serviced if it continues.

Problem: Recoil starter rope is extremely hard to pull or snaps back violently.
Possible cause: High hydrostatic water pressure is trapped in the pump head and high-pressure hose; engine cylinder is flooded with fuel.
Corrective action:
1. Squeeze and hold the spray gun trigger to release trapped pressure before pulling the recoil starter handle.
2. Remove the F7RTC spark plug and pull the recoil starter rope several times to expel excess fuel, then reinstall the plug and start again.

### Pump and Hydraulic Subsystem Troubleshooting

Problem: Pump does not build pressure or produces very low discharge pressure.
Possible cause: Incorrect nozzle installed; municipal water supply flow rate is low; inlet water screen is clogged; garden hose is kinked; trapped air in pump.
Corrective action:
1. Install a high-pressure nozzle (0, 25, or 40-degree); do not use the soap nozzle.
2. Verify the water supply provides at least 4.6 GPM at 20 to 80 PSI.
3. Disconnect the hose and clean the screen inside the inlet coupling.
4. Straighten the garden hose to remove all kinks or flat spots.
5. Squeeze the trigger to purge air until water flows steadily.

Problem: Pump pressure fluctuates, pulses, or is inconsistent.
Possible cause: Municipal water supply is restricted; inlet water screen is blocked; spray nozzle is partially clogged; air leak at pump inlet connection.
Corrective action:
1. Confirm the garden hose is at least 5/8 inch in diameter and under 50 feet long.
2. Clean the inlet screen and clear any obstruction from the nozzle orifice using the wire clean-out tool.
3. Tighten the garden hose connection at the pump inlet to ensure an airtight seal.

Problem: Thermal relief valve is continuously discharging hot water.
Possible cause: Engine has been idling without spraying for over two minutes; relief valve is dirty or stuck open; relief valve is defective.
Corrective action:
1. Squeeze the trigger immediately to discharge hot water. Do not idle the engine over two minutes.
2. Shut off the engine, turn off water, allow the unit to cool.
3. If the valve leaks cold water when the machine is first started, replace the valve.

Problem: Water leaks from the high-pressure hose connection at the pump or gun.
Possible cause: Threaded M22 collar is loose; internal high-pressure rubber O-ring is worn, damaged, or missing; connection threads are stripped.
Corrective action:
1. Relieve pressure and hand-tighten the M22 collar clockwise.
2. Inspect and replace the rubber O-ring at the tip of the connector if worn, applying water-resistant silicone grease to the seal.
3. Replace the 25-foot high-pressure hose if the metal threads are stripped or cross-threaded.

Problem: Water leaks from the garden hose connection at the pump inlet.
Possible cause: Garden hose coupling is loose or cross-threaded; inlet hose washer is worn or missing; inlet coupling is cracked.
Corrective action:
1. Unthread the garden hose, align the threads carefully, and hand-tighten the connection collar clockwise until snug.
2. Ensure the rubber hose washer is present and seated flat; replace if cracked.
3. Replace the pump inlet fitting if cracked.

Problem: Spray nozzle is ejected from the wand or leaks water around the coupler.
Possible cause: Brass quick-connect collar was not fully locked; nozzle hex shank is worn; coupler ball bearings are dirty.
Corrective action:
1. Pull back the quick-connect collar, push the nozzle firmly until it bottoms out, then release the collar and pull the nozzle to verify it is locked.
2. Replace the color-coded nozzle if the hex shank is worn.
3. Clean and lubricate the coupler sleeve with silicone grease.

Problem: Water flow at the nozzle drops off after a few minutes of spraying, and the garden hose inlet filter screen (part RP-IS-26) is found collapsed inward when the supply hose is removed.
Possible cause: The RP-IS-26 filter screen was installed with its dome facing inward, so supply pressure folds it into the inlet passage and chokes flow as the pump warms.
Corrective action: Shut off the engine and water supply, unscrew the garden hose from the pump inlet, remove the RP-IS-26 screen, reshape or replace it, and reinstall it with the dome facing outward toward the hose before reconnecting.

### Detergent and Accessory Subsystem Troubleshooting

Problem: Detergent is not siphoning or mixing with the water stream.
Possible cause: High-pressure nozzle is installed; clear plastic chemical siphon tube is disconnected; siphon tube is kinked; siphon inlet filter screen is clogged; detergent is too thick; check-valve is stuck.
Corrective action:
1. Install the Black Soap Nozzle. The chemical siphon will not draw detergent under high pressure.
2. Connect the siphon tube to the brass chemical injection barb on the pump outlet.
3. Clear any kinks or pinches along the siphon tube.
4. Clean the siphon screen at the bottom of the clear plastic tube.
5. Dilute the detergent with water.
6. Free a stuck check-ball inside the brass barb using a toothpick, then flush with warm water.

Problem: Clear plastic chemical siphon tube is leaking detergent or drawing air.
Possible cause: Siphon tube is split, cracked, or hardened due to chemical exposure; tube is loose on the brass barb fitting.
Corrective action:
1. Replace the chemical siphon tube if cracked or hardened.
2. Push the tube completely over the brass chemical injection barb and secure it with a small zip-tie to ensure an airtight seal.

Problem: High-pressure hose is stiff, kinked, or shows localized blistering.
Possible cause: Hose was stored with water under freezing conditions; hose was dragged over sharp corners; hose touched the hot engine muffler.
Corrective action:
1. Discard the damaged hose immediately and replace it with an original Ridgeline replacement hose.
2. Uncoil the hose completely and keep it away from the hot muffler.
